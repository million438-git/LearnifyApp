⚙️ Lab Setup

Model — SIR-style with resistance

Each day, sensitive cells grow, mutate to resistant at rate μ, and die from the antibiotic at rate d. Resistant cells grow slower (fitness cost φ) but are not killed by the antibiotic.

  • Sensitive: S(t+1) = S·(1 + r) − μ·S − d·S
  • Resistant: R(t+1) = R·(1 + r(1−φ)) + μ·S
  • Total: N = S + R
  • Growth rate: r = ln(2) / t_d per hour
SymbolMeaningTypical value
N₀Initial population1000 cells
t_dDoubling time20 h (E. coli)
μMutation rate10⁻⁶–10⁻³ per cell·gen
dAntibiotic kill rate0.5–1.0 (fraction/day)
φFitness cost of resistance0.05–0.30
rDaily growth rateln(2)·24/t_d

Key insight: Antibiotics select for pre-existing resistant mutants. The mutation supplies variation; the antibiotic is the selection pressure. Without antibiotic, resistant cells often lose out due to fitness cost.
