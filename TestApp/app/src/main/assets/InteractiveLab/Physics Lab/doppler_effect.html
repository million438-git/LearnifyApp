Core Formulas — Doppler Effect

  • General Doppler: f' = f₀ · (v ± v_o) / (v ∓ v_s)
  • Approaching source: f' = f₀ · v / (v − v_s) → pitch rises
  • Receding source: f' = f₀ · v / (v + v_s) → pitch falls
  • Approaching observer: f' = f₀ · (v + v_o) / v → pitch rises
  • Receding observer: f' = f₀ · (v − v_o) / v → pitch falls
  • Observed wavelength: λ' = (v ± v_o) / f₀ (or use v/f')
  • Mach number: M = v_s / v (M > 1 → supersonic)

💡 Key insights: The frequency change is NOT due to the source or observer "emitting differently" — the source always emits f₀. It's due to relative motion causing wavefronts to compress (approaching) or expand (receding). Approaching source → shorter wavelength → higher pitch. Receding source → longer wavelength → lower pitch. Both approaches give the same direction of shift. When v_s > v (Mach > 1), the source outruns its own sound — creating a sonic boom cone. The classic ambulance siren: pitch rises as it approaches, drops suddenly as it passes you.
