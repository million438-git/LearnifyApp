Core Fusion Formulas

  • Mass-energy: E = Δm·c²
  • Q-value: Q = (m_reactants − m_products)·c²
  • Coulomb barrier: E_c = k·Z₁·Z₂·e²/r ≈ 1.4 MeV for D-T
  • Gamow factor (tunneling): P ~ exp(−2π·Z₁·Z₂·e²/(ℏ·v))
  • Reaction rate: R = n_D·n_T·⟨σv⟩
  • Power density: P = R · Q = n_D·n_T·⟨σv⟩·Q
  • Lawson criterion: n·T·τ ≥ 3×10²¹ keV·s/m³ (for D-T)
  • Triple product: n·T·τ = density × temperature × confinement time
  • Q factor: Q = P_fusion / P_input (Q > 1 means net positive)
  • Plasma beta: β = 2·μ₀·n·k·T / B² (pressure / magnetic pressure)

💡 Quick tips: D-T is the easiest fusion reaction because it has the largest cross-section at achievable temperatures (~150 million K, ten times hotter than the Sun's core). Even so, D-T has a Coulomb barrier of ~1.4 MeV — but quantum tunneling allows fusion to occur at temperatures ten times lower. The Lawson triple product n·T·τ defines when fusion becomes self-sustaining: if it exceeds ~3×10²¹ keV·s/m³, the alpha particles alone can heat the plasma. The National Ignition Facility achieved Q>1 in December 2022 — the first-ever net-positive fusion reaction.
