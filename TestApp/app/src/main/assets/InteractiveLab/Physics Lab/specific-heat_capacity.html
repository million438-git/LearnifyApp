Core Formulas — Specific Heat Capacity

  • Heat equation: Q = m · c · ΔT
  • Temperature change: ΔT = Q / (m · c)
  • Final temperature: T = T₀ + ΔT
  • Heat capacity (total): C = m · c
  • Heat rate (power): P = Q / t = m · c · (ΔT/t)
  • Calorimetry mixing: m₁c₁(T_f − T₁) = m₂c₂(T₂ − T_f)
  • Latent heat (phase change): Q = m · L (no ΔT during phase change)

💡 Quick tips: Water has an unusually high c (~4186 J/kg·°C) — that is why oceans moderate climate and it takes 4.18 kJ to heat 1 kg water by 1 °C. Metals like lead have low c (~128 J/kg·°C), so they heat up and cool down fast. Doubling mass or heat doubles ΔT; doubling c halves ΔT.
