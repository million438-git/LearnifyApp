Core Formulas — Rocket Thrust

  • Thrust: F = ṁ · v_e (mass flow rate × exhaust velocity)
  • Specific impulse: I_sp = v_e / g₀ (in seconds)
  • Tsiolkovsky rocket equation: Δv = v_e · ln(m₀/m_f)
  • Current mass: m(t) = m₀ + m_p − ṁ · t (while burning)
  • Net force: F_net = F − m·g (near Earth's surface)
  • Acceleration: a = F_net / m
  • Thrust-to-weight ratio: TWR = F / (m·g) — must exceed 1 for liftoff
  • Burn time: t_burn = m_p / ṁ

💡 Quick tips: A rocket must have TWR > 1 to lift off. Thrust comes from expelling mass at high speed — Newton's third law. Higher Isp (exhaust velocity) is better for efficiency, but real engines trade thrust for Isp. Ion engines have huge Isp but tiny thrust. g₀ = 9.80665 m/s².
