Core Formulas — Electric Fields

  • Coulomb's law: F = k·q₁·q₂/r²
  • Electric field of a point charge: E = k·|q|/r²
  • Field direction: away from positive, toward negative
  • Force on test charge: F = q·E
  • Superposition: E_total = ΣE_i (vector sum)
  • Electric potential: V = k·q/r (scalar sum)
  • Potential energy: U = q·V
  • Field from potential: E = −∇V
  • Uniform field (parallel plates): E = V/d

💡 Key insights: Field lines point away from positive charges and toward negative charges. They never cross. Their density indicates field strength (closer = stronger). The field is always perpendicular to equipotential lines. Around an isolated charge the field falls as 1/r² and the potential as 1/r. For a dipole, the field is strongest between the charges and weakest perpendicular to the axis.
