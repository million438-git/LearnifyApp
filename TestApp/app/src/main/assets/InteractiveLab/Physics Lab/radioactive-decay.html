Core Formulas — Radioactive Decay

  • Decay law: N(t) = N₀ · e−λt
  • Half-life relation: T½ = ln(2) / λ ≈ 0.693 / λ
  • Decay constant: λ = ln(2) / T½
  • Number of half-lives: n = t / T½
  • Alternative form: N(t) = N₀ · (½)t/T½
  • Activity: A(t) = λ · N(t)
  • Activity ratio: A/A₀ = N/N₀ = e−λt
  • Mean lifetime: τ = 1/λ = T½ / ln(2) ≈ 1.443 · T½

💡 Quick tips: After 1 half-life, 50% remains. After 2 half-lives, 25%. After 10 half-lives, only 0.1%. Radioactive decay is a random process — we can only predict averages. Each individual nucleus is equally likely to decay per unit time, but we cannot predict which one or when. Geiger counters click when they detect individual decay events — a real random signature of quantum physics.
