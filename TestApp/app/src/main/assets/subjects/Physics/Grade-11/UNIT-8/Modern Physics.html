⚛️ Introduction to Modern Physics

✨ WOW: Modern physics began around 1900 when classical physics couldn't explain phenomena like blackbody radiation, the photoelectric effect, and atomic spectra. It gave us quantum mechanics, relativity, and nuclear physics – technologies like lasers, GPS, and MRI all rely on these ideas!
Modern physics is the physics of the very small (quantum) and the very fast (relativity). It revolutionised our understanding of reality.

📸 8.1 Photoelectric Effect

Einstein's photon theory

Light consists of particles (photons) each with energy E = h f, where h = 6.626 × 10^-34 J·s.

When a photon strikes a metal surface, it can transfer its energy to an electron. If the energy exceeds the work function φ, the electron is ejected with kinetic energy K_max = h f - φ.

✨ WOW: Einstein won the Nobel Prize for this explanation – it proved light has particle nature!

Key experiments & laws

  • Threshold frequency f₀ = φ / h – below f₀ no emission.
  • K_max depends on frequency, not intensity.
  • Intensity increases number of photoelectrons (current), not their energy.
  • Emission is instantaneous (less than 10^-9 s).
Example: A metal with φ = 2.0 eV illuminated with λ = 400 nm. Photon energy = hc/λ = 3.1 eV → K_max = 1.1 eV.

🔬 8.2 Atomic Models & Spectra

Bohr model (1913)

Electrons orbit in quantized angular momentum: mvr = n h/(2π). Energy levels: E_n = -13.6 eV / n² (for hydrogen). Transitions ΔE = h f.

✨ WOW: Bohr's model explained the hydrogen spectrum exactly – a huge leap for quantum theory!

Line spectra

Emission lines: electron jumps from higher to lower level. Absorption lines: electron absorbs photon and jumps up. Rydberg formula: 1/λ = R (1/n₁² – 1/n₂²).

Example: Lyman series (n₁=1) ultraviolet; Balmer series (n₁=2) visible.

🌊 8.3 Wave‑Particle Duality

de Broglie hypothesis (1924)

Every particle has a wavelength λ = h / p, where p = mv. Electrons show diffraction (Davisson‑Germer).

✨ WOW: Electrons – once considered particles – can behave like waves! This is why we have electron microscopes.

Complementarity & uncertainty

Heisenberg uncertainty: Δx Δp ≥ h/(4π). You cannot know position and momentum simultaneously with perfect precision.

🌀 8.4 Quantum Mechanics

Schrödinger equation

Wavefunction ψ describes probability amplitude. |ψ|² gives probability density. Quantized energy levels arise naturally.

✨ WOW: Quantum mechanics predicts that particles can tunnel through barriers – used in scanning tunneling microscopes!

Applications

Lasers, semiconductors, quantum cryptography, MRI.

☢️ 8.5 Nuclear Physics

Radioactivity

Alpha (α): helium nucleus, low penetration. Beta (β): electron or positron, medium. Gamma (γ): high‑energy photon, very penetrating.

Decay law: N = N₀ e^(–λt), half‑life T₁/₂ = ln2 / λ.

✨ WOW: Carbon‑14 dating lets us determine the age of ancient artifacts – using half‑life of 5730 years!

Binding energy & fission/fusion

Mass defect Δm → binding energy B = Δm c². Fission splits heavy nuclei; fusion combines light nuclei – both release energy because products have higher binding per nucleon.

Example: 1 kg U‑235 fission releases ~8×10¹³ J – equivalent to 20 kilotons of TNT.

⚡ 8.6 Relativity (Special)

Einstein's postulates

1. Laws of physics same in all inertial frames. 2. Speed of light c constant.

Time dilation: Δt = γ Δτ, length contraction: L = L₀/γ, γ = 1/√(1 – v²/c²).

Mass‑energy equivalence: E₀ = m c², total energy E = γ m c².

✨ WOW: GPS satellites must correct for time dilation (both special and general relativity) – otherwise they'd be off by kilometers per day!

📌 Key Equations

Photoelectric: K_max = hf – φ
de Broglie: λ = h/p
Energy levels: E_n = –13.6 eV / n²
Radioactive decay: N = N₀ e^(–λt), T₁/₂ = ln2/λ
Mass‑energy: E = Δm c²
Relativity: γ = 1/√(1 – v²/c²), Δt = γ Δτ, L = L₀/γ

🎯 40 Advanced Modern Physics Problems

Q1
A metal has work function 2.5 eV. Find the threshold frequency and the maximum wavelength that can eject electrons. (h = 4.14×10^-15 eV·s, c = 3×10^8 m/s)
✅ solution: Threshold frequency f₀ = φ/h = 2.5 eV / (4.14e-15 eV·s) = 6.04×10^14 Hz. Maximum wavelength λ_max = c/f₀ = 3e8 / 6.04e14 = 4.97×10^-7 m = 497 nm (blue-green).
Q2
Light of wavelength 200 nm falls on a metal with work function 4.0 eV. Find the maximum kinetic energy of photoelectrons in eV and J. (h = 6.63×10^-34 J·s, c = 3×10^8 m/s, 1 eV = 1.6×10^-19 J)
✅ solution: Photon energy E = hc/λ = 6.63e-34·3e8 / 200e-9 = 1.989e-25/2e-7 = 9.945e-19 J = 9.945e-19 / 1.6e-19 = 6.22 eV. K_max = 6.22 – 4.0 = 2.22 eV = 2.22·1.6e-19 = 3.55e-19 J.
Q3
If the intensity of light in Q2 is doubled, what happens to (a) maximum kinetic energy, (b) photoelectric current?
✅ solution: (a) K_max unchanged – depends only on frequency. (b) Current doubles because more photons eject more electrons per second.
Q4
Calculate the de Broglie wavelength of an electron accelerated through 100 V. (m_e = 9.11×10^-31 kg, h = 6.63×10^-34 J·s, e = 1.6×10^-19 C)
✅ solution: Kinetic energy K = e V = 1.6e-17 J. v = √(2K/m) = √(2·1.6e-17/9.11e-31) = √(3.51e13) = 5.93e6 m/s. λ = h/p = h/(mv) = 6.63e-34 / (9.11e-31·5.93e6) = 6.63e-34 / 5.40e-24 = 1.23e-10 m = 0.123 nm. (Alternatively, λ = h/√(2mK) = 12.3 Å/√100 = 0.123 nm).
Q5
Find the energy of the first excited state (n=2) of hydrogen in eV.
✅ solution: E_n = –13.6/n² eV. For n=2, E₂ = –13.6/4 = –3.40 eV.
Q6
What is the wavelength of the photon emitted when an electron in hydrogen falls from n=3 to n=2? (Rydberg constant R = 1.097×10^7 m⁻¹)
✅ solution: 1/λ = R(1/2² – 1/3²) = 1.097e7 (1/4 – 1/9) = 1.097e7 (0.25 – 0.1111) = 1.097e7·0.1389 = 1.524e6 m⁻¹. λ = 1/1.524e6 = 6.56e-7 m = 656 nm (red, Balmer line).
Q7
An electron has a de Broglie wavelength of 0.2 nm. Find its kinetic energy in eV.
✅ solution: λ = h/p → p = h/λ = 6.63e-34/2e-10 = 3.315e-24 kg·m/s. K = p²/(2m) = (3.315e-24)²/(2·9.11e-31) = 1.099e-47/1.822e-30 = 6.03e-18 J = 6.03e-18/1.6e-19 = 37.7 eV.
Q8
A radioactive sample has a half‑life of 10 days. How long will it take for 75% of the sample to decay?
✅ solution: 75% decay means 25% remains. N/N₀ = 0.25 = (1/2)^n → n = 2 half‑lives. Time = 2·10 = 20 days.
Q9
A sample of radium‑226 has an initial activity of 1000 Bq. Its half‑life is 1600 years. Find the activity after 3200 years.
✅ solution: 3200 years = 2 half‑lives. Activity = 1000 / 2² = 1000/4 = 250 Bq.
Q10
What is the binding energy of a nucleus with mass defect 0.0025 u? (1 u = 931.5 MeV/c²)
✅ solution: B = Δm·931.5 = 0.0025·931.5 = 2.33 MeV.
Q11
How much energy is released when 1 g of mass is completely converted to energy? (c = 3×10^8 m/s)
✅ solution: E = m c² = 0.001 kg · (3e8)^2 = 0.001·9e16 = 9e13 J.
Q12
A spaceship moves at 0.8c relative to Earth. How much time does a clock on the spaceship lose compared to Earth clocks after 10 years Earth time?
✅ solution: γ = 1/√(1 – 0.8²) = 1/√(0.36) = 1/0.6 = 1.6667. Proper time on ship Δτ = Δt / γ = 10 / 1.6667 = 6 years. Ship clock is 4 years behind.
Q13
What is the speed of an electron whose kinetic energy is 1 MeV? (m_e c² = 0.511 MeV)
✅ solution: Total energy E = K + m c² = 1 + 0.511 = 1.511 MeV. γ = E/(m c²) = 1.511/0.511 = 2.957. γ = 1/√(1 – v²/c²) → v/c = √(1 – 1/γ²) = √(1 – 1/8.745) = √(0.8856) = 0.941c.
Q14
Calculate the wavelength of a photon with energy 10 keV. (h = 4.14×10^-15 eV·s, c = 3×10^8 m/s)
✅ solution: λ = hc/E = (4.14e-15 eV·s · 3e8 m/s) / 10000 eV = (1.242e-6 eV·m) / 1e4 = 1.242e-10 m = 0.1242 nm.
Q15
The half‑life of carbon‑14 is 5730 years. A bone has 1/16 of its original C‑14. How old is the bone?
✅ solution: 1/16 = (1/2)^4 → 4 half‑lives → age = 4·5730 = 22,920 years.
Q16
An electron is confined in a box of length 0.1 nm. Estimate its minimum kinetic energy using uncertainty principle. (h = 6.63×10^-34 J·s)
✅ solution: Δx ≈ 0.1 nm = 10^-10 m. Minimum Δp ≈ h/(4πΔx) ≈ 6.63e-34/(4·3.14·1e-10) = 5.28e-25 kg·m/s. Energy (non‑relativistic) ≈ (Δp)²/(2m) = (2.79e-49)/(1.82e-30) = 1.53e-19 J = 0.96 eV.
Q17
A proton has a de Broglie wavelength of 0.01 nm. Find its speed. (m_p = 1.67×10^-27 kg)
✅ solution: p = h/λ = 6.63e-34 / 1e-11 = 6.63e-23 kg·m/s. v = p/m = 6.63e-23 / 1.67e-27 = 3.97e4 m/s = 39.7 km/s.
Q18
Calculate the energy (in J) of a photon with frequency 5×10^14 Hz.
✅ solution: E = h f = 6.63e-34·5e14 = 3.315e-19 J.
Q19
A neutron has a mass of 1.008665 u. Find its rest energy in MeV.
✅ solution: E = m·931.5 MeV = 1.008665·931.5 = 939.6 MeV.
Q20
What is the shortest wavelength in the Lyman series of hydrogen? (R = 1.097×10^7 m⁻¹)
✅ solution: Shortest λ corresponds to n₂ → ∞. 1/λ = R (1/1² – 0) = R. λ = 1/R = 9.11×10^-8 m = 91.1 nm.
Q21
A uranium‑238 nucleus (mass 238.0508 u) decays by alpha emission into thorium‑234 (mass 234.0436 u) and an alpha particle (mass 4.0026 u). Find the energy released in MeV.
✅ solution: Mass defect = 238.0508 – (234.0436 + 4.0026) = 238.0508 – 238.0462 = 0.0046 u. Energy = 0.0046·931.5 = 4.28 MeV.
Q22
A spacecraft is moving at 0.95c. Find the Lorentz factor γ.
✅ solution: γ = 1/√(1 – 0.9025) = 1/√0.0975 = 1/0.3122 = 3.20.
Q23
How fast must a particle move so that its kinetic energy equals its rest energy?
✅ solution: K = (γ – 1) m c² = m c² → γ = 2. γ = 1/√(1 – v²/c²) → 1/√(1 – v²/c²) = 2 → 1 – v²/c² = 1/4 → v²/c² = 3/4 → v = 0.866c.
Q24
A muon travels at 0.99c toward Earth from a height of 10 km. Its proper lifetime is 2.2 μs. Will it reach Earth's surface?
✅ solution: γ = 1/√(1 – 0.9801) = 1/√0.0199 = 1/0.141 = 7.09. Lifetime in Earth frame = γ τ = 7.09·2.2 = 15.6 μs. Distance traveled in this time = v t = 0.99c·15.6e-6 = 0.99·3e8·1.56e-5 = 4630 m = 4.63 km, not enough. But length contraction: distance in muon frame is 10 km / γ = 1410 m, which it covers in proper time 2.2 μs at 0.99c (distance = 0.99c·2.2e-6 = 653 m) – still not enough? Actually in muon frame the distance is contracted to 1410 m, and it takes t' = 1410/(0.99c) = 4.75 μs, longer than its lifetime. Wait, careful: In Earth frame, time is dilated, but distance is fixed. In muon frame, distance is contracted. Let's compute Earth frame: time to reach surface = 10 km / (0.99c) = 10000 / (2.97e8) = 33.7 μs. Muon lifetime in Earth frame is 15.6 μs, so it decays before reaching. So no, it does not reach. (The classic result with higher γ may reach, but here γ=7.09, not enough.)
Q25
A nuclear reaction releases 200 MeV. How much mass is converted to energy? (1 u = 931.5 MeV)
✅ solution: Δm = 200 / 931.5 = 0.2147 u = 0.2147·1.66e-27 kg = 3.56e-28 kg.
Q26
A metal has threshold wavelength 600 nm. Find the work function in eV.
✅ solution: φ = hc/λ = 1240 eV·nm / 600 nm = 2.07 eV. (Using hc = 1240 eV·nm).
Q27
What is the momentum of a photon with energy 5 eV? (h = 6.63×10^-34 J·s, c = 3×10^8 m/s, 1 eV = 1.6×10^-19 J)
✅ solution: p = E/c = (5·1.6e-19) / 3e8 = 8e-19/3e8 = 2.67e-27 kg·m/s. Also p = h/λ, but not needed.
Q28
An electron is moving at 2×10^6 m/s. Find its de Broglie wavelength.
✅ solution: λ = h/(mv) = 6.63e-34 / (9.11e-31·2e6) = 6.63e-34 / 1.822e-24 = 3.64e-10 m = 0.364 nm.
Q29
Calculate the binding energy per nucleon of iron‑56 if its mass defect is 0.528 u.
✅ solution: Total binding energy = 0.528·931.5 = 492 MeV. Per nucleon = 492/56 = 8.79 MeV.
Q30
A photon of energy 1.2 MeV undergoes pair production. Find the total kinetic energy of the electron and positron.
✅ solution: Minimum energy for pair = 2·0.511 = 1.022 MeV. K.E. = 1.2 – 1.022 = 0.178 MeV.
Q31
How many half‑lives are required for a sample to decay to 6.25% of its original amount?
✅ solution: 6.25% = 1/16 = (1/2)^4 → 4 half‑lives.
Q32
A star converts 1 million tons of hydrogen into helium each second. How much energy is released per second if 0.7% of mass is converted? (c = 3×10^8 m/s)
✅ solution: Mass converted per second = 0.7% of 1e9 kg = 7e6 kg. E = m c² = 7e6·9e16 = 6.3e23 J.
Q33
A particle has a total energy 3 times its rest energy. Find its speed.
✅ solution: γ = 3. γ = 1/√(1 – v²/c²) → 1 – v²/c² = 1/9 → v²/c² = 8/9 → v = 0.943c.
Q34
What is the energy of a photon whose momentum is 5×10^-28 kg·m/s?
✅ solution: E = p c = 5e-28·3e8 = 1.5e-19 J = 1.5e-19/1.6e-19 = 0.938 eV.
Q35
A radioactive nucleus decays with decay constant λ = 0.05 s⁻¹. Find its half‑life.
✅ solution: T₁/₂ = ln2/λ = 0.693/0.05 = 13.86 s.
Q36
Calculate the frequency of a photon with energy 2 eV.
✅ solution: f = E/h = 2·1.6e-19 / 6.63e-34 = 3.2e-19/6.63e-34 = 4.83e14 Hz.
Q37
An electron in hydrogen jumps from n=4 to n=1. Find the wavelength of the emitted photon. (R = 1.097×10^7 m⁻¹)
✅ solution: 1/λ = R (1/1² – 1/4²) = 1.097e7 (1 – 1/16) = 1.097e7·0.9375 = 1.028e7 m⁻¹. λ = 9.73e-8 m = 97.3 nm (ultraviolet).
Q38
How much energy is released when 2 kg of coal is completely converted to energy? (c = 3×10^8 m/s)
✅ solution: E = m c² = 2·9e16 = 1.8e17 J.
Q39
A laser emits light of wavelength 632 nm. What is the energy of each photon in eV?
✅ solution: E = 1240 / 632 = 1.96 eV.
Q40
The mass of a proton is 1.007276 u. Find its rest energy in MeV.
✅ solution: E = 1.007276·931.5 = 938.3 MeV.
