📘 Motion in 1D and 2D – key ideas

Motion is described by displacement, velocity, and acceleration. In one dimension, direction is given by +/–. In two dimensions, vectors are essential. Projectile motion combines constant horizontal velocity and constant vertical acceleration. Graphs (position–time, velocity–time) help visualise motion. The independence of perpendicular motions is a cornerstone of physics.

📌 REMEMBER: For constant acceleration, use v = u + at, s = ut + ½at², v² = u² + 2as.
✨ WOW: In projectile motion, the only acceleration is gravity (9.8 m/s² downward) – it acts independently on the vertical motion!
Q1 (conceptual)
A particle moves with constant speed in a circular path. Which statement is correct?
  • A) Velocity is constant
  • B) Acceleration is zero
  • C) Velocity changes continuously
  • D) Net force is zero
Correct answer: C
Although speed is constant, the direction of velocity changes at every point, so velocity changes continuously. Hence acceleration exists.
Q2 (conceptual)
Which condition guarantees zero acceleration?
  • A) Constant speed
  • B) Constant velocity
  • C) Zero displacement
  • D) Uniform circular motion
Correct answer: B
Acceleration depends on change in velocity. Constant velocity means no change in magnitude or direction.
Q3 (conceptual)
Why is displacement a vector quantity?
Answer: Because it has both magnitude and direction.
Q4 (conceptual)
Which situation gives zero displacement but non‑zero distance?
  • A) Straight‑line motion
  • B) Motion in a circle returning to start
  • C) Motion at rest
  • D) Uniform motion
Correct answer: B
If you return to the starting point, displacement is zero but total path length (distance) is positive.
Q5 (conceptual)
Why is average speed always greater than or equal to the magnitude of average velocity?
Explanation: Average speed depends on total distance, while average velocity depends on displacement, which is always less than or equal to distance. They are equal only for straight‑line motion without changing direction.
Q6 (conceptual)
At the highest point of projectile motion, which quantity becomes zero?
  • A) Acceleration
  • B) Horizontal velocity
  • C) Vertical velocity
  • D) Resultant velocity
Correct answer: C
At the top, the vertical component of velocity becomes zero; horizontal velocity remains constant.
Q7 (conceptual)
Which quantity remains constant throughout projectile motion (neglect air resistance)?
  • A) Speed
  • B) Vertical velocity
  • C) Acceleration
  • D) Direction of motion
Correct answer: C
The acceleration is gravity (9.8 m/s² downward) and is constant in magnitude and direction.
Q8 (conceptual)
Why does horizontal velocity not affect time of flight?
Explanation: Time of flight depends only on vertical motion, which is independent of horizontal motion (they are perpendicular).
Q9 (conceptual)
Which graph represents uniform acceleration?
  • A) Straight‑line velocity–time graph
  • B) Curved velocity–time graph
  • C) Horizontal velocity–time graph
  • D) Vertical distance–time graph
Correct answer: A
A straight‑line v–t graph means constant slope, which is constant acceleration.
Q10 (conceptual)
If the slope of a velocity–time graph is negative, what does it indicate?
Answer: Negative acceleration (deceleration) – velocity is decreasing.
Q11 (conceptual)
Why is motion in two dimensions treated as two independent motions?
Explanation: Motion along horizontal and vertical directions do not affect each other; they are perpendicular and independent.
Q12 (conceptual)
Which case gives maximum displacement for a given distance?
  • A) Circular motion
  • B) Zigzag motion
  • C) Straight‑line motion
  • D) Back‑and‑forth motion
Correct answer: C
In straight‑line motion without reversal, distance equals the magnitude of displacement, so it is the maximum possible.
Q13 (conceptual)
An object moves east, then north, then west. Which quantity must be calculated using vectors?
Answer: Displacement. (Distance is a scalar sum, but displacement requires vector addition.)
Q14 (conceptual)
Why does acceleration exist even when speed is constant in circular motion?
Explanation: Direction of velocity changes continuously, so there is acceleration (centripetal acceleration).
Q15 (conceptual)
Which physical quantity is represented by the area under a velocity–time graph?
Answer: Displacement.
Q16 (numerical)
A car starts from rest and reaches a velocity of 36 m/s in 6 s. Find acceleration.
Step‑by‑step:
u = 0 m/s, v = 36 m/s, t = 6 s.
a = (v – u) / t = 36 / 6 = 6 m/s².
✅ Answer: 6 m/s²
Q17 (numerical)
A train slows down from 30 m/s to 10 m/s in 10 s. Find acceleration.
Step‑by‑step:
a = (10 – 30) / 10 = –20 / 10 = –2 m/s².
✅ Answer: –2 m/s² (deceleration)
Q18 (numerical)
An object moves with velocity 15 m/s for 20 s. Find displacement.
Step‑by‑step:
s = v × t = 15 × 20 = 300 m.
✅ Answer: 300 m
Q19 (numerical)
A body accelerates uniformly at 4 m/s² for 5 s starting from rest. Find final velocity.
Step‑by‑step:
v = u + a t = 0 + 4×5 = 20 m/s.
✅ Answer: 20 m/s
Q20 (numerical)
A body moving with velocity 10 m/s accelerates at 2 m/s² for 8 s. Find displacement.
Step‑by‑step:
s = u t + ½ a t² = (10×8) + ½×2×8² = 80 + 64 = 144 m.
✅ Answer: 144 m
Q21 (numerical)
A stone is dropped from rest. Find velocity after 4 s. Take g = 10 m/s².
Step‑by‑step:
v = u + g t = 0 + 10×4 = 40 m/s downward.
✅ Answer: 40 m/s downward
Q22 (numerical)
A car travels 100 m east, then 40 m west. Find displacement.
Step‑by‑step:
Displacement = 100 – 40 = 60 m east.
✅ Answer: 60 m east
Q23 (numerical)
A projectile is fired horizontally with speed 20 m/s from a height of 80 m. Find time of flight. Take g = 10 m/s².
Step‑by‑step:
Vertical motion: s = ½ g t² → 80 = ½ ×10 × t² → 80 = 5 t² → t² = 16 → t = 4 s.
✅ Answer: 4 s
Q24 (numerical)
Using Q23, find horizontal distance traveled.
Step‑by‑step:
Range = horizontal velocity × time = 20 × 4 = 80 m.
✅ Answer: 80 m
Q25 (numerical)
A car covers 400 m in 20 s and then 600 m in 30 s. Find average speed.
Step‑by‑step:
Total distance = 400 + 600 = 1000 m. Total time = 20 + 30 = 50 s.
Average speed = 1000 / 50 = 20 m/s.
✅ Answer: 20 m/s
Q26 (conceptual/numerical)
For Q25, is average velocity equal to average speed? Explain.
Answer: No, unless motion is in a straight line in one direction. Here we don't know directions, so average velocity could be different.
Q27 (numerical)
A body completes one full circular lap of radius 14 m. Find displacement.
Answer: Displacement = 0 (returns to start).
Q28 (numerical)
A vehicle accelerates uniformly from 5 m/s to 25 m/s in 10 s. Find acceleration.
Step‑by‑step:
a = (25 – 5) / 10 = 20 / 10 = 2 m/s².
✅ Answer: 2 m/s²
Q29 (conceptual)
An object moves with increasing speed but constant direction. Does acceleration exist?
Answer: Yes, because the magnitude of velocity is changing (acceleration in the direction of motion).
Q30 (conceptual)
Why is motion analysis essential before studying dynamics?
Explanation: Dynamics explains the causes of motion (forces). To understand causes, we first need to describe motion accurately (kinematics).
