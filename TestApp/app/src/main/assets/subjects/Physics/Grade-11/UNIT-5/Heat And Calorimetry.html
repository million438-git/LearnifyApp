🔥 Introduction

Heat is energy in transit due to temperature difference. This unit covers:
  • Concept of heat, internal energy, thermodynamic work
  • Heat transfer: conduction, convection, radiation
  • Heat capacity & specific heat capacity
  • Thermal expansion (linear, area, volume)
  • Change of phase & latent heat
  • Calorimetry – measurement of heat transfer

🌡️ 5.1 The Concept of Heat

Heat vs Temperature

Heat is energy transferred due to temperature difference (Joules).
Temperature is average kinetic energy of particles (K or °C).

Internal energy & work

Internal energy U = KE_molecular + PE_molecular. Heat and work are two ways to change U.
First law of thermodynamics: \(\Delta U = Q - W\) (will be used in calorimetry).

Example: Gas in cylinder – heating increases U, expansion does work.

⚡ 5.2 Heat Transfer Mechanisms

Conduction

Transfer through collisions in solids. Fourier’s law: \(Q = \frac{kA\Delta T}{L}t\).
Good conductors (metals), insulators (wood, air).

Convection

Transfer by fluid movement – natural (buoyancy) or forced.

Radiation

Electromagnetic waves; Stefan‑Boltzmann: \(P = \sigma \epsilon A T^4\). No medium needed.

📊 5.3 Heat Capacity & Specific Heat

Heat capacity

\(C = \frac{Q}{\Delta T}\) (J/K).

Specific heat capacity

\(c = \frac{Q}{m\Delta T}\) (J/(kg·K)). Water: \(c_w = 4186\) J/(kg·K).

Example 5.1: 600 g lead, c=128, ΔT=80 K → Q = 0.6·128·80 = 6144 J.

📏 5.4 Thermal Expansion

Linear expansion

\(\Delta L = L_0 \alpha \Delta T\)

Area expansion

\(\Delta A = A_0 \beta \Delta T,\; \beta = 2\alpha\)

Volume expansion

\(\Delta V = V_0 \gamma \Delta T,\; \gamma = 3\alpha\)

For liquids: apparent expansion \(\gamma_{app} = \gamma_{real} - \gamma_{container}\).

Example 5.2: Steel bridge L0=20 m, α=1.1e-5, gap 4 cm → ΔT = ΔL/(αL0) = 0.04/(1.1e-5·20) = 180°C.

❄️🔥 5.5 Change of Phase

Latent heat

\(Q = mL\) (fusion \(L_f\), vaporization \(L_v\)). Temperature constant during phase change.

Water: \(L_f=3.33\times10^5\) J/kg, \(L_v=2.26\times10^6\) J/kg.

Phase diagram

Triple point, critical point.

Example 5.6: 4 kg ice -50°C → steam 100°C: total Q = 132.4 MJ.

⚖️ 5.6 Calorimetry

Principle

Heat lost = heat gained (isolated system).

Methods

Mixture method, electrical method.

\(m_1c_1(T_1-T_f) = m_2c_2(T_f-T_2) + m_{cal}c_{cal}(T_f-T_2)\).

Example 5.7: 0.05 kg metal 200°C into 0.4 kg water 20°C → Tf=22.4°C → c_metal = 453 J/kg·K (iron).

📌 Key Equations

\(Q = mc\Delta T\) · \(Q = mL\) · \(\Delta L = \alpha L_0\Delta T\)
\(\Delta A = 2\alpha A_0\Delta T\) · \(\Delta V = 3\alpha V_0\Delta T\)
Heat lost = heat gained · \(P = \frac{kA\Delta T}{L}\)

Q1
How much heat is required to raise the temperature of 200 g of aluminum from 20°C to 120°C? (c_al = 900 J/kg·K)
✅ solution: Q = m c ΔT = 0.2·900·100 = 18000 J = 18 kJ.
Q2
A 0.5 kg copper block (c=385 J/kg·K) at 150°C is placed in 1 kg water at 20°C. Find final temperature (ignore container).
✅ solution: Heat lost = heat gained: 0.5·385·(150-Tf) = 1·4186·(Tf-20). 192.5(150-Tf) = 4186(Tf-20) → 28875 -192.5Tf = 4186Tf -83720 → 28875+83720 = (4186+192.5)Tf → 112595 = 4378.5 Tf → Tf ≈ 25.7°C.
Q3
A 100 g ice cube at -10°C is dropped into 300 g water at 30°C. Find final temperature. (c_ice=2100, c_w=4186, L_f=3.33e5)
✅ solution: Warm water cools to 0°C: Q_water = 0.3·4186·30 = 37674 J. Ice to 0°C: Q1 = 0.1·2100·10 = 2100 J. Melt ice: Q2 = 0.1·3.33e5 = 33300 J. Total needed = 35400 J. 37674 > 35400, so all ice melts and final T>0. Extra heat = 37674-35400 = 2274 J heats melted water (0.1 kg) + original water: total mass = 0.4 kg. ΔT = 2274/(0.4·4186) ≈ 1.36°C. Tf = 1.36°C.
Q4
A 50 g aluminum calorimeter (c=900) contains 150 g water at 25°C. A 80 g metal at 95°C is added, final T=30°C. Find specific heat of metal.
✅ solution: Heat gained by water + cal: Q = (0.15·4186 + 0.05·900)·(30-25) = (627.9 +45)·5 = 672.9·5 = 3364.5 J. This equals heat lost by metal: 0.08·c·(95-30)=0.08·c·65 = 5.2 c = 3364.5 → c = 647 J/kg·K.
Q5

Q6

Q7
