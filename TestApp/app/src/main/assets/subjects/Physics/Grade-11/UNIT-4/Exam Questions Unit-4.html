📘 Dynamics – forces and motion

Dynamics explains why objects move – it connects force and motion. Newton's laws are central: inertia (first law), F = m a (second law), action‑reaction (third law). Key concepts include friction, normal force, tension, centripetal force, and equilibrium. Understanding dynamics allows us to predict the motion of everything from cars to planets.

📌 REMEMBER: Net force = mass × acceleration. Forces are vectors – always consider components.
✨ WOW: The same laws that govern a falling apple also govern the orbit of the Moon!
Q1 (conceptual)
A block is on a frictionless horizontal surface. A force F is applied at an angle θ above the horizontal. Which component of the force causes acceleration along the surface?
Answer: Only the horizontal component: F cos θ.
Concept: Vertical component affects the normal force but does not accelerate the block horizontally.
Q2 (conceptual)
Why does mass resist acceleration?
Answer: Due to inertia – mass is a measure of resistance to change in motion. Newton’s second law: F = m × a.
Q3 (conceptual)
A car rounds a circular track at constant speed. Is there acceleration?
Answer: Yes, centripetal acceleration toward the center. Magnitude = v² / r.
Q4 (conceptual)
Explain why two forces of equal magnitude acting at an angle θ produce a smaller resultant than their sum.
Explanation: Because they are not collinear; the resultant depends on the angle: R = square root of (F² + F² + 2 F F cos θ). When θ > 0, cos θ < 1, so R < 2F.
Q5 (conceptual)
A body is in equilibrium under three forces. What is the condition?
Answer: The vector sum of forces must be zero in both horizontal and vertical directions. The three forces form a closed triangle when drawn head‑to‑tail.
Q6 (conceptual)
Why does friction depend on the normal force?
Answer: Frictional force = μ × N, where N is the normal force. Higher normal force increases contact and interlocking, so friction is stronger.
Q7 (conceptual)
A person in an elevator feels heavier or lighter. Explain using dynamics.
Explanation: Apparent weight = m (g + a). Upward acceleration increases apparent weight (heavier); downward acceleration decreases it (lighter).
Q8 (conceptual)
Why does an object sliding on a rough incline eventually reach terminal speed?
Explanation: As speed increases, friction (which may depend on speed) balances the component of weight along incline, net force becomes zero, so acceleration = 0 and speed becomes constant.
Q9 (conceptual)
A box is pushed along a rough horizontal surface at constant speed. What is the net force?
Answer: Net force = 0 because acceleration = 0. Applied force = friction.
Q10 (conceptual)
Why is centripetal force not a new force?
Answer: It is the resultant of actual forces (tension, gravity, normal, etc.) that point toward the center. Centripetal force is the required net force for circular motion.
Q11 (conceptual)
Two blocks connected over a frictionless pulley. Which block accelerates more?
Answer: Both blocks have the same magnitude of acceleration (they are connected). The acceleration is given by a = (m₁ − m₂)g / (m₁ + m₂). The lighter mass accelerates upward, heavier downward.
Q12 (conceptual)
A body moves in a circle at non‑uniform speed. What types of acceleration exist?
Answer: Tangential acceleration (due to change in speed) and radial (centripetal) acceleration (due to change in direction).
Q13 (conceptual)
Why is rolling friction smaller than sliding friction?
Answer: Because the deformation area is smaller and there is no interlocking of surfaces at the microscopic level; energy loss is much less.
Q14 (conceptual)
Explain the effect of angle of incline on acceleration of a sliding block (frictionless).
Explanation: a = g sin θ, so acceleration increases with θ. Greater angle → larger component of gravity along incline → higher acceleration.
Q15 (conceptual)
Why does the normal force change on an inclined plane when a force is applied vertically?
Explanation: Normal force = mg cos θ − F vertical component (if force is upward). It changes because the vertical component either lifts the block or presses it down, altering the contact force.
Q16 (numerical)
A 5 kg block is on a frictionless table. A force of 20 N acts at 30° above horizontal. Find acceleration of the block.
Step‑by‑step:
Horizontal component = 20 × cos30° = 20 × 0.8660 ≈ 17.32 N.
Acceleration a = F / m = 17.32 / 5 ≈ 3.464 m/s².
✅ Answer: 3.46 m/s² (approx)
Q17 (numerical)
A 10 kg mass is pulled up a 30° incline with friction μ = 0.2. g = 10 m/s². Find acceleration if applied force along incline is 100 N.
Step‑by‑step:
Weight component down incline = m g sin30° = 10×10×0.5 = 50 N.
Normal force N = m g cos30° = 10×10×0.8660 = 86.6 N.
Friction = μ N = 0.2 × 86.6 ≈ 17.32 N.
Net force up incline = 100 – 50 – 17.32 = 32.68 N.
a = F / m = 32.68 / 10 ≈ 3.27 m/s².
✅ Answer: 3.27 m/s²
Q18 (numerical)
Two blocks, 8 kg and 12 kg, connected over a frictionless pulley. Find acceleration.
Step‑by‑step:
a = (m₂ – m₁) g / (m₁ + m₂) = (12 – 8) × 10 / (8+12) = 4 × 10 / 20 = 2 m/s².
✅ Answer: 2 m/s² (the 12 kg mass accelerates downward, 8 kg upward)
Q19 (numerical)
A block of 5 kg slides down an incline 30° with μ = 0.1. Find acceleration. (g = 10 m/s²)
Step‑by‑step:
a = g (sinθ – μ cosθ) = 10 (0.5 – 0.1 × 0.8660) = 10 (0.5 – 0.0866) = 10 × 0.4134 = 4.134 m/s².
✅ Answer: 4.13 m/s² (approx)
Q20 (numerical)
A 2 kg ball moves in a circle of radius 1 m at 4 m/s. Find centripetal force.
Step‑by‑step:
F = m v² / r = 2 × 16 / 1 = 32 N.
✅ Answer: 32 N
Q21 (numerical)
A 1 kg block on a horizontal surface with μ = 0.3 is pulled with 10 N. g = 10 m/s². Find acceleration.
Step‑by‑step:
Friction = μ m g = 0.3 × 1 × 10 = 3 N.
Net force = 10 – 3 = 7 N.
a = 7 / 1 = 7 m/s².
✅ Answer: 7 m/s²
Q22 (numerical)
A car accelerates from 20 m/s to 50 m/s in 10 s. Find acceleration and distance traveled.
Step‑by‑step:
a = (50 – 20) / 10 = 3 m/s².
s = average speed × time = ((50+20)/2) × 10 = 35 × 10 = 350 m.
✅ Answer: a = 3 m/s², s = 350 m
Q23 (numerical)
A body moves along a circle of radius 5 m with speed increasing from 2 m/s to 6 m/s in 4 s. Find tangential acceleration.
Step‑by‑step:
a_t = (v – u) / t = (6 – 2) / 4 = 4 / 4 = 1 m/s².
✅ Answer: 1 m/s²
Q24 (numerical)
A 3 kg block is acted upon by two perpendicular forces: 4 N east, 3 N north. Find resultant force.
Step‑by‑step:
F = square root of (4² + 3²) = square root of (16 + 9) = square root of 25 = 5 N.
✅ Answer: 5 N
Q25 (numerical)
A 5 kg mass hangs vertically. Find tension in rope when mass accelerates upward at 2 m/s². (g = 10 m/s²)
Step‑by‑step:
T = m (g + a) = 5 × (10 + 2) = 5 × 12 = 60 N.
✅ Answer: 60 N
Q26 (numerical)
A 10 kg block is pulled horizontally with 50 N. μ = 0.2, g = 10 m/s². Find acceleration.
Step‑by‑step:
Friction = μ m g = 0.2 × 10 × 10 = 20 N.
Net force = 50 – 20 = 30 N.
a = 30 / 10 = 3 m/s².
✅ Answer: 3 m/s²
Q27 (numerical)
A car moves in a circle of radius 50 m at 20 m/s. Find centripetal acceleration.
Step‑by‑step:
a_c = v² / r = 400 / 50 = 8 m/s².
✅ Answer: 8 m/s²
Q28 (numerical)
A 4 kg mass is pulled at 30° with 40 N. Find horizontal and vertical components.
Step‑by‑step:
Horizontal = 40 × cos30° ≈ 40 × 0.8660 = 34.64 N.
Vertical = 40 × sin30° = 40 × 0.5 = 20 N.
✅ Answer: 34.64 N, 20 N
Q29 (numerical)
Two blocks 6 kg and 4 kg on a frictionless pulley. Find acceleration and tension. (g = 10 m/s²)
Step‑by‑step:
a = (6 – 4) × 10 / (6+4) = 20 / 10 = 2 m/s².
For the 4 kg block (upward): T – 4g = 4a → T = 4(g + a) = 4(10 + 2) = 48 N.
✅ Answer: a = 2 m/s², T = 48 N
Q30 (numerical)
A 5 kg block slides down a frictionless incline 30°. Find acceleration. (g = 10 m/s²)
Step‑by‑step:
a = g sinθ = 10 × 0.5 = 5 m/s².
✅ Answer: 5 m/s²
