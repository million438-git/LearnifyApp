⚙️ Introduction

Dynamics studies motion and its causes. Covers Newton's laws, friction, equilibrium, work, energy, power, impulse, momentum, and center of mass.

📌 4.1 Force & Newton's Laws

Force

Push or pull (vector). Contact (friction, tension) or field (gravity, electromagnetic). SI unit: newton (N).

Fundamental forces: strong nuclear, electromagnetic, weak nuclear, gravitational.

Newton's first law (inertia)

Object remains at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon by net force. Inertia = resistance to change in motion; mass = measure of inertia.

[seatbelt example – inertia during crash]

Newton's second law

Net force = mass × acceleration. Acceleration is proportional to net force and inversely proportional to mass.

Newton's third law

Every action has equal and opposite reaction. Forces act on different bodies and never cancel.

Example: Weight on moon = 1/6 of Earth weight. Normal force = perpendicular support force. On incline: Normal force = mass × gravity × cosθ.

🧱 4.2 Friction

Types

Static friction: maximum = coefficient × normal force.
Kinetic friction: force = coefficient × normal force (usually smaller than static).

Example: 8 kg block, static coefficient = 0.4 → Force needed to start = 31.4 N. To accelerate at 4 m/s² → applied force = 55.5 N.

⚖️ 4.3 Equilibrium

For translational equilibrium: sum of forces = 0. Object at rest or constant velocity.

Example: Traffic light 100 N, angles 37° & 53° → Tension T1=60 N, T2=80 N.

💡 4.4 Work, Energy, Power

Work

Work = force × distance × cosθ. Positive, negative, or zero.

Kinetic Energy

KE = 0.5 × mass × velocity².

Potential Energy

Gravitational: PE = mass × g × height; Elastic: PE = 0.5 × k × x².

Power

Average = Work / time; Instantaneous = force × velocity.
Example: Push 20 N at 30° for 5 m → Work = 86.6 J.
Block on incline → compute work by each force.

💥 4.5 Impulse & Momentum

Momentum

Momentum = mass × velocity. Impulse = change in momentum = average force × time.

Collisions

Elastic: KE conserved; Inelastic: KE not conserved; perfectly inelastic → stick together.

Center of Mass

position = sum(mass × position)/total mass; velocity = sum(mass × velocity)/total mass.
Example: 2 kg ball rebounds from 2.5 m/s to 2.3 m/s in 0.2 s → impulse = 9.6 kg·m/s, force = 48 N.

📝 Key Equations

Force = mass × acceleration
Static friction ≤ coefficient × normal force
Kinetic friction = coefficient × normal force
Work = force × distance × cosθ
KE = 0.5 × mass × velocity²
PE = mass × g × height
Momentum = mass × velocity
Impulse = change in momentum
Conservation of momentum: m1u1 + m2u2 = m1v1 + m2v2
