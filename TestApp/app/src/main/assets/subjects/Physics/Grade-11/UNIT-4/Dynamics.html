⚙️ Introduction

Dynamics studies motion together with its cause – force. This unit covers Newton's laws, friction, equilibrium, work, energy, power, impulse, momentum, and center of mass.

📌 4.1 Force & Newton's Laws

Force

Push or pull (vector). Contact (friction, tension) or field (gravity, electromagnetic). SI unit: newton (N).

Fundamental forces: strong nuclear, electromagnetic, weak nuclear, gravitational.

Newton's first law (inertia)

Object at rest / constant velocity unless acted by net force. Inertia = resistance to change in motion; mass = measure of inertia.

[seatbelt example – inertia during crash]

Newton's second law

\(\vec{F}_{net}=m\vec{a}\) or \(\vec{F}_{net}=\frac{\Delta\vec{p}}{\Delta t}\)

Acceleration ∝ net force, ∝ 1/m.

Newton's third law

Action–reaction: \(\vec{F}_{AB}=-\vec{F}_{BA}\). Forces on different bodies, never cancel.

Example 4.1: Weight on moon = 1/6 of Earth weight.
Normal force: perpendicular support force. On incline: \(F_N = mg\cos\theta\).

🧱 4.2 Frictional Force

Types

Static friction: \(f_s \le \mu_s F_N\) ; maximum \(f_{s,max}=\mu_s F_N\).
Kinetic friction: \(f_k = \mu_k F_N\) (usually \(\mu_k < \mu_s\)).

Example 4.3: 8 kg block, μs=0.4 → F_needed to start = 31.4 N. To accelerate at 4 m/s², F_app = 55.5 N.
Example 4.4: Block on 30° incline, at rest → f_s = mg sin30 = 29.4 N, μs = tan30 = 0.58.

⚖️ 4.3 Equilibrium

\(\sum \vec{F}=0\) (translational equilibrium). Object at rest or constant velocity.

Example 4.9: Traffic light 100 N, angles 37° & 53° → tensions T1=60 N, T2=80 N.

💡 4.4 Work, Energy, Power

Work

\(W = \vec{F}\cdot\vec{s}=F s\cos\theta\) (Joule). Work can be positive, negative, zero.

Kinetic energy

\(K = \frac12 mv^2\)

Work–energy theorem

\(W_{net} = \Delta K = \frac12 mv_f^2 - \frac12 mv_i^2\)

Potential energy

Gravitational: \(U_g = mgh\) ; Elastic: \(U_e = \frac12 kx^2\)

Conservative / non‑conservative

Gravity, spring force – conservative. Friction – non‑conservative (dissipative).

Power

\(P_{avg}=W/t\) ; instantaneous \(P=\vec{F}\cdot\vec{v}\) (watt).

Example 4.10: F=20 N, 30°, s=5 m → W=86.6 J.
Example 4.13: Block pushed up incline – work by each force computed.
Example 4.16: Power from two forces on box → net power = 2 W.

🔄 4.5 Conservation of Energy

If only conservative forces: \(E_{mech}=K+U = constant\).
With non‑conservative: \(W_{nc} = \Delta E_{mech}\).

Example 4.17: 10 kg block slides 10 m smooth incline (30°) → v=9.9 m/s.
Example 4.18: with μ=0.2 → v=8 m/s.

💥 4.6 Impulse & Momentum

Momentum

\(\vec{p}=m\vec{v}\) (kg·m/s). Impulse \(\vec{J}=\vec{F}_{avg}\Delta t = \Delta\vec{p}\).

Conservation of momentum

For isolated system: \(\sum \vec{p}_i = \sum \vec{p}_f\).

Collisions

Elastic: KE conserved. Inelastic: KE not conserved; perfectly inelastic → objects stick.

Center of mass

\(x_{cm}=\frac{\sum m_i x_i}{\sum m_i}\), \(v_{cm}=\frac{\sum m_i v_i}{\sum m_i}\)

Example 4.23: 2 kg ball rebounds 2.3 m/s from 2.5 m/s, contact time 0.2 s → impulse = 9.6 kg·m/s, force = 48 N.
Example 4.25: elastic collision 4 kg & 6 kg, v1i=2, v2i=-1 → final v1=-1.6, v2=1.4 m/s.
Example 4.28: ballistic pendulum → bullet speed v = (m+M)/m √(2gh).

📝 Key Equations

\(\sum F = ma\) ; \(f_s\le\mu_s F_N\) ; \(f_k=\mu_k F_N\)
\(W=F s\cos\theta\) ; \(K=\frac12 mv^2\) ; \(U_g=mgh\) ; \(P=Fv\)
\(p=mv\) ; \(J=\Delta p\) ; \(m_1u_1+m_2u_2 = m_1v_1+m_2v_2\)

🎯 60 Dynamics Challenge Problems

Q1
A 5 kg block is pulled horizontally with a force of 20 N. The coefficient of kinetic friction is 0.2. Find acceleration. (g=10 m/s²)
✅ solution: \(F_N = mg = 50 N\), \(f_k = 0.2·50 =10 N\). Net force = 20-10=10 N. a = F_net/m = 10/5 = 2 m/s².
Q2
A 3 kg block is placed on a 30° incline. μs=0.4. Will it slide? If yes, find acceleration. (g=10)
✅ solution: mg sinθ = 30·0.5=15 N; max friction = μs mg cosθ = 0.4·30·0.866=10.39 N. 15 > 10.39 → slides. μk not given, assume μk=0.3 then f_k =0.3·30·0.866=7.794 N. a = (15-7.794)/3 = 2.402 m/s².
Q3
A force of 50 N at 37° above horizontal pulls a 10 kg box. μk=0.2. Find acceleration. (cos37=0.8, sin37=0.6, g=10)
✅ solution: Fx=50·0.8=40 N; Fy=50·0.6=30 N up. F_N = mg - Fy = 100-30=70 N. f_k = 0.2·70=14 N. Net force = 40-14=26 N. a = 26/10 = 2.6 m/s².
Q4
A 2 kg ball is dropped from 5 m and rebounds to 3.2 m. Find impulse from floor. (g=10)
✅ solution: v_before = √(2·10·5)=10 m/s down; v_after = √(2·10·3.2)=8 m/s up. Δp = m(v_after - v_before)=2(8 - (-10))=36 kg·m/s upward.
Q5
A 1000 kg car accelerates from 20 m/s to 30 m/s in 5 s. Find average force.
✅ solution: a = (30-20)/5 = 2 m/s². F = ma = 1000·2 = 2000 N.
Q6
Two masses m1=4 kg, m2=6 kg hang over pulley (Atwood). Find acceleration and tension. (g=10)
✅ solution: a = (m2-m1)g/(m1+m2)= (2·10)/10 = 2 m/s². T = 2m1m2g/(m1+m2)= 2·24·10/10 = 48 N.
Q7
A 0.5 kg ball moving at 10 m/s strikes a wall and rebounds at 6 m/s. Contact time 0.1 s. Find average force.
✅ solution: Δv = 6 - (-10) =16 m/s. Δp = 0.5·16=8 kg·m/s. F_avg = Δp/Δt = 8/0.1 = 80 N.
Q8
A 5 kg block is pushed up a 37° incline with initial speed 10 m/s. μk=0.2. How far up does it go? (g=10, sin37=0.6, cos37=0.8)
✅ solution: Forces down incline: mg sinθ + f_k = 50·0.6 + 0.2·50·0.8 =30+8=38 N. Deceleration a = -38/5 = -7.6 m/s². v² = u² + 2as → 0 = 100 +2(-7.6)s → s = 100/15.2 = 6.58 m.
Q9
A 10 kg box is pulled at constant speed on horizontal surface with μk=0.3. What force at 30° above horizontal?
✅ solution: constant speed → F_net=0. F cos30 = f_k = μk (mg - F sin30). F·0.866 = 0.3(100 - 0.5F) → 0.866F =30 -0.15F → 1.016F=30 → F=29.5 N.
Q10
A 2 kg stone at the end of a 1 m string is whirled in vertical circle. At the top, tension = 10 N. Find speed at top. (g=10)
✅ solution: At top: T + mg = mv²/r → 10+20 = 2·v²/1 → 30 = 2v² → v²=15 → v≈3.87 m/s.
