⚡ Introduction

This unit covers electrostatics (charges, Coulomb's law, electric field, potential) and electric circuits (current, resistance, Ohm's law, Kirchhoff's rules, capacitors). Fundamental for entrance exams.

🔋 6.1 Coulomb's Law

Properties of charges

  • Like charges repel, unlike attract.
  • Charge is conserved: total charge remains constant.
  • Charge is quantized: q = n e, where e = 1.6 × 10^-19 C.

Coulomb's law

F = k * q1 * q2 / r^2, with k = 8.99 × 10^9 N·m²/C².

Vector form: force acts along the line joining the charges.

Example 6.2: Two charges -1.0 × 10^-10 C each, 1 cm apart → repulsive force 9.0 × 10^-7 N.

Multiple charges

Superposition principle: net force = vector sum of individual forces.

🧲 6.2 Electric Fields

Electric field definition

E = F / q (test charge). For point charge: E = k |Q| / r^2, direction away from positive, toward negative.

Electric field lines

Start on positive, end on negative; density indicates strength; never cross.

Electric flux

Φ = E · A = E A cosθ.

Gauss's law: Φ = Q_inside / ε0, with ε0 = 8.85 × 10^-12.

Example 6.4: E due to 2.0 nC at 5.0 mm = 7.2 × 10^5 N/C away from charge.

🔌 6.3 Electric Potential

Electric potential energy & potential

U = k q Q / r, V = U / q = k Q / r.

Potential difference: ΔV = V_B - V_A = -∫ E·dl.

For uniform field: ΔV = E d.

Equipotential surfaces

Surfaces of constant V; perpendicular to E; no work moving along them.

Example 6.7: V from 1 μC at 1 mm = 9 × 10^6 V = 9 MV.

⚡ 6.4 Current, Resistance, Ohm's Law

Electric current

I = ΔQ / Δt (A). Direction = flow of positive charge.

Current density

J = I / A = σ E = n e v_d.

Resistance & resistivity

R = ρ L / A ; Ohm's law: V = I R (for ohmic materials).

Example 6.10: I=6 mA, A=12 mm² → J = 500 A/m².

🔗 6.5 Resistors in series & parallel

Series

R_eq = R1 + R2 + ... ; same current, voltage divides.

Parallel

1/R_eq = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + ... ; same voltage, current divides.

Example 6.12: 5Ω, 4Ω||12Ω, 8Ω → R_eq = 5+3+8=16Ω; currents: 1.5 A through 5Ω, 8Ω; 1.125 A through 4Ω, 0.375 A through 12Ω.

📦 6.6 Capacitors

Capacitance

C = Q / V (F). Parallel plate: C = ε0 A / d, with dielectric C = κ C0.

Series/parallel capacitors

Parallel: C_eq = C1 + C2 + ... ; Series: 1/C_eq = 1/C1 + 1/C2 + ...

Energy stored

U = 1/2 C V^2 = Q^2 / (2C).

Example 6.19: A=200 cm², d=2 mm, κ=6, V=12V → C=5.31×10^-10 F, Q=6.37 nC.

🔄 6.7 Kirchhoff's Rules

Junction rule

Sum of currents entering = sum leaving (charge conservation).

Loop rule

Sum of potential changes around any closed loop = 0 (energy conservation).

Example 6.17: Multi‑loop circuit → solve simultaneous equations for currents.

📌 Key Equations

F = k q1 q2 / r^2 ; E = F/q = k Q / r^2
V = k Q / r ; ΔV = E d
I = ΔQ / Δt ; V = I R ; R = ρ L / A
C = Q / V = ε0 A / d ; 1/C_series = 1/C1 + 1/C2 ... ; C_parallel = C1 + C2 ...
Kirchhoff: Σ I_in = Σ I_out ; Σ ΔV = 0

🎯 70 Electrostatics & Circuits Problems

Q1
Two charges q1 = +2 μC and q2 = -3 μC are 10 cm apart. Find the force on each (magnitude and direction).
✅ solution: F = k |q1 q2| / r^2 = 8.99e9 * 2e-6 * 3e-6 / (0.1)^2 = 8.99e9 * 6e-12 / 0.01 = 8.99e9 * 6e-10 = 5.394 N. Attractive, so force on q1 is toward q2, on q2 toward q1.
Q2
Three charges q1 = +4 μC at (0,0), q2 = -2 μC at (0.3 m, 0), q3 = +5 μC at (0,0.4 m). Find net force on q1.
✅ solution: Force from q2 (attractive) magnitude = k·4e-6·2e-6/(0.3)^2 = 8.99e9·8e-12/0.09 = 8.99e9·8.888e-11 = 0.799 N in +x. Force from q3 (repulsive) magnitude = k·4e-6·5e-6/(0.4)^2 = 8.99e9·20e-12/0.16 = 8.99e9·1.25e-10 = 1.124 N in +y. Net force = √(0.799^2 + 1.124^2) = 1.38 N at θ = arctan(1.124/0.799) = 54.6° above +x.
Q3
What is the electric field at (0,0) due to a charge Q = +6 μC placed at (0.2 m, 0)?
✅ solution: E = k Q / r^2 = 8.99e9 * 6e-6 / (0.2)^2 = 8.99e9 * 6e-6 / 0.04 = 8.99e9 * 1.5e-4 = 1.3485e6 N/C directed away from Q, so along -x direction.
Q4
Two point charges q1 = +5 nC at x=0, q2 = -5 nC at x=10 cm. Find E at x=5 cm.
✅ solution: At midpoint, fields from both are in same direction (toward negative charge). E1 = k·5e-9/(0.05)^2 = 8.99e9·5e-9/0.0025 = 8.99e9·2e-6 = 17980 N/C (away from q1, so right). E2 = same magnitude but toward q2 (also right). Total = 35960 N/C right.

✅ solution: I = 12/(3+6) = 1.333 A. V6 = 1.333·6 = 8 V.
Q66
A 100 μF capacitor is charged to 50 V and then discharged through a 10 kΩ resistor. Find time constant and initial current.
✅ solution: τ = R C = 10000·100e-6 = 1 s. I_initial = V/R = 50/10000 = 5 mA.
Q67
A 2 μC charge is at (0,0), a -3 μC at (0.3 m, 0). Find point on x-axis where V=0.
✅ solution: Let x between 0 and 0.3. Then k·2/x + k·(-3)/(0.3-x) = 0 → 2/x = 3/(0.3-x) → 2(0.3-x) = 3x → 0.6 -2x = 3x → 0.6 = 5x → x = 0.12 m. For x>0.3, equation leads to negative x, so only x=0.12 m.
Q68
A 10 Ω and 20 Ω are in parallel with a 30 Ω in series with this combination. All connected to 24 V. Find power in 20 Ω.
✅ solution: Parallel: 1/Rp = 1/10+1/20 = 0.1+0.05=0.15 → Rp=6.667 Ω. Total R = 6.667+30=36.667 Ω. I_total = 24/36.667 = 0.6545 A. V_parallel = 0.6545·6.667 = 4.3636 V. I20 = 4.3636/20 = 0.21818 A. P20 = I^2·20 = 0.04762·20 = 0.9524 W.
Q69
A 4 μF capacitor is charged to 60 V and then connected across an uncharged 6 μF capacitor. Find final charge on 4 μF.
✅ solution: Initial Q = 4·60 = 240 μC. Final V = 240/10 = 24 V. Q4 = 4·24 = 96 μC.
Q70
A 1.5 V cell with internal resistance 0.5 Ω is short‑circuited. Find short‑circuit current and power dissipated internally.
✅ solution: I_sc = 1.5/0.5 = 3 A. Power dissipated = I^2 r = 9·0.5 = 4.5 W.
