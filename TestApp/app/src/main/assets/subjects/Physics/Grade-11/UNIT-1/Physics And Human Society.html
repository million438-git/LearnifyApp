🔭 Introduction

Physics is a major driver of innovation and economic growth worldwide — from energy, security, advanced manufacturing, data analysis to healthcare, finance and communication.

In this chapter you will understand the career opportunities of physics, its contribution to individuals and economic growth, and the methods of gaining scientific knowledge.
After this unit, you will be able to:
  • Get acquainted with the impact of physics on society.
  • Familiarize yourself with physics communities and their roles.
  • Develop basic understanding of the making of physics knowledge.
  • Familiarize yourself with basic principles and applications of physics in various disciplines.
  • Acquire basic knowledge and understanding of nature and appreciate it.
  • Update yourself with the current status of physics.

📌 1.1 Importance of Physics to Society

🧠 Core Ideas

  • Physics generates fundamental knowledge needed for future technological advances.
  • It contributes to technological infrastructure and provides trained personnel.
  • Essential for chemists, engineers, computer scientists, and biomedical sciences.
  • Helps develop strategies to combat climate change and develop cleaner energies.
  • Improves quality of life: medical imaging (CT, MRI, PET), laser surgery, etc.
  • Modern conveniences (TV, computers, phones) rely on physics discoveries: magnetism, electricity, conductors.
  • Transportation (aircraft, automobiles, space shuttles) depend on physics.
  • Military applications: weapons design, nuclear physics.

🔬 1.3 Making of Physics Knowledge

Two types of knowledge

  • A posteriori (experiential): gained through experience — sensory perception, introspection, memory, testimony.
  • A priori (non-experiential): gained through reason — logic, mathematics (e.g., 2+2=4).

🔎 Experiential knowledge sources

  • Sensory perception: seeing, hearing, touching.
  • Introspection: looking into our own feelings and thoughts.
  • Memory: recording and recalling events.
  • Testimony: information from written or oral sources.

⚗️ Scientific Method

Ordered series of steps to acquire knowledge based on experimental evidence.

Example: Boiling water at different altitudes
Observation → Question → Research → Hypothesis → Experiment → Analysis → Conclusion.

📡 1.5 Current Status of Physics

Major recent discoveries (Nobel prizes, breakthroughs)

  • Exoplanets: planets outside our solar system. Michel Mayor & Didier Queloz (Nobel 2019). As of Oct 2022: 5,197 confirmed exoplanets.
  • Black hole: region of spacetime with gravity so strong nothing escapes. Roger Penrose, Reinhard Genzel, Andrea Ghez (Nobel 2020) — discovery of supermassive black hole at Milky Way's center.
  • Quantum cryptography: secure information exchange using quantum physics.
  • Higgs boson: discovered at CERN in 2012 (60 years after prediction).
  • Gravitational wave: predicted by Einstein 1916, detected by LIGO in 2016.
  • Global warming modelling: Syukuro Manabe & Klaus Hasselmann (Nobel 2021).
  • James Webb Space Telescope (JWST): infrared telescope 1.5 million km from Earth; studies galaxy formation, star birth, exoplanets.

🎯 20 Advanced Exam Questions – Full Answers

Q1
Explain the role of physics in the development of medical imaging technologies such as MRI and CT scans. How do these applications directly improve quality of life?
✅ full answer: Physics provides the fundamental understanding of electromagnetism, nuclear magnetic resonance, and radiation that underlies modern medical imaging. MRI (Magnetic Resonance Imaging) uses strong magnetic fields and radio waves to align hydrogen nuclei in the body; the signals emitted as they relax are processed into detailed images of soft tissues. CT (Computed Tomography) uses X‑rays and computer reconstruction to create cross‑sectional images. These technologies enable non‑invasive diagnosis, early detection of diseases (tumors, cardiovascular issues), and precise guidance for surgeries and treatments, directly improving patient outcomes, reducing mortality, and enhancing quality of life.
Q2
List four major areas where physics contributes to technological infrastructure and trained personnel. Give a concrete example for each.
✅ full answer: 1) Energy – physics enables design of solar panels (photovoltaics based on semiconductor physics) and nuclear reactors. 2) Healthcare – radiation therapy (using particle accelerators and dosimetry) treats cancer. 3) Transportation – aerodynamics of aircraft (fluid mechanics) reduces drag and improves fuel efficiency. 4) Communication – fibre optics (total internal reflection) allows high‑speed internet. Trained physicists are essential in R&D, design, and maintenance of these technologies.
Q3
What is the mission of the Ethiopian Physical Society (EPS)? List at least three of its objectives.
✅ full answer: EPS promotes physics education and research in Ethiopia. Its mission is to advance physics and its applications for national development. Objectives: (i) organize conferences on physics education and research to exchange knowledge; (ii) popularize physics among students and the public to develop interest; (iii) promote active participation in curriculum design and teaching methods; (iv) create means for disseminating scientific information and foster collaboration.
Q4
Distinguish between a posteriori (experiential) knowledge and a priori (non‑experiential) knowledge. Give one example of each from the context of physics.
✅ full answer: A posteriori knowledge is gained through experience, observation, or experiment. For example, measuring the boiling point of water at different altitudes (experiment) gives empirical data. A priori knowledge is derived from reason and logic, independent of experience. For example, mathematical truths like 2+2=4, or the Pythagorean theorem used in vector addition, are known a priori. In physics, both are essential: a priori reasoning builds theories, and a posteriori experimentation tests them.
Q5
Describe the steps of the scientific method using the example of how the boiling point of water varies with altitude.
✅ full answer: Steps: 1) Observation – people notice water boils at lower temperature on mountains. 2) Question – why does boiling point change with altitude? 3) Research – background on atmospheric pressure. 4) Hypothesis – boiling point decreases as atmospheric pressure decreases (higher altitude). 5) Experiment – measure boiling point at various altitudes (sea level, 1000 m, 2000 m). 6) Analysis – compare data, plot graph, observe trend. 7) Conclusion – data support hypothesis; boiling point indeed decreases with altitude. This illustrates the iterative process of scientific inquiry.
Q6
Why are physics communities like the African Association of Physics Students (AAPS) important for developing countries? Give two reasons.
✅ full answer: AAPS encourages physics students in scientific and professional work, promotes international collaboration, and supplements education with international experience. For developing countries, this: (a) builds local expertise by connecting students to global networks and resources; (b) fosters research and innovation, which drives technological and economic growth; (c) creates opportunities for scholarships, mentorship, and exposure to modern physics, thereby strengthening the scientific community.
Q7
List three distinct career paths that a physics graduate can pursue, and briefly describe the role of physics in each.
✅ full answer: 1) Medical physicist – uses radiation (X‑rays, gamma rays) for diagnosis and therapy; ensures safe and effective use of radiation in hospitals. 2) Data scientist – applies physics modelling, statistical analysis, and numerical skills to interpret complex data in finance, tech, or research. 3) Renewable energy engineer – designs solar panels (photovoltaics) or wind turbines using principles of thermodynamics and electromagnetism to optimise energy conversion. Physics provides the fundamental understanding and problem‑solving abilities essential for these roles.
Q8
What was the 2020 Nobel Prize in Physics awarded for? Briefly explain the discovery.
✅ full answer: The 2020 Nobel Prize in Physics was awarded to Roger Penrose, Reinhard Genzel, and Andrea Ghez for their discoveries concerning black holes. Penrose proved that black hole formation is a robust prediction of general relativity. Genzel and Ghez independently observed the motion of stars at the centre of our Milky Way galaxy, providing evidence for a supermassive compact object (black hole) with 4 million times the Sun's mass.
Q9
Explain how physics contributes to combating climate change. Provide two specific examples.
✅ full answer: Physics develops cleaner energy technologies and provides climate models. Example 1: Solar photovoltaic cells convert sunlight into electricity using semiconductor physics, reducing reliance on fossil fuels. Example 2: Understanding radiative transfer and thermodynamics allows climate scientists to model global warming (Nobel 2021 to Manabe and Hasselmann), predict extreme weather, and inform policy. Physics also improves energy efficiency in buildings, transport, and industry.
Q10
What is the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) and why is it significant for physics?
✅ full answer: JWST is an infrared space telescope launched in 2021, orbiting 1.5 million km from Earth. It observes the universe in infrared, allowing it to peer through dust clouds and study the first galaxies, star formation, and exoplanet atmospheres. Its significance lies in advancing astrophysics: testing theories of galaxy evolution, understanding planetary systems, and probing fundamental physics in extreme environments (e.g., high‑redshift universe). It represents cutting‑edge optics, cryogenics, and orbital mechanics.
Q11
Identify the four main sources of experiential knowledge according to the textbook, and give a physics‑related example of each.
✅ full answer: The four sources: 1) Sensory perception – seeing the colour of a heated metal change (red to white). 2) Introspection – realising you feel heavier in an accelerating elevator. 3) Memory – recalling the formula for velocity (v = s/t). 4) Testimony – learning about gravitational waves from a lecture or scientific paper. These sources together form the basis of empirical knowledge in physics.
Q12
Why is international collaboration common in modern physics research (e.g., CERN, LIGO)? Give at least two reasons.
✅ full answer: International collaboration is essential because large‑scale experiments (like the Large Hadron Collider or LIGO) are extremely expensive (billions of dollars) and technically complex, requiring expertise from many fields. Collaboration shares costs and risks, pools intellectual resources, and accelerates discovery through data sharing and peer review. It also fosters peaceful scientific exchange and builds global scientific capacity.
Q13
Describe the role of the Ethiopian Science and Art Museum (inaugurated 2022) in promoting physics education.
✅ full answer: The museum, located in Addis Ababa, features interactive display screens, a planetarium, and exhibits on cybersecurity, GIS, manufacturing, and robotics. It makes physics tangible and exciting, stimulating curiosity and attracting young students toward science. By demonstrating real‑world applications, it helps bridge the gap between classroom theory and practical innovation, thereby promoting STEM education and inspiring future scientists.
Q14
Explain the difference between the mission of EPSNA and that of AAPS.
✅ full answer: EPSNA (Ethiopian Physics Society in North America) focuses on promoting physics education and research among Ethiopians in Ethiopia and North America by donating educational materials, field equipment, computing facilities, and providing scholarships. AAPS (African Association of Physics Students) is a student‑run continental organisation that encourages physics students across Africa in scientific and professional work, fostering international collaboration and networking. While EPSNA is diaspora‑centered, AAPS is pan‑African and student‑focused.
Q15
What is quantum cryptography and why is it considered a major physics‑based advancement?
✅ full answer: Quantum cryptography uses principles of quantum mechanics (e.g., superposition, entanglement, and the no‑cloning theorem) to secure information exchange. The most famous example is quantum key distribution (QKD), where any eavesdropping attempt disturbs the quantum state and is detectable. It provides theoretically unbreakable encryption, protecting sensitive data like bank accounts and passwords. This advancement harnesses fundamental quantum physics to solve real‑world security problems.
Q16
List at least five technological benefits that physics has contributed to society (other than medical).
✅ full answer: 1) GPS devices – rely on relativity (time dilation) and atomic clocks. 2) Semiconductors – transistors, computers, microchips (quantum physics). 3) Lasers – barcode scanners, fibre‑optic communication, welding. 4) X‑rays – security scanning in airports. 5) Radio transmitters – wireless communication, Wi‑Fi. 6) Solar panels – photovoltaics for renewable energy. 7) Microwave ovens – electromagnetic waves. 8) Touchscreens – capacitive sensing. These technologies have revolutionised daily life.
Q17
How did the discovery of exoplanets (Nobel 2019) change our understanding of the universe?
✅ full answer: The discovery of exoplanets (planets orbiting stars beyond our solar system) by Michel Mayor and Didier Queloz in 1995 proved that planetary systems are common. As of 2022, over 5,000 exoplanets are known, with diverse sizes, orbits, and compositions. This revolutionised planetary science, challenging models of planet formation and opening the search for extraterrestrial life. It also refined our understanding of stellar evolution and the uniqueness of our solar system.
Q18
Explain the concept of ‘testimony’ as a source of a posteriori knowledge. Why is its reliability important in physics?
✅ full answer: Testimony is knowledge gained from written or oral sources, such as textbooks, lectures, or scientific papers. In physics, we rely on testimony to learn about discoveries and theories that we cannot directly experience. Reliability depends on the credibility of the source (peer review, reputation of the author, replication of results). If testimony is unreliable, it can propagate errors. Hence physicists emphasise peer‑reviewed publications and independent verification to ensure knowledge is accurate.
Q19
What is the significance of the Higgs boson discovery (2012) for particle physics?
✅ full answer: The Higgs boson, discovered at CERN in 2012, confirmed the Brout‑Englert‑Higgs mechanism, which gives mass to elementary particles. It was the last missing piece of the Standard Model of particle physics. Its discovery validated decades of theoretical work and opened new questions about the nature of mass, symmetry breaking, and the early universe. It also demonstrates the power of international collaboration and large‑scale experiments.
Q20
Based on the chapter, what skills do physics graduates possess that are in high demand across diverse sectors?
✅ full answer: Physics graduates develop strong analytical and problem‑solving skills, numeracy, data analysis, modelling, and the ability to communicate complex ideas clearly. They are trained to think critically, design experiments, and use advanced technology. These skills are highly valued in finance (quantitative analysis), engineering (design and innovation), information technology (algorithms, data science), research and development, education, and even policy making. Physics provides a versatile foundation for many careers.
