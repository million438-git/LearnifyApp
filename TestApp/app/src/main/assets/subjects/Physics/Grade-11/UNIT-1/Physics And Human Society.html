🔭 Introduction

Physics is a major driver of innovation and economic growth worldwide — from energy, security, advanced manufacturing, data analysis to healthcare, finance and communication.

In this chapter you will understand the career opportunities of physics, its contribution to individuals and economic growth, and the methods of gaining scientific knowledge.
After this unit, you will be able to:
  • Get acquainted with the impact of physics on society.
  • Familiarize yourself with physics communities and their roles.
  • Develop basic understanding of the making of physics knowledge.
  • Familiarize yourself with basic principles and applications of physics in various disciplines.
  • Acquire basic knowledge and understanding of nature and appreciate it.
  • Update yourself with the current status of physics.

📌 1.1 Importance of Physics to Society

🧠 Core Ideas

  • Physics generates fundamental knowledge needed for future technological advances.
  • It contributes to technological infrastructure and provides trained personnel.
  • Essential for chemists, engineers, computer scientists, and biomedical sciences.
  • Helps develop strategies to combat climate change and develop cleaner energies.
  • Improves quality of life: medical imaging (CT, MRI, PET), laser surgery, etc.
  • Modern conveniences (TV, computers, phones) rely on physics discoveries: magnetism, electricity, conductors.
  • Transportation (aircraft, automobiles, space shuttles) depend on physics.
  • Military applications: weapons design, nuclear physics.

🌍 1.2 Physics Communities and Their Roles

Definition

Physics communities are organized groups of persons associated for scientific purposes — at national, continental, and worldwide levels.

🇪🇹 Ethiopian Physical Society (EPS)

  • Promotes physics education and research in Ethiopia.
  • Organizes conferences and exchanges scientific information.
  • Popularizes physics to develop student interest.
  • Promotes active participation in curriculum design.
  • Creates means for disseminating scientific information.

🌐 Ethiopian Physics Society in North America (EPSNA)

  • Promotes physics education and research among Ethiopians in Ethiopia and North America.
  • Donates educational materials, field equipment, computing facilities, and provides scholarships.

🌍 African Association of Physics Students (AAPS)

  • Student-run educational association for physics students and recent graduates.
  • Encourages scientific and professional work in African and international contexts.
  • Affiliated with physics societies and departments.

🔬 1.3 Making of Physics Knowledge

Two types of knowledge

  • A posteriori (experiential): gained through experience — sensory perception, introspection, memory, testimony.
  • A priori (non-experiential): gained through reason — logic, mathematics (e.g., 2+2=4).

🔎 Experiential knowledge sources

  • Sensory perception: seeing, hearing, touching.
  • Introspection: looking into our own feelings and thoughts.
  • Memory: recording and recalling events.
  • Testimony: information from written or oral sources.

⚗️ Scientific Method

Ordered series of steps to acquire knowledge based on experimental evidence.

Example: Boiling water at different altitudes
Observation → Question → Research → Hypothesis → Experiment → Analysis → Conclusion.

🚀 1.4 Mission of Physics & Career Awareness

Mission

To advance science, engineering, and innovation throughout the world for the benefit of all and serving society.

💼 Typical careers in physics

  • Space & astronomy: astronomer, collecting data from satellites, telescopes.
  • Healthcare: medical physicist (radiology, radiation oncology, nuclear medicine).
  • Engineering: improving products, manufacturing processes (medicine, energy, transport, defense).
  • Energy: renewable (wind, solar) and non‑renewable (oil, gas) — efficient extraction, R&D.
  • Technology: robotics, nanoscience, nanotechnology — in research centers and tech companies.
  • Geophysics & meteorology: natural disaster prediction, weather forecasting, climate research.
  • Research scientist: astrophysics, particle physics, biotechnology, nanotechnology, climate science.

📡 1.5 Current Status of Physics

Major recent discoveries (Nobel prizes, breakthroughs)

  • Exoplanets: planets outside our solar system. Michel Mayor & Didier Queloz (Nobel 2019). As of Oct 2022: 5,197 confirmed exoplanets.
  • Black hole: region of spacetime with gravity so strong nothing escapes. Roger Penrose, Reinhard Genzel, Andrea Ghez (Nobel 2020) — discovery of supermassive black hole at Milky Way's center.
  • Quantum cryptography: secure information exchange using quantum physics.
  • Higgs boson: discovered at CERN in 2012 (60 years after prediction).
  • Gravitational wave: predicted by Einstein 1916, detected by LIGO in 2016.
  • Global warming modelling: Syukuro Manabe & Klaus Hasselmann (Nobel 2021).
  • James Webb Space Telescope (JWST): infrared telescope 1.5 million km from Earth; studies galaxy formation, star birth, exoplanets.
🌍 Ethiopia's role: Ethiopian Science and Art Museum inaugurated in 2022 (Addis Ababa) — includes planetarium, interactive displays, cyber security, GIS, robotics. Stimulates young students toward science.

🎯 20 Advanced Exam Questions

Q1
Explain the role of physics in the development of medical imaging technologies such as MRI and CT scans. How do these applications directly improve quality of life?
✅ answer: Physics provides the fundamental understanding necessary for developing new instrumentation. MRI uses nuclear magnetic resonance (a physics principle) to image soft tissues; CT scans use X‑ray physics and computer reconstruction. They enable non‑invasive diagnosis, early disease detection, and precise treatment, directly improving patient outcomes and quality of life.
Q2
List four major areas where physics contributes to technological infrastructure and trained personnel. Give a concrete example for each.
✅ answer: 1) Energy – design of solar panels (photovoltaics). 2) Healthcare – radiation therapy for cancer. 3) Transportation – aerodynamics of aircraft. 4) Communication – fibre optics and satellite transmission.
Q3
What is the mission of the Ethiopian Physical Society (EPS)? List at least three of its objectives.
✅ answer: EPS promotes physics education and research in Ethiopia. Objectives: (i) organize conferences on physics education, (ii) popularize physics to develop student interest, (iii) promote active participation in curriculum design, (iv) create means for disseminating scientific information.
Q4
Distinguish between a posteriori (experiential) knowledge and a priori (non‑experiential) knowledge. Give one example of each from the context of physics.
✅ answer: A posteriori knowledge comes from experience (e.g., measuring the boiling point of water at different altitudes). A priori knowledge comes from reason/logic (e.g., mathematical truth 2+2=4, or the Pythagorean theorem used in vector addition).
Q5
Describe the steps of the scientific method using the example of how the boiling point of water varies with altitude.
✅ answer: Observation → Question → Research → Hypothesis → Experiment → Analysis → Conclusion. Example: Observing that water boils at a lower temperature in mountains → hypothesis that boiling point depends on atmospheric pressure → experiment measuring boiling point at different altitudes → analysis shows decrease with altitude → conclusion supports hypothesis.
Q6
Why are physics communities like the African Association of Physics Students (AAPS) important for developing countries? Give two reasons.
✅ answer: They encourage physics students in scientific and professional work, promote international collaboration, and supplement physics education with international experience. This helps build local expertise, fosters research, and connects students to global scientific networks, which is vital for technological growth.
Q7
List three distinct career paths that a physics graduate can pursue, and briefly describe the role of physics in each.
✅ answer: 1) Medical physicist – uses radiation for diagnosis/therapy (oncology). 2) Data scientist – applies physics modelling and numerical skills to analyse complex data. 3) Renewable energy engineer – designs efficient solar/wind systems using principles of thermodynamics and electromagnetism.
Q8
What was the 2020 Nobel Prize in Physics awarded for? Briefly explain the discovery.
✅ answer: The discovery of the supermassive compact object (black hole) at the centre of our Milky Way galaxy. Roger Penrose, Reinhard Genzel, and Andrea Ghez contributed to black hole formation and observation.
Q9
Explain how physics contributes to combating climate change. Provide two specific examples.
✅ answer: Physics develops cleaner energy technologies (solar cells, wind turbines) and provides climate models that predict global warming. For instance, understanding thermodynamics and radiation helps improve energy efficiency and model atmospheric changes (Nobel 2021 to Manabe and Hasselmann).
Q10
What is the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) and why is it significant for physics?
✅ answer: JWST is an infrared telescope located 1.5 million km from Earth. It studies galaxy assembly, star birth, exoplanets, and objects in our solar system. It represents cutting‑edge physics (optics, thermodynamics, orbital mechanics) and expands our understanding of the universe.
Q11
Identify the four main sources of experiential knowledge according to the textbook, and give a physics‑related example of each.
✅ answer: 1) Sensory perception – observing the colour of a heated metal. 2) Introspection – realising you feel heavier in an accelerating elevator. 3) Memory – recalling the formula for velocity. 4) Testimony – learning about gravitational waves from a lecture or paper.
Q12
Why is international collaboration common in modern physics research (e.g., CERN, LIGO)? Give at least two reasons.
✅ answer: Research at the frontier (like Higgs boson or gravitational waves) is extremely expensive (billions of dollars) and requires expertise from many fields. Collaboration shares costs, pools intellectual resources, and accelerates discovery.
Q13
Describe the role of the Ethiopian Science and Art Museum (inaugurated 2022) in promoting physics education.
✅ answer: It features interactive display screens, a planetarium, and exhibits on cybersecurity, GIS, manufacturing, and robotics. It stimulates and attracts young students towards science by making physics tangible and inspiring.
Q14
Explain the difference between the mission of EPSNA and that of AAPS.
✅ answer: EPSNA (Ethiopian Physics Society in North America) focuses on promoting physics education and research among Ethiopians in Ethiopia and North America by donating materials, equipment, and scholarships. AAPS (African Association of Physics Students) is student‑run and aims to encourage physics students across Africa in scientific and professional work within an international context.
Q15
What is quantum cryptography and why is it considered a major physics‑based advancement?
✅ answer: Quantum cryptography uses principles of quantum physics (e.g., superposition, entanglement) to secure information exchange. It provides theoretically unbreakable encryption, protecting sensitive data like bank accounts and passwords.
Q16
List at least five technological benefits that physics has contributed to society (other than medical).
✅ answer: 1) GPS devices (relativity). 2) Semiconductors (transistors, computers). 3) Lasers (barcode scanners, fibre optics). 4) X‑rays (security scanning). 5) Radio transmitters (communication). 6) Solar panels (renewable energy).
Q17
How did the discovery of exoplanets (Nobel 2019) change our understanding of the universe?
✅ answer: It confirmed that planets outside our solar system are common, revolutionising planetary science and the search for life. As of 2022, over 5,000 exoplanets are known, showing diverse systems and challenging previous models.
Q18
Explain the concept of ‘testimony’ as a source of a posteriori knowledge. Why is its reliability important in physics?
✅ answer: Testimony is knowledge gained from written or oral sources (books, lectures). Reliability depends on the integrity of the author and methods. In physics, we rely on peer‑reviewed publications and reputable sources to build accurate knowledge.
Q19
What is the significance of the Higgs boson discovery (2012) for particle physics?
✅ answer: The Higgs boson confirms the mechanism that gives mass to elementary particles (Brout‑Englert‑Higgs mechanism). It completed the Standard Model of particle physics and opened new avenues for understanding the early universe.
Q20
Based on the chapter, what skills do physics graduates possess that are in high demand across diverse sectors?
✅ answer: Numeracy, problem‑solving, data analysis, communication of complex ideas, and a wide understanding of how the world works on a scientific and human level. These are valuable in finance, engineering, IT, research, and education.
