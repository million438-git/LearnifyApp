5.1 Electric current

Current is the rate of flow of electric charge through a conductor. It is measured in amperes (A).

Conventional current direction is from positive to negative terminal (opposite to electron flow).

📘 REMEMBER: Current I = charge / time (I = Q / t) – in words, current equals charge divided by time.
💡 WOW: A current of 1 ampere means 1 coulomb of charge passes a point every second – that's about 6.24×10¹⁸ electrons!

5.2 Resistance & Ohm’s law

Resistance (R) measures how much a component opposes current. Unit: ohm (Ω).

Ohm’s law: voltage across a resistor equals current times resistance – V = I × R.

Resistance depends on material, length, cross‑sectional area, and temperature: R = resistivity × length / area.

📘 REMEMBER: In series, R_total = R₁ + R₂ + … ; in parallel, 1/R_total = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + …

5.3 Electric circuits & Kirchhoff’s laws

  • Series circuit: current same everywhere, voltage divides.
  • Parallel circuit: voltage same across each branch, current divides.
  • Kirchhoff’s current law (junction): total current entering = total current leaving.
  • Kirchhoff’s voltage law (loop): sum of voltage rises = sum of voltage drops.

5.4 Electric power & energy

Power (P) = voltage × current = I² R = V² / R. Unit: watt (W).

Electrical energy (E) = power × time. In daily life, energy is measured in kilowatt‑hours (kWh).

💰 WOW: 1 kWh is the energy used by a 1000 W appliance running for 1 hour – that’s what your electricity bill charges!

5.5 AC & DC

Direct current (DC) – constant direction (batteries).

Alternating current (AC) – reverses direction periodically; standard frequency in Ethiopia is 50 Hz.

Peak voltage V₀ and RMS voltage V_rms = V₀ / √2. For AC, I_rms = V_rms / Z.

5.6 Reactance & impedance

Capacitive reactance X_C = 1 / (2πfC) – opposition from capacitor.

Inductive reactance X_L = 2πfL – opposition from inductor.

Impedance Z = √(R² + (X_L – X_C)²) for series RLC. Phase angle φ = arctan((X_L–X_C)/R).

📘 REMEMBER: At resonance (X_L = X_C), impedance is minimum (=R) and current is maximum.

5.7 Power factor & transformers

Power factor = cos φ = R / Z. It tells how much of the apparent power is real power.

Transformer: V_p / V_s = N_p / N_s = I_s / I_p (ideal). Step‑up increases voltage, step‑down decreases.

🏭 WOW: Power factor correction saves industries huge amounts of money!

Q1 (conceptual)
Define electric current and state its SI unit.
Electric current is the rate of flow of electric charge through a conductor. SI unit: ampere (A).
📘 REMEMBER: 1 A = 1 coulomb per second.
Q2 (conceptual)
What is the direction of conventional current? How does it differ from electron flow?
Conventional current flows from positive to negative terminal. Electron flow is from negative to positive – opposite to conventional current.
⚡ WOW: This is just a historical convention; physics still works!
Q3 (conceptual)
State Ohm’s law in words and give its formula.
Ohm’s law: The voltage across a conductor is directly proportional to the current flowing through it, provided temperature and other physical conditions remain constant. Formula: V = I × R (voltage equals current times resistance).
Q4 (conceptual)
Define resistance. What factors does it depend on?
Resistance is the opposition a material offers to current. It depends on: material (resistivity), length (longer → higher R), cross‑sectional area (thicker → lower R), and temperature.
Q5 (conceptual)
How do you combine resistors in series? Give the formula in words.
In series, the total resistance is the sum of individual resistances: R_total = R₁ + R₂ + R₃ + …
Q6 (conceptual)
How do you combine resistors in parallel? Write the rule in words.
For parallel resistors, the reciprocal of the total resistance equals the sum of the reciprocals of each resistance: 1/R_total = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + …
Q7 (conceptual)
State Kirchhoff’s current law (junction rule).
Kirchhoff’s current law: At any junction, the sum of currents entering the junction equals the sum of currents leaving it.
Q8 (conceptual)
State Kirchhoff’s voltage law (loop rule).
Kirchhoff’s voltage law: The algebraic sum of all the potential differences around any closed loop is zero.
Q9 (conceptual)
What is the difference between series and parallel circuits regarding current and voltage?
In series: current is the same through all components, voltage divides. In parallel: voltage is the same across all branches, current divides.
Q10 (conceptual)
Define electric power and write its formula in terms of voltage and current.
Electric power is the rate at which electrical energy is transferred. Formula: P = V × I (power equals voltage times current).
Q11 (conceptual)
What is a kilowatt‑hour? What does it measure?
A kilowatt‑hour (kWh) is a unit of energy. It is the energy consumed by a 1000‑watt device running for one hour. 1 kWh = 3.6 million joules.
Q12 (conceptual)
Distinguish between AC and DC.
DC (direct current) flows in one direction constantly. AC (alternating current) reverses direction periodically. AC is used in homes; DC in batteries.
Q13 (conceptual)
What is the frequency of AC in Ethiopia? What does it mean?
50 hertz (Hz). It means the current changes direction 50 times per second.
Q14 (conceptual)
Define capacitive reactance. What is its SI unit?
Capacitive reactance is the opposition to AC offered by a capacitor. It decreases with higher frequency and higher capacitance. Unit: ohm (Ω).
Q15 (conceptual)
Define inductive reactance. What is its SI unit?
Inductive reactance is the opposition to AC offered by an inductor. It increases with higher frequency and higher inductance. Unit: ohm (Ω).
Q16 (conceptual)
What is impedance in an AC circuit?
Impedance Z is the total opposition to AC, combining resistance R, capacitive reactance X_C, and inductive reactance X_L. For series RLC: Z = square root of [R² + (X_L – X_C)²].
Q17 (conceptual)
What is resonance in an AC circuit? What happens to current at resonance?
Resonance occurs when X_L = X_C. Then impedance is minimum (=R) and current is maximum.
Q18 (conceptual)
Define power factor. What is its ideal value?
Power factor = cos φ = R / Z. It indicates the fraction of apparent power that is real power. Ideal value is 1 (when phase angle φ = 0).
Q19 (conceptual)
What is a transformer? Name its two types.
A transformer is a device that changes AC voltage. Step‑up transformer increases voltage; step‑down decreases voltage.
Q20 (conceptual)
State the transformer equation relating primary and secondary voltages and turns.
For an ideal transformer: V_p / V_s = N_p / N_s, where V_p = primary voltage, V_s = secondary voltage, N_p = primary turns, N_s = secondary turns.
Q21 (conceptual)
What is the relationship between current and voltage in an ideal transformer?
V_p × I_p = V_s × I_s (power in equals power out). So current ratio is inverse of voltage ratio: I_p / I_s = N_s / N_p.
Q22 (conceptual)
Define electromotive force (emf) and distinguish it from terminal voltage.
Emf is the energy per unit charge supplied by a source. Terminal voltage is the voltage across the source when current is drawn – it is less than emf due to internal resistance.
Q23 (conceptual)
What is internal resistance of a cell?
Internal resistance is the opposition to current within the battery itself. It causes a voltage drop inside the battery when current flows.
Q24 (conceptual)
What happens to the brightness of bulbs connected in series if one bulb blows?
In series, if one bulb blows (open circuit), the circuit breaks and all bulbs go off.
Q25 (conceptual)
What happens to the brightness of bulbs connected in parallel if one bulb blows?
In parallel, each bulb has its own path. If one blows, the others remain on with the same brightness (assuming ideal supply).
Q26 (conceptual)
Why is AC used for power transmission instead of DC?
AC voltage can be easily stepped up or down using transformers. High voltage reduces current, thus reducing power loss (I²R) in transmission lines.
Q27 (conceptual)
What is meant by RMS value of AC? How is it related to peak value?
RMS (root‑mean‑square) value is the effective value of AC that produces the same heating effect as an equal DC. For sinusoidal AC, V_rms = V_peak / √2.
Q28 (conceptual)
What is the phase difference between voltage and current in a purely resistive AC circuit?
In a purely resistive circuit, voltage and current are in phase – phase difference is 0°.
Q29 (conceptual)
What is the phase difference in a purely inductive circuit?
In a purely inductive circuit, voltage leads current by 90° (current lags voltage).
Q30 (conceptual)
What is the phase difference in a purely capacitive circuit?
In a purely capacitive circuit, current leads voltage by 90° (voltage lags current).
Q31 (conceptual)
Define resistivity. How does it depend on temperature for conductors?
Resistivity is a material property that measures how strongly it opposes current. For conductors, resistivity increases with temperature.
Q32 (conceptual)
What is a superconductor?
A superconductor is a material that has zero electrical resistance below a certain critical temperature.
Q33 (conceptual)
What is meant by “short circuit”? Why is it dangerous?
A short circuit occurs when a low‑resistance path bypasses the normal load. Very large current flows, overheating wires and possibly causing fire.
Q34 (conceptual)
What is the function of a fuse or circuit breaker?
A fuse melts (or circuit breaker trips) when current exceeds a safe value, breaking the circuit and preventing damage/fire.
Q35 (conceptual)
Why do we connect appliances in parallel in home wiring?
Parallel connection ensures each appliance gets the same voltage (230 V) and can be switched on/off independently without affecting others.
Q36 (conceptual)
What is earthing? Why is it important?
Earthing provides a safe path for leakage current to flow to ground, preventing electric shock.
Q37 (conceptual)
Define electric energy and give its commercial unit.
Electric energy is the total work done by electricity. Commercial unit is kilowatt‑hour (kWh).
Q38 (conceptual)
What is the effect of frequency on capacitive reactance?
Capacitive reactance decreases as frequency increases (X_C ∝ 1/f).
Q39 (conceptual)
What is the effect of frequency on inductive reactance?
Inductive reactance increases as frequency increases (X_L ∝ f).
Q40 (conceptual)
What is meant by the term “phase angle” in AC circuits?
Phase angle φ is the angle by which the voltage leads or lags the current. It depends on the circuit components.
Q41 (calculation)
A 12 V battery is connected to a 4 Ω resistor. Find the current flowing.
Using Ohm’s law: I = V / R = 12 / 4 = 3 amperes.
📘 REMEMBER: I = V / R.
Q42 (calculation)
A current of 2 A flows through a 6 Ω resistor. Calculate the voltage across it.
V = I × R = 2 × 6 = 12 volts.
Q43 (calculation)
A 9 V battery causes 1.5 A in a circuit. Find the resistance.
R = V / I = 9 / 1.5 = 6 ohms.
Q44 (calculation)
Calculate the total resistance of three resistors 5 Ω, 10 Ω, and 15 Ω connected in series.
In series, R_total = 5 + 10 + 15 = 30 ohms.
Q45 (calculation)
Two resistors 6 Ω and 12 Ω are connected in parallel. Find the equivalent resistance.
1/R_total = 1/6 + 1/12 = 2/12 + 1/12 = 3/12 = 1/4, so R_total = 4 ohms.
Q46 (calculation)
A 24 V supply is connected to a series combination of 4 Ω and 8 Ω. Find the current and voltage across each resistor.
Total R = 4+8=12 Ω. I = V/R = 24/12 = 2 A. Voltage across 4 Ω: V₁ = 2×4 = 8 V; across 8 Ω: V₂ = 2×8 = 16 V. Check: 8+16=24 V.
Q47 (calculation)
In a parallel circuit, a 12 V battery is connected to two resistors: 3 Ω and 6 Ω. Find total current.
R_parallel: 1/R = 1/3+1/6 = 2/6+1/6=3/6=1/2 → R=2 Ω. I_total = V/R = 12/2 = 6 A. Or I₁ = 12/3 = 4 A, I₂ = 12/6 = 2 A, total 6 A.
Q48 (calculation)
A 100 W bulb operates at 220 V. Find the current it draws and its resistance.
P = V I → I = P/V = 100/220 ≈ 0.455 A. R = V/I = 220/0.455 ≈ 484 Ω. (Or R = V²/P = 220²/100 = 484 Ω.)
Q49 (calculation)
How much energy is consumed by a 1500 W heater running for 3 hours? Express in kWh.
Energy (kWh) = power (kW) × time (h) = 1.5 kW × 3 h = 4.5 kWh.
Q50 (calculation)
A 12 V battery has an internal resistance of 0.5 Ω. If it supplies 2 A, find the terminal voltage.
Internal voltage drop = I × r = 2 × 0.5 = 1 V. Terminal voltage = emf – drop = 12 – 1 = 11 V.
Q51 (calculation)
A 200 V AC supply has a peak voltage of about 283 V. Find the RMS voltage (given peak).
V_rms = V_peak / √2 ≈ 283 / 1.414 ≈ 200 V (matches).
Q52 (calculation)
A capacitor of 50 μF is connected to a 50 Hz AC supply. Calculate its capacitive reactance. (Use π ≈ 3.14)
X_C = 1 / (2πf C) = 1 / (2 × 3.14 × 50 × 50×10⁻⁶) = 1 / (2×3.14×50×5×10⁻⁵) = 1 / (0.0157) ≈ 63.7 Ω.
Q53 (calculation)
An inductor of 0.1 H is connected to a 60 Hz AC source. Find inductive reactance.
X_L = 2πf L = 2×3.14×60×0.1 = 37.68 Ω.
Q54 (calculation)
A series RLC circuit has R = 10 Ω, X_L = 20 Ω, X_C = 8 Ω. Find impedance and phase angle.
X_net = X_L – X_C = 12 Ω. Z = √(R² + X_net²) = √(100 + 144) = √244 ≈ 15.62 Ω. tan φ = X_net / R = 12/10 = 1.2 → φ ≈ 50.2° (voltage leads current).
Q55 (calculation)
A transformer has 200 primary turns and 50 secondary turns. If primary voltage is 230 V, find secondary voltage.
V_s = V_p × (N_s/N_p) = 230 × (50/200) = 230 × 0.25 = 57.5 V.
Q56 (calculation)
In the above transformer, if secondary current is 4 A, find primary current (ideal).
I_p = I_s × (N_s/N_p) = 4 × (50/200) = 4 × 0.25 = 1 A. (Or using power: V_p I_p = V_s I_s → 230 × I_p = 57.5 × 4 → I_p = 230/230 = 1 A.)
Q57 (calculation)
A 240 V, 60 W lamp is used on 220 V supply. Find the current drawn and power consumed. (Assume resistance constant.)
Resistance R = V_rated² / P_rated = 240²/60 = 57600/60 = 960 Ω. Current at 220 V: I = 220/960 ≈ 0.229 A. Power = I²R = (0.229)² × 960 ≈ 50.4 W.
Q58 (calculation)
Three 6 Ω resistors are connected in parallel. Find equivalent resistance.
1/R = 1/6 + 1/6 + 1/6 = 3/6 = 1/2 → R = 2 Ω.
Q59 (calculation)
A 12 Ω and a 24 Ω resistor are in parallel, and this combination is in series with a 6 Ω resistor. Find total resistance.
Parallel part: 1/Rp = 1/12 + 1/24 = 2/24+1/24=3/24=1/8 → Rp = 8 Ω. Total = 8 + 6 = 14 Ω.
Q60 (calculation)
A 220 V, 50 Hz AC supply is connected to a 20 μF capacitor. Find the capacitive reactance and RMS current.
X_C = 1/(2π×50×20×10⁻⁶) = 1/(2×3.14×50×2×10⁻⁵) = 1/(0.00628) ≈ 159.2 Ω. I_rms = V_rms / X_C = 220 / 159.2 ≈ 1.38 A.
Q61 (calculation)
A coil of inductance 0.2 H and resistance 5 Ω is connected to 100 V, 50 Hz supply. Find impedance and current.
X_L = 2π×50×0.2 = 62.8 Ω. Z = √(5² + 62.8²) ≈ √(25 + 3944) = √3969 = 63 Ω (approx). I = V/Z = 100/63 ≈ 1.59 A.
Q62 (calculation)
In a series RLC circuit, R = 15 Ω, L = 0.1 H, C = 100 μF, f = 50 Hz. Find impedance.
X_L = 2π×50×0.1 = 31.4 Ω. X_C = 1/(2π×50×100×10⁻⁶) = 1/(0.0314) ≈ 31.85 Ω. X_net = X_L – X_C ≈ –0.45 Ω. Z = √(15² + 0.45²) ≈ √(225 + 0.2) = √225.2 ≈ 15.01 Ω (almost resistive).
Q63 (calculation)
A 230 V, 100 W bulb is connected to a 230 V supply. Find its resistance and current.
R = V²/P = 230²/100 = 52900/100 = 529 Ω. I = P/V = 100/230 ≈ 0.435 A.
Q64 (calculation)
Calculate the power dissipated in a 10 Ω resistor carrying 2 A.
P = I² R = 2² × 10 = 4 × 10 = 40 W.
Q65 (calculation)
A 220 V heater draws 5 A. Find its power and energy consumed in 2 hours in kWh.
P = V I = 220 × 5 = 1100 W = 1.1 kW. Energy = P × t = 1.1 × 2 = 2.2 kWh.
Q66 (calculation)
A cell of emf 1.5 V and internal resistance 0.3 Ω is connected to a 2.7 Ω external resistor. Find current and terminal voltage.
Total R = 0.3 + 2.7 = 3 Ω. I = emf / total R = 1.5 / 3 = 0.5 A. Terminal voltage = I × external R = 0.5 × 2.7 = 1.35 V.
Q67 (calculation)
A toaster is rated 800 W, 230 V. Find its resistance and current.
R = V²/P = 230²/800 = 52900/800 = 66.125 Ω. I = P/V = 800/230 ≈ 3.48 A.
Q68 (calculation)
An AC circuit has a voltage v = 311 sin(314t). Find the frequency and RMS voltage.
ω = 314 rad/s = 2πf → f = 314/(2×3.14) = 50 Hz. V_peak = 311 V, so V_rms = 311/√2 ≈ 220 V.
Q69 (calculation)
A transformer has 500 primary turns and 1000 secondary turns. If primary voltage is 110 V, find secondary voltage.
V_s = V_p × (N_s/N_p) = 110 × (1000/500) = 110 × 2 = 220 V.
Q70 (calculation)
A 20 Ω resistor and a 40 Ω resistor are in parallel. The combination is connected to a 12 V battery. Find total current.
Parallel R: 1/R = 1/20 + 1/40 = 2/40+1/40=3/40 → R = 40/3 ≈ 13.33 Ω. I = V/R = 12 / (40/3) = 12 × 3/40 = 36/40 = 0.9 A.
Q71 (calculation)
An AC circuit has an impedance of 50 Ω and resistance 30 Ω. Find the reactance and power factor.
Z² = R² + X² → X = √(Z² – R²) = √(2500–900) = √1600 = 40 Ω. Power factor cos φ = R/Z = 30/50 = 0.6.
Q72 (calculation)
A 10 μF capacitor is connected to a 200 V, 50 Hz supply. Find the current (RMS).
X_C = 1/(2π×50×10×10⁻⁶) = 1/(2×3.14×50×10⁻⁵) = 1/(0.0314) ≈ 31.85 Ω. I = V / X_C = 200 / 31.85 ≈ 6.28 A.
Q73 (calculation)
A 0.5 H inductor is connected to a 100 V, 60 Hz supply. Find the reactance and current.
X_L = 2π×60×0.5 = 188.4 Ω. I = 100 / 188.4 ≈ 0.531 A.
Q74 (calculation)
A series RL circuit has R = 5 Ω, L = 0.02 H, f = 50 Hz, V = 230 V. Find impedance and current.
X_L = 2π×50×0.02 = 6.28 Ω. Z = √(5² + 6.28²) = √(25 + 39.4) = √64.4 ≈ 8.03 Ω. I = 230 / 8.03 ≈ 28.6 A.
Q75 (calculation)
A 40 Ω resistor, a 30 Ω inductive reactance, and a 60 Ω capacitive reactance are in series across 200 V. Find current and power factor.
X_net = 30 – 60 = –30 Ω (capacitive dominant). Z = √(40² + 30²) = √(1600+900)=√2500=50 Ω. I = 200/50 = 4 A. cos φ = R/Z = 40/50 = 0.8 leading (since X_net negative).
Q76 (calculation)
A 220 V, 50 Hz AC source is connected to a 10 Ω resistor in series with a 0.05 H inductor. Find the phase angle and power consumed.
X_L = 2π×50×0.05 = 15.7 Ω. Z = √(10²+15.7²) = √(100+246.5) = √346.5 ≈ 18.62 Ω. I = 220/18.62 ≈ 11.82 A. cos φ = 10/18.62 = 0.537. Power P = I² R = (11.82)² ×10 = 139.7×10 = 1397 W. Or P = V I cos φ = 220×11.82×0.537 ≈ 1397 W.
Q77 (calculation)
A transformer has 400 primary turns and 1200 secondary turns. The primary current is 5 A. Find secondary current (ideal).
I_s = I_p × (N_p/N_s) = 5 × (400/1200) = 5 × 1/3 = 1.67 A.
Q78 (calculation)
A 12 V battery with internal resistance 0.2 Ω is short‑circuited. Find the short‑circuit current.
I_sc = emf / internal resistance = 12 / 0.2 = 60 A.
Q79 (calculation)
Three resistors 10 Ω, 20 Ω, and 30 Ω are connected in parallel to a 12 V battery. Find total current.
1/R = 1/10+1/20+1/30 = 6/60+3/60+2/60 = 11/60 → R = 60/11 ≈ 5.455 Ω. I = 12 / 5.455 ≈ 2.2 A. Or I₁=1.2, I₂=0.6, I₃=0.4, sum=2.2 A.
Q80 (calculation)
An AC circuit has a current i = 14.14 sin(314t). Find the RMS current and frequency.
I_peak = 14.14 A → I_rms = 14.14/√2 ≈ 10 A. ω = 314 rad/s → f = 314/(2π) = 50 Hz.
