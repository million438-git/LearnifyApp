7.1 Electromagnetic waves

Electromagnetic (EM) waves are transverse waves consisting of oscillating electric and magnetic fields perpendicular to each other and to the direction of propagation. They travel at speed c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s in vacuum.

Properties: no medium needed, carry energy and momentum, can be polarized, obey the wave equation.

📘 REMEMBER: c = f λ (speed = frequency × wavelength).
🌟 WOW: All light, radio, X‑rays, and gamma rays are EM waves – they differ only in frequency/wavelength.

7.2 Electromagnetic spectrum

The EM spectrum spans from low‑frequency radio waves to high‑frequency gamma rays. In order: radio, microwaves, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X‑rays, gamma rays.

Visible light is a tiny part: violet (≈400 nm) to red (≈700 nm).

📘 REMEMBER: Higher frequency means shorter wavelength and higher photon energy.
🌈 (Diagram: EM spectrum chart)

7.3 Photoelectric effect

When light of sufficient frequency shines on a metal, electrons are emitted. Einstein explained it using photons – light consists of energy packets E = h f (h = Planck constant ≈ 6.63×10⁻³⁴ J·s).

Key equation: K.E.max = h f – φ, where φ is the work function (minimum energy to eject an electron).

Threshold frequency f₀ = φ / h. Below f₀, no electrons are emitted regardless of intensity.

⚡ WOW: This proved the particle nature of light and won Einstein the Nobel Prize.
📸 (Diagram: photoelectric setup)

7.4 Atomic spectra & Bohr model

Atoms have discrete energy levels. When an electron jumps from higher to lower energy, it emits a photon with energy ΔE = h f = E₂ – E₁. This produces line spectra unique to each element.

For hydrogen: 1/λ = R (1/n₂² – 1/n₁²) (Rydberg formula).

📘 REMEMBER: Absorption lines are dark lines; emission lines are bright.

7.5 Wave‑particle duality & de Broglie wavelength

Matter also exhibits wave nature. de Broglie proposed λ = h / p, where p = mv (momentum).

Electrons show diffraction patterns, confirming wave nature.

🌊 WOW: Everything has a wavelength, but it's noticeable only for tiny particles like electrons.

7.6 Nuclear radiation

Radioactive decay: unstable nuclei emit α (helium nucleus), β (electron/positron), or γ (high‑energy photon).

Half‑life T½: time for half the nuclei to decay. N = N₀ (½)t/T½.

Activity A = λ N, λ = decay constant = ln2 / T½.

📘 REMEMBER: α particles are heavy and easily stopped, β are faster, γ are very penetrating.

7.7 Mass‑energy equivalence & nuclear reactions

Einstein’s famous E = m c² (in words: energy equals mass times speed of light squared). In nuclear reactions, mass defect is converted to energy.

Fission: heavy nucleus splits; Fusion: light nuclei combine. Both release huge energy.

☀️ WOW: The Sun’s energy comes from nuclear fusion of hydrogen into helium.

📋 70 exam‑style questions

Q1 (conceptual)
What are electromagnetic waves? How do they differ from mechanical waves?
EM waves are transverse waves with oscillating electric and magnetic fields. They do not require a medium; mechanical waves need a medium. EM waves travel at speed c in vacuum.
📘 REMEMBER: Light is an EM wave.
Q2 (conceptual)
Write the relation between frequency, wavelength, and speed for EM waves.
c = f × λ (speed = frequency times wavelength).
Q3 (conceptual)
Arrange the following in order of increasing wavelength: X‑rays, radio, visible, infrared.
Increasing wavelength: X‑rays → visible → infrared → radio.
🌟 WOW: Radio waves can be metres long, while X‑rays are fractions of a nanometre.
Q4 (conceptual)
What is the photoelectric effect? State Einstein’s photon explanation.
Photoelectric effect: emission of electrons from a metal when light shines on it. Einstein proposed that light consists of photons, each with energy E = h f. An electron is emitted if photon energy exceeds the work function φ.
Q5 (conceptual)
Define work function and threshold frequency.
Work function φ is the minimum energy needed to eject an electron from a metal surface. Threshold frequency f₀ = φ / h; below this, no electrons are emitted.
Q6 (conceptual)
What is the de Broglie hypothesis?
de Broglie proposed that particles have a wavelength given by λ = h / p, where p is momentum. This wave nature is observed for electrons (diffraction).
Q7 (conceptual)
Explain the line emission spectrum of hydrogen.
Electrons in hydrogen atoms occupy discrete energy levels. When an electron drops from a higher level to a lower one, it emits a photon of energy equal to the difference. This produces spectral lines at specific wavelengths.
Q8 (conceptual)
State Bohr’s postulates for hydrogen atom.
1. Electrons orbit nucleus in fixed circular orbits without radiating. 2. Angular momentum is quantised: mvr = n h/2π. 3. Energy is emitted/absorbed when electron jumps between orbits: ΔE = h f.
Q9 (conceptual)
What are the three types of radioactive decay? Describe each briefly.
Alpha (α): emission of helium nucleus (2 protons, 2 neutrons). Beta (β): emission of electron or positron. Gamma (γ): emission of high‑energy photon.
Q10 (conceptual)
Define half‑life. Write the decay formula.
Half‑life T½ is the time for half the radioactive nuclei to decay. N = N₀ × (½)^(t / T½).
Q11 (conceptual)
What is mass defect? How is it related to nuclear energy?
Mass defect is the difference between the mass of a nucleus and the sum of its constituent nucleons. This mass is converted to energy according to E = m c², released in nuclear reactions.
Q12 (conceptual)
Distinguish between nuclear fission and fusion.
Fission: splitting of a heavy nucleus (e.g., uranium) into lighter ones, releasing energy. Fusion: combining light nuclei (e.g., hydrogen) into heavier ones, also releasing energy.
Q13 (conceptual)
Why are gamma rays more penetrating than alpha particles?
Gamma rays have no mass and high energy, so they interact weakly with matter. Alpha particles are heavy and charged, losing energy quickly via ionisation.
Q14 (conceptual)
What is the photoelectric threshold? If light of lower frequency is used, what happens?
Threshold is the minimum frequency for photoelectric emission. Below threshold, no electrons are emitted, regardless of intensity.
Q15 (conceptual)
Explain the term ‘quantisation of energy’.
Energy is not continuous but exists in discrete packets (quanta). For example, atomic energy levels and photon energies are quantised.
Q16 (conceptual)
What is the Compton effect? What does it prove?
Compton effect: X‑rays scattered by electrons lose energy (wavelength increases), showing that photons have momentum and particle nature.
Q17 (conceptual)
Write Einstein’s photoelectric equation and explain each term.
K.E.max = h f – φ, where K.E.max = maximum kinetic energy of emitted electron, h = Planck constant, f = frequency, φ = work function.
Q18 (conceptual)
What is the significance of Planck’s constant?
Planck’s constant (h) relates energy to frequency: E = h f. It sets the scale of quantum phenomena.
Q19 (conceptual)
Define electron volt (eV).
One electron volt is the kinetic energy gained by an electron accelerated through 1 volt: 1 eV = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ J.
Q20 (conceptual)
What is an atomic energy level? How does an atom emit light?
Energy levels are fixed energies that electrons can occupy. When an electron jumps from higher to lower level, it emits a photon with energy equal to the difference.
Q21 (conceptual)
What is the difference between emission and absorption spectra?
Emission spectra: bright lines on dark background (light emitted at specific wavelengths). Absorption spectra: dark lines on continuous spectrum (light absorbed at specific wavelengths).
Q22 (conceptual)
Explain why only certain frequencies are present in hydrogen emission spectrum.
Electrons can only occupy discrete energy levels. Transitions between these levels produce photons with energies equal to the difference, hence only specific frequencies.
Q23 (conceptual)
What is radioactivity? Give an example.
Radioactivity is the spontaneous emission of particles or radiation from unstable nuclei. Example: uranium‑238 decays to thorium‑234 by emitting alpha particles.
Q24 (conceptual)
What is the decay constant? Relate it to half‑life.
Decay constant λ is the probability per unit time for a nucleus to decay. Relation: T½ = ln2 / λ (half‑life = natural log of 2 divided by decay constant).
Q25 (conceptual)
What is meant by ‘binding energy per nucleon’?
Binding energy per nucleon is the total binding energy of a nucleus divided by the number of nucleons. It measures stability; higher means more stable.
Q26 (conceptual)
How does a nuclear reactor produce energy?
Controlled fission of uranium‑235 produces heat, which is used to make steam, driving turbines to generate electricity.
Q27 (conceptual)
What is a photon? State its properties.
A photon is a quantum of electromagnetic energy. It has zero rest mass, travels at c, carries energy E = h f and momentum p = h/λ.
Q28 (conceptual)
What is the ultraviolet catastrophe?
Classical physics predicted that blackbody radiation intensity would go to infinity at high frequencies – an absurd result. Planck’s quantum hypothesis resolved it.
Q29 (conceptual)
What is the difference between coherent and incoherent light?
Coherent light: waves have constant phase difference (lasers). Incoherent: random phase (ordinary light bulbs).
Q30 (conceptual)
What is stimulated emission? How does a laser work?
Stimulated emission: incoming photon triggers an excited atom to emit a second identical photon. Lasers use this to produce intense, coherent light.
Q31 (conceptual)
Define 1 amu (atomic mass unit) in kg.
1 amu = 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ kg. It is 1/12 the mass of a carbon‑12 atom.
Q32 (conceptual)
What is meant by ‘critical mass’ in nuclear fission?
Critical mass is the minimum mass of fissile material needed to sustain a chain reaction.
Q33 (conceptual)
What is a positron? Give its properties.
Positron is the antiparticle of electron; same mass but positive charge. Emitted in β⁺ decay.
Q34 (conceptual)
What is meant by ‘pair production’? What condition is necessary?
Pair production: a high‑energy photon converts into an electron and a positron near a nucleus. Requires photon energy > 2mₑc² = 1.02 MeV.
Q35 (conceptual)
What is the Heisenberg uncertainty principle?
It states that certain pairs of properties (e.g., position and momentum) cannot both be known precisely: Δx·Δp ≥ h/4π.
Q36 (calculation)
Calculate the wavelength of an EM wave with frequency 5×10¹⁴ Hz (use c = 3×10⁸ m/s).
λ = c / f = (3×10⁸) / (5×10¹⁴) = 6×10⁻⁷ m = 600 nm (visible light).
📘 REMEMBER: c = f λ.
Q37 (calculation)
A photon has energy 3.3×10⁻¹⁹ J. Find its frequency (h = 6.63×10⁻³⁴ J·s).
f = E / h = 3.3×10⁻¹⁹ / 6.63×10⁻³⁴ ≈ 4.98×10¹⁴ Hz.
Q38 (calculation)
The work function of a metal is 2.0 eV. What is its threshold frequency? (1 eV = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ J, h = 6.63×10⁻³⁴ J·s)
φ = 2.0 × 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ = 3.2×10⁻¹⁹ J. f₀ = φ / h = 3.2×10⁻¹⁹ / 6.63×10⁻³⁴ ≈ 4.83×10¹⁴ Hz.
Q39 (calculation)
Light of frequency 6.5×10¹⁴ Hz shines on a metal with work function 2.1 eV. Find maximum kinetic energy of photoelectrons (in J and eV).
E_photon = h f = 6.63×10⁻³⁴ × 6.5×10¹⁴ = 4.31×10⁻¹⁹ J. φ = 2.1 × 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ = 3.36×10⁻¹⁹ J. K.E.max = 4.31×10⁻¹⁹ – 3.36×10⁻¹⁹ = 0.95×10⁻¹⁹ J = 0.59 eV (since 1 eV = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹).
Q40 (calculation)
A photon has wavelength 400 nm. Find its energy in eV. (h = 6.63×10⁻³⁴ J·s, c = 3×10⁸ m/s, 1 eV = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ J)
E = h c / λ = (6.63×10⁻³⁴ × 3×10⁸) / (400×10⁻⁹) = (1.989×10⁻²⁵) / (4×10⁻⁷) = 4.97×10⁻¹⁹ J. In eV: 4.97×10⁻¹⁹ / 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ = 3.11 eV.
Q41 (calculation)
Calculate the de Broglie wavelength of an electron moving at 2×10⁶ m/s. (m_e = 9.11×10⁻³¹ kg, h = 6.63×10⁻³⁴ J·s)
p = m v = 9.11×10⁻³¹ × 2×10⁶ = 1.822×10⁻²⁴ kg·m/s. λ = h / p = 6.63×10⁻³⁴ / 1.822×10⁻²⁴ = 3.64×10⁻¹⁰ m = 0.364 nm.
Q42 (calculation)
Find the momentum of a photon of wavelength 500 nm.
p = h / λ = 6.63×10⁻³⁴ / (500×10⁻⁹) = 1.326×10⁻²⁷ kg·m/s.
Q43 (calculation)
Calculate the energy difference between the n=2 and n=1 levels in hydrogen atom (given: E_n = –13.6/n² eV).
E₁ = –13.6 eV, E₂ = –13.6/4 = –3.4 eV. ΔE = E₂ – E₁ = (–3.4) – (–13.6) = 10.2 eV. This corresponds to a photon of energy 10.2 eV.
Q44 (calculation)
What wavelength is emitted when hydrogen electron jumps from n=3 to n=2? (R = 1.097×10⁷ m⁻¹)
1/λ = R (1/2² – 1/3²) = 1.097×10⁷ (1/4 – 1/9) = 1.097×10⁷ (0.25 – 0.1111) = 1.097×10⁷ × 0.1389 = 1.524×10⁶ m⁻¹. λ = 1 / (1.524×10⁶) = 6.56×10⁻⁷ m = 656 nm (red).
Q45 (calculation)
A radioactive sample has half‑life 10 days. If you start with 80 g, how much remains after 30 days?
30 days = 3 half‑lives. N = N₀ × (½)³ = 80 × 1/8 = 10 g.
Q46 (calculation)
The half‑life of a substance is 4 hours. What fraction remains after 12 hours?
12 hours = 3 half‑lives. Fraction remaining = (½)³ = 1/8.
Q47 (calculation)
A sample has decay constant λ = 0.05 per year. Find its half‑life (in years).
T½ = ln2 / λ = 0.693 / 0.05 = 13.86 years.
Q48 (calculation)
How much energy is released if 1 g of mass is completely converted to energy? (c = 3×10⁸ m/s)
E = m c² = 0.001 kg × (3×10⁸)² = 0.001 × 9×10¹⁶ = 9×10¹³ J. That’s about 25 million kWh!
Q49 (calculation)
A nuclear reaction has mass defect of 0.1 u. Find energy released (1 u = 1.66×10⁻²⁷ kg, c = 3×10⁸ m/s, 1 eV = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ J).
Mass defect in kg = 0.1 × 1.66×10⁻²⁷ = 1.66×10⁻²⁸ kg. E = m c² = 1.66×10⁻²⁸ × 9×10¹⁶ = 1.494×10⁻¹¹ J. Convert to eV: divide by 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ → 93.4 MeV.
Q50 (calculation)
Calculate the binding energy per nucleon for helium‑4 (mass = 4.002603 u, proton mass = 1.007825 u, neutron mass = 1.008665 u).
Helium‑4 has 2 protons + 2 neutrons. Mass of constituents = 2×1.007825 + 2×1.008665 = 2.01565 + 2.01733 = 4.03298 u. Mass defect = 4.03298 – 4.002603 = 0.030377 u. Binding energy = 0.030377 × 931.5 MeV ≈ 28.3 MeV. Per nucleon = 28.3 / 4 ≈ 7.07 MeV.
Q51 (calculation)
A metal has threshold frequency 5×10¹⁴ Hz. What is the work function in eV? (h = 6.63×10⁻³⁴ J·s)
φ = h f₀ = 6.63×10⁻³⁴ × 5×10¹⁴ = 3.315×10⁻¹⁹ J. Convert to eV: divide by 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ = 2.07 eV.
Q52 (calculation)
Light of wavelength 200 nm shines on a metal with work function 4.0 eV. Will photoelectrons be emitted? If yes, find their K.E. in eV.
Photon energy E = h c / λ = (6.63×10⁻³⁴ × 3×10⁸)/(200×10⁻⁹) = 9.945×10⁻¹⁹ J = 9.945×10⁻¹⁹ / 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ = 6.22 eV. Since 6.22 eV > 4.0 eV, electrons are emitted. K.E. = 6.22 – 4.0 = 2.22 eV.
Q53 (calculation)
What is the de Broglie wavelength of a 0.1 kg ball moving at 10 m/s?
λ = h / (m v) = 6.63×10⁻³⁴ / (0.1 × 10) = 6.63×10⁻³⁴ / 1 = 6.63×10⁻³⁴ m. Extremely small – wave nature not observable for macroscopic objects.
Q54 (calculation)
A radioactive source has activity 2000 Bq. After 6 hours, activity becomes 500 Bq. Find half‑life.
Activity dropped from 2000 to 500 in 6 hours, that’s a factor of ¼ = (½)², so 6 hours = 2 half‑lives. Half‑life = 3 hours.
Q55 (calculation)
The half‑life of a substance is 5 years. How long will it take for 90% to decay?
If 90% decayed, 10% remains. N/N₀ = 0.1 = (½)^n → n = log(0.1)/log(0.5) ≈ 3.32 half‑lives. Time = 3.32 × 5 = 16.6 years.
Q56 (calculation)
A photon has frequency 10¹⁸ Hz. Find its energy in keV. (h = 4.14×10⁻¹⁵ eV·s)
E = h f = (4.14×10⁻¹⁵ eV·s) × 10¹⁸ Hz = 4.14×10³ eV = 4.14 keV.
Q57 (calculation)
An electron in hydrogen falls from n=4 to n=2. Find the wavelength of emitted photon (R = 1.097×10⁷ m⁻¹).
1/λ = R (1/2² – 1/4²) = 1.097×10⁷ (1/4 – 1/16) = 1.097×10⁷ (0.25 – 0.0625) = 1.097×10⁷ × 0.1875 = 2.057×10⁶ m⁻¹. λ = 1 / (2.057×10⁶) = 4.86×10⁻⁷ m = 486 nm.
Q58 (calculation)
Calculate the energy (in J) of a photon with wavelength 0.1 nm (X‑ray). h = 6.63×10⁻³⁴ J·s, c = 3×10⁸ m/s.
E = h c / λ = (6.63×10⁻³⁴ × 3×10⁸) / (0.1×10⁻⁹) = 1.989×10⁻²⁵ / 1×10⁻¹⁰ = 1.989×10⁻¹⁵ J. In eV: 1.989×10⁻¹⁵ / 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ = 12430 eV = 12.43 keV.
Q59 (calculation)
What is the momentum of a photon with energy 10 keV? (Use h = 6.63×10⁻³⁴ J·s, c = 3×10⁸ m/s, 1 eV = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ J)
E = 10 keV = 10×10³ × 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ = 1.6×10⁻¹⁵ J. p = E / c = 1.6×10⁻¹⁵ / 3×10⁸ = 5.33×10⁻²⁴ kg·m/s.
Q60 (calculation)
A radioactive sample has decay constant 0.0231 per year. What is its half‑life?
T½ = ln2 / λ = 0.693 / 0.0231 = 30 years.
Q61 (calculation)
A 1 mg sample of pure carbon‑14 has half‑life 5730 years. Find its initial activity (decays per second). (N₀ = mass / atomic mass × Avogadro number; 1 u = 1.66×10⁻²⁷ kg; Avogadro = 6.02×10²³)
Atomic mass of C‑14 ≈ 14 g/mol = 0.014 kg/mol. Number of atoms N₀ = (1×10⁻⁶ kg / 0.014 kg/mol) × 6.02×10²³ = (7.14×10⁻⁵) × 6.02×10²³ = 4.3×10¹⁹ atoms. λ = ln2 / T½ = 0.693 / (5730×365×24×3600) = 0.693 / (1.807×10¹¹) = 3.83×10⁻¹² s⁻¹. Activity A = λ N₀ = 3.83×10⁻¹² × 4.3×10¹⁹ = 1.65×10⁸ Bq.
Q62 (calculation)
A metal surface has work function 2.5 eV. What is the maximum wavelength that can cause photoelectric emission?
Maximum wavelength corresponds to threshold: λ₀ = h c / φ. φ = 2.5 × 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ = 4.0×10⁻¹⁹ J. λ₀ = (6.63×10⁻³⁴ × 3×10⁸) / 4.0×10⁻¹⁹ = 1.989×10⁻²⁵ / 4.0×10⁻¹⁹ = 4.97×10⁻⁷ m = 497 nm (visible blue‑green).
Q63 (calculation)
A 100 W sodium lamp emits light of wavelength 589 nm. How many photons are emitted per second? (h = 6.63×10⁻³⁴ J·s)
Energy per photon = h c / λ = (6.63×10⁻³⁴ × 3×10⁸)/(589×10⁻⁹) = 3.38×10⁻¹⁹ J. Number of photons per second = power / energy per photon = 100 / 3.38×10⁻¹⁹ = 2.96×10²⁰ photons/s.
Q64 (calculation)
Find the de Broglie wavelength of a neutron (mass = 1.675×10⁻²⁷ kg) moving with kinetic energy 0.025 eV (thermal neutron). (1 eV = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ J, h = 6.63×10⁻³⁴ J·s)
K.E. = 0.025 eV = 0.025 × 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ = 4×10⁻²¹ J. v = √(2K.E./m) = √(2×4×10⁻²¹ / 1.675×10⁻²⁷) = √(8×10⁻²¹/1.675×10⁻²⁷) = √(4.78×10⁶) = 2186 m/s. p = m v = 1.675×10⁻²⁷ × 2186 = 3.66×10⁻²⁴ kg·m/s. λ = h / p = 6.63×10⁻³⁴ / 3.66×10⁻²⁴ = 1.81×10⁻¹⁰ m = 0.181 nm.
Q65 (calculation)
Calculate the shortest wavelength of Lyman series for hydrogen (R = 1.097×10⁷ m⁻¹).
Lyman series corresponds to n₁ = 1. Shortest wavelength (highest energy) when n₂ → ∞: 1/λ = R (1/1² – 1/∞²) = R. λ = 1/R = 1 / 1.097×10⁷ = 9.11×10⁻⁸ m = 91.1 nm.
Q66 (calculation)
A radioactive sample has an initial activity of 5000 Bq and half‑life 2 hours. What is the activity after 6 hours?
6 hours = 3 half‑lives. Activity = 5000 × (½)³ = 5000 × 1/8 = 625 Bq.
Q67 (calculation)
A photon has wavelength 0.02 nm. Find its energy in MeV. (h c = 1240 eV·nm approx)
E (eV) = 1240 / λ(nm). λ = 0.02 nm = 0.02. E = 1240 / 0.02 = 62000 eV = 62 keV = 0.062 MeV.
Q68 (calculation)
The rest mass of an electron is 9.11×10⁻³¹ kg. Find its rest energy in MeV.
E₀ = mₑ c² = 9.11×10⁻³¹ × (3×10⁸)² = 9.11×10⁻³¹ × 9×10¹⁶ = 8.199×10⁻¹⁴ J. Convert to eV: 8.199×10⁻¹⁴ / 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ = 5.12×10⁵ eV = 0.512 MeV.
Q69 (calculation)
How much energy is released when 1 kg of uranium‑235 undergoes fission, assuming 200 MeV per fission? (Avogadro’s number = 6.02×10²³, molar mass of U‑235 = 235 g/mol)
Number of atoms in 1 kg = (1000 g / 235 g/mol) × 6.02×10²³ = 4.255 × 2.56×10²⁴? Actually 1000/235 = 4.255 mol. Atoms = 4.255 × 6.02×10²³ = 2.56×10²⁴ atoms. Energy per fission = 200 MeV = 200×10⁶ × 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ J = 3.2×10⁻¹¹ J. Total energy = 2.56×10²⁴ × 3.2×10⁻¹¹ = 8.19×10¹³ J. That’s about 8.2×10¹³ J, equivalent to 20 kilotons of TNT.
Q70 (calculation)
Calculate the wavelength of an electron accelerated through 100 V. (Use λ = h/√(2 m e V) or λ = 12.27/√V Å)
Using de Broglie: λ = h / p, p = √(2 m e V). m = 9.11×10⁻³¹ kg, e = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C, V = 100 V. p = √(2 × 9.11×10⁻³¹ × 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ × 100) = √(2.915×10⁻⁴⁷) = 5.4×10⁻²⁴ kg·m/s. λ = 6.63×10⁻³⁴ / 5.4×10⁻²⁴ = 1.23×10⁻¹⁰ m = 0.123 nm. Or using shortcut: λ (Å) = 12.27/√V = 12.27/10 = 1.227 Å = 0.1227 nm.
