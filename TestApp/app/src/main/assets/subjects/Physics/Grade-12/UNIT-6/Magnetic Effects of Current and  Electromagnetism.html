6.1 Magnetic field around a current‑carrying conductor

An electric current produces a magnetic field. The field lines are concentric circles around the wire. The direction is given by the right‑hand grip rule: thumb in current direction, fingers curl along field.

Strength: For a long straight wire, B = μ₀ I / (2π r) (in words: magnetic field strength = permeability × current ÷ (2π × distance)).

📘 REMEMBER: Field strength increases with current and decreases as you move away from the wire.
💡 WOW: Earth’s magnetic field is about 0.00005 tesla, while a powerful electromagnet can reach 1–2 tesla.

6.2 Magnetic field of a solenoid

A solenoid is a long coil of wire. When current flows, it produces a nearly uniform magnetic field inside, and a field like that of a bar magnet outside.

Field inside: B = μ₀ n I (where n = number of turns per metre). Adding a soft iron core greatly increases B (electromagnet).

📘 REMEMBER: Inside a solenoid, field lines are parallel and equally spaced – uniform field.
🔩 WOW: Electromagnets can lift entire cars in scrapyards!
🧲 (Image: solenoid field lines)

6.3 Force on a current‑carrying conductor in a magnetic field

A wire carrying current I, of length L, placed in a magnetic field B, experiences a force: F = B I L sinθ (θ = angle between current and field). Direction: right‑hand rule (fingers = field, thumb = current, palm = force).

If current is perpendicular (θ = 90°), F = B I L.

📘 REMEMBER: This is the principle behind electric motors.

6.4 Electromagnetic induction – Faraday & Lenz

When the magnetic flux through a circuit changes, an EMF is induced. Faraday’s law: induced EMF = –N ΔΦ/Δt (magnitude N ΔΦ/Δt).

Lenz’s law: The induced current opposes the change that produced it.

Ways to change flux: move magnet, move coil, change area, change orientation.

⚡ WOW: All electricity in power stations is generated by electromagnetic induction!
🧲 (Image: induction experiment)

6.5 Motional EMF

When a conductor moves perpendicular to a magnetic field, an EMF is induced across its ends: EMF = B L v (L = length, v = speed).

This is a special case of Faraday’s law.

6.6 Electric motor

A motor converts electrical energy into mechanical energy. A current‑carrying coil in a magnetic field experiences torque, causing rotation. A commutator reverses current every half turn to keep rotation in the same direction.

Torque on a coil: τ = B I A N sinθ (A = area, N = number of turns).

📘 REMEMBER: Motors are everywhere – fans, pumps, electric vehicles.
🧲 (Image: DC motor diagram)

6.7 Electric generator

A generator converts mechanical energy into electrical energy. Rotating a coil in a magnetic field changes flux, inducing an alternating EMF. Slip rings deliver AC to external circuit.

Peak EMF: ε₀ = N B A ω (ω = angular speed).

🏭 WOW: Power plants use huge generators spinning at 3000 rpm to produce 50 Hz AC.

6.8 Transformers (basics)

A transformer transfers electrical energy between two coils via mutual induction. For an ideal transformer: Vₚ / Vₛ = Nₚ / Nₛ = Iₛ / Iₚ.

Step‑up: more turns in secondary → higher voltage. Step‑down: fewer turns in secondary → lower voltage.

📘 REMEMBER: Transformers only work with AC – they rely on changing flux.

6.9 Self‑inductance & mutual inductance

Self‑inductance L is the property of a coil to oppose change in current. EMF = –L ΔI/Δt. Mutual inductance M relates EMF in one coil to current change in another: EMF₂ = –M ΔI₁/Δt.

Inductors store energy in magnetic fields: U = ½ L I².

📋 80 exam‑style questions

Q1 (conceptual)
State the right‑hand grip rule for a straight current‑carrying conductor.
If you grasp the wire with your right hand such that the thumb points in the direction of current, your fingers curl in the direction of the magnetic field lines.
📘 REMEMBER: The field is circular around the wire.
Q2 (conceptual)
How does the magnetic field strength vary with distance from a long straight wire?
It decreases as the distance increases. For a straight wire, B is inversely proportional to distance (B ∝ 1/r).
Q3 (conceptual)
What is a solenoid? Sketch its magnetic field lines.
A solenoid is a long coil of wire. Inside, field lines are parallel and uniform; outside, they resemble those of a bar magnet.
🌟 WOW: The uniform field inside a solenoid is used in many experiments.
Q4 (conceptual)
State Faraday’s law of electromagnetic induction.
The magnitude of induced EMF is proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux through a circuit. In words: induced EMF = number of turns × rate of change of flux.
Q5 (conceptual)
State Lenz’s law. What does it imply about energy conservation?
Lenz’s law: The induced current opposes the change in magnetic flux that produced it. This ensures energy conservation – the work done against the opposing force appears as electrical energy.
Q6 (conceptual)
What is motional EMF? Give an expression.
When a conductor moves perpendicular to a magnetic field, an EMF is induced across its ends. EMF = magnetic field × length × velocity (B L v).
Q7 (conceptual)
List three ways to change magnetic flux through a coil.
1. Move a magnet toward or away from the coil. 2. Move the coil in a non‑uniform field. 3. Change the area or orientation of the coil in a constant field.
Q8 (conceptual)
Define the tesla (SI unit of magnetic field).
One tesla is the magnetic field strength that exerts a force of one newton on a one‑metre wire carrying one ampere of current perpendicular to the field.
Q9 (conceptual)
What is the principle of an electric motor?
A motor uses the force on a current‑carrying conductor in a magnetic field. The torque on a coil makes it rotate, converting electrical energy to mechanical energy.
Q10 (conceptual)
Why does a commutator reverse current in a DC motor?
To keep the torque in the same direction every half turn, so the motor continues rotating in one direction.
Q11 (conceptual)
What is the function of brushes in a DC motor?
Brushes (usually carbon) make sliding electrical contact with the commutator, allowing current to flow into the rotating coil.
Q12 (conceptual)
Explain the principle of an AC generator.
A coil rotated in a magnetic field experiences changing flux, inducing an alternating EMF. Slip rings connect the coil to an external circuit, producing AC.
Q13 (conceptual)
What is the difference between an AC generator and a DC generator (dynamo)?
AC generator uses slip rings and produces AC; DC generator uses a split‑ring commutator and produces DC (pulsating).
Q14 (conceptual)
What is a transformer? Name the two types.
A transformer transfers electrical energy between two coils via mutual induction. Step‑up increases voltage; step‑down decreases voltage.
Q15 (conceptual)
Why does a transformer not work with DC?
DC produces constant flux, so no change in flux – no induced EMF in secondary coil.
Q16 (conceptual)
State the transformer equation relating voltages and turns.
Vₚ / Vₛ = Nₚ / Nₛ, where Vₚ, Vₛ are primary and secondary voltages, and Nₚ, Nₛ are turns.
Q17 (conceptual)
What is self‑inductance? Give its SI unit.
Self‑inductance is the property of a coil to oppose any change in current through itself. Unit: henry (H).
Q18 (conceptual)
What is mutual inductance?
Mutual inductance between two coils is the ratio of induced EMF in one coil to the rate of change of current in the other.
Q19 (conceptual)
How does a soft iron core increase the inductance of a coil?
Soft iron has high permeability, which concentrates magnetic flux, increasing the inductance.
Q20 (conceptual)
What is the direction of force on a positive charge moving in a magnetic field?
The force is perpendicular to both velocity and magnetic field. Use the right‑hand rule: fingers = field, thumb = velocity, palm = force (for positive charge).
Q21 (conceptual)
Define magnetic flux. Write its SI unit.
Magnetic flux = magnetic field × area perpendicular to the field (Φ = B A cosθ). Unit: weber (Wb).
Q22 (conceptual)
What is eddy current? Give one application.
Eddy currents are loops of induced current in bulk conductors moving in magnetic fields. Application: electromagnetic braking in trains.
Q23 (conceptual)
Why is the core of a transformer laminated?
To reduce eddy currents and minimise energy loss as heat.
Q24 (conceptual)
What is the peak EMF of a generator coil rotating in a uniform field?
ε₀ = N B A ω, where N = turns, B = field, A = area, ω = angular speed.
Q25 (conceptual)
What happens to the direction of induced current when you pull a magnet out of a coil?
It reverses compared to when you push it in – it always opposes the motion (Lenz’s law).
Q26 (conceptual)
Define 1 henry.
A coil has self‑inductance of 1 henry if an EMF of 1 volt is induced when the current changes at 1 ampere per second.
Q27 (conceptual)
What is the energy stored in an inductor?
Energy = ½ L I², where L is inductance and I is current.
Q28 (conceptual)
What is the role of the earth wire in electrical appliances?
It provides a low‑resistance path for leakage current to ground, preventing electric shock.
Q29 (conceptual)
Why are high‑voltage power lines often bundled?
Bundling reduces corona discharge and increases effective radius, reducing line inductance.
Q30 (conceptual)
What is a magnetic hysteresis loop?
It is the B‑H curve showing how magnetic material responds to magnetising field; area of loop represents energy loss per cycle.
Q31 (conceptual)
What is the advantage of using AC over DC for power transmission?
AC voltage can be easily stepped up/down with transformers, allowing high‑voltage, low‑current transmission to reduce I²R loss.
Q32 (conceptual)
Define root‑mean‑square (RMS) value of AC.
The RMS value of AC is the equivalent DC that produces the same heating effect in a resistor. For sinusoidal AC, I_rms = I_peak / √2.
Q33 (conceptual)
What is the phase relationship between voltage and current in a pure inductor?
Voltage leads current by 90°.
Q34 (conceptual)
What is the phase relationship in a pure capacitor?
Current leads voltage by 90°.
Q35 (conceptual)
What is power factor? Why is it important?
Power factor = cos φ = R/Z. It indicates the fraction of apparent power that is real power. A low power factor causes higher current for the same real power, increasing losses.
Q36 (conceptual)
How can the power factor of an inductive load be improved?
By connecting a capacitor in parallel (or series) to compensate for lagging current.
Q37 (conceptual)
What is resonance in an RLC circuit?
Resonance occurs when inductive reactance equals capacitive reactance; impedance is minimum ( = R) and current is maximum.
Q38 (conceptual)
What is the function of a fuse?
A fuse melts when current exceeds a safe value, breaking the circuit and preventing damage/fire.
Q39 (conceptual)
What is meant by “magnetic shielding”?
Using high‑permeability materials to divert magnetic field lines away from sensitive components.
Q40 (conceptual)
Give two applications of electromagnets.
Electric bells, magnetic cranes, relays, MRI machines.
Q41 (calculation)
A long straight wire carries 5 A. Calculate the magnetic field at a distance 0.1 m from the wire. (μ₀ = 4π × 10⁻⁷)
B = μ₀ I / (2π r) = (4π × 10⁻⁷ × 5) / (2π × 0.1) = (2 × 10⁻⁷ × 5) / 0.1? Wait: Simplify: μ₀/(2π) = 2×10⁻⁷, so B = 2×10⁻⁷ × I / r = 2×10⁻⁷ × 5 / 0.1 = 2×10⁻⁷ × 50 = 1×10⁻⁵ T.
📘 REMEMBER: Use B = 2×10⁻⁷ × I/r (with I in A, r in m).
Q42 (calculation)
A solenoid has 500 turns per metre and carries 2 A. Find the magnetic field inside (μ₀ = 4π×10⁻⁷).
B = μ₀ n I = 4π×10⁻⁷ × 500 × 2 = 4π×10⁻⁷ × 1000 = 4π×10⁻⁴ = 0.001256 T ≈ 1.26 mT.
Q43 (calculation)
A 0.2 m wire carrying 4 A is placed at 30° to a 0.3 T field. Find the force on the wire.
F = B I L sinθ = 0.3 × 4 × 0.2 × sin30° = 0.3 × 4 × 0.2 × 0.5 = 0.3 × 4 × 0.1 = 0.12 N.
Q44 (calculation)
A wire of length 0.5 m carries 2 A perpendicular to a 0.4 T field. Find force.
F = B I L = 0.4 × 2 × 0.5 = 0.4 N.
Q45 (calculation)
A proton (q = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C) moves at 2×10⁶ m/s perpendicular to a 0.2 T field. Find the magnetic force.
F = q v B = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ × 2×10⁶ × 0.2 = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ × 4×10⁵ = 6.4×10⁻¹⁴ N.
Q46 (calculation)
A coil of 100 turns, area 0.01 m², rotates in a 0.1 T field. Find the peak EMF if it rotates at 50 rev/s.
ω = 2πf = 2π×50 = 314 rad/s. ε₀ = N B A ω = 100 × 0.1 × 0.01 × 314 = 100 × 0.001 × 314 = 0.1 × 314 = 31.4 V.
Q47 (calculation)
A coil has 200 turns, and flux changes from 0.02 Wb to 0.08 Wb in 0.1 s. Find average induced EMF.
ΔΦ = 0.08 – 0.02 = 0.06 Wb. EMF = N ΔΦ/Δt = 200 × 0.06 / 0.1 = 200 × 0.6 = 120 V.
Q48 (calculation)
A 0.3 m rod moves at 5 m/s perpendicular to a 0.2 T field. Find motional EMF.
EMF = B L v = 0.2 × 0.3 × 5 = 0.3 V.
Q49 (calculation)
A transformer has 400 primary turns and 1200 secondary turns. Primary voltage 230 V. Find secondary voltage.
Vₛ = Vₚ × (Nₛ/Nₚ) = 230 × (1200/400) = 230 × 3 = 690 V.
Q50 (calculation)
In an ideal transformer, primary voltage 220 V, secondary 55 V. If secondary current is 4 A, find primary current.
Power in = power out → Vₚ Iₚ = Vₛ Iₛ → 220 × Iₚ = 55 × 4 = 220 → Iₚ = 1 A.
Q51 (calculation)
A coil of self‑inductance 0.5 H has current changing at 2 A/s. Find induced EMF.
EMF = L ΔI/Δt = 0.5 × 2 = 1 V.
Q52 (calculation)
Calculate the energy stored in a 0.2 H inductor carrying 3 A.
U = ½ L I² = 0.5 × 0.2 × 9 = 0.1 × 9 = 0.9 J.
Q53 (calculation)
A 10 Ω resistor and 0.1 H inductor in series are connected to 100 V DC. Find the initial rate of change of current.
At t=0, current = 0, back EMF = L dI/dt = supply voltage → dI/dt = V/L = 100/0.1 = 1000 A/s.
Q54 (calculation)
A 20 μF capacitor has a capacitive reactance of 159 Ω at 50 Hz. Find the capacitance (using X_C = 1/(2πfC)).
From X_C = 1/(2πfC) → C = 1/(2πf X_C) = 1/(2×3.14×50×159) = 1/(2×3.14×7950) = 1/49926 ≈ 2.0×10⁻⁵ F = 20 μF (consistent).
Q55 (calculation)
An inductor has reactance 62.8 Ω at 50 Hz. Find its inductance.
X_L = 2πf L → L = X_L / (2πf) = 62.8 / (2×3.14×50) = 62.8 / 314 = 0.2 H.
Q56 (calculation)
A series RLC circuit has R = 20 Ω, X_L = 50 Ω, X_C = 30 Ω. Find impedance and phase angle.
X_net = X_L – X_C = 20 Ω. Z = √(R² + X_net²) = √(400 + 400) = √800 ≈ 28.28 Ω. tan φ = X_net/R = 20/20 = 1 → φ = 45° (voltage leads current).
Q57 (calculation)
A 230 V, 50 Hz AC supply is connected to a 50 Ω resistor. Find current and power.
I = V/R = 230/50 = 4.6 A. Power = I² R = (4.6)² × 50 = 21.16 × 50 = 1058 W. Or P = V I = 230×4.6 = 1058 W.
Q58 (calculation)
A 0.2 H inductor is connected to 100 V, 60 Hz supply. Find reactance and current.
X_L = 2π×60×0.2 = 75.4 Ω. I = V / X_L = 100 / 75.4 ≈ 1.33 A.
Q59 (calculation)

Q60 (calculation)

Q61 (calculation)
A 240 V, 60 W lamp is used on 220 V. Find the current and power consumed (assume constant resistance).
R = V²/P = 240²/60 = 57600/60 = 960 Ω. At 220 V: I = 220/960 = 0.229 A. Power = I²R = (0.229)² × 960 ≈ 50.4 W.
Q62 (calculation)

η = P_out / P_in → P_in = P_out / η = 200 / 0.9 ≈ 222.2 W.

ΔI/Δt = 5/0.2 = 25 A/s. EMF = L ΔI/Δt = 0.1 × 25 = 2.5 V.
Q65 (calculation)
Two coils have mutual inductance 0.2 H. If current in primary changes at 10 A/s, find induced EMF in secondary.
