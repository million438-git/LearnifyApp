📘 Thermodynamics – key ideas

Thermodynamics studies heat, work, temperature, and energy. The first law (ΔU = Q – W) is energy conservation. The second law (entropy increases) sets limits on efficiency. Key concepts: heat capacity, latent heat, ideal gas law (PV = nRT), isothermal, adiabatic, isobaric, isochoric processes. Phase changes, engines, refrigerators all rely on these principles.

📌 REMEMBER: For ideal gases, ΔU = nCvΔT, work is area under P–V curve, efficiency η = 1 – Tc/Th (Carnot).
✨ WOW: The same laws that explain why steam burns also tell us why you can’t build a 100% efficient engine!
Q1 (conceptual)
Explain why heat and temperature are different physical quantities.
Answer:
Temperature measures the average kinetic energy of particles. Heat is the energy transferred due to a temperature difference. Two objects can have the same temperature but different heat content (e.g., large vs small object).
Q2 (conceptual)
Why does specific heat capacity depend on the material?
Answer:
Different materials store kinetic energy differently due to molecular mass, bonding, and degrees of freedom. Q = mcΔT shows that for the same mass and ΔT, different materials need different Q.
Q3 (conceptual)
Why does conduction occur better in metals than in liquids?
Answer:
Metals have free electrons that move rapidly and transfer energy efficiently. In liquids, energy transfer relies on slower molecular collisions.
Q4 (conceptual)
Explain why thermal expansion occurs.
Answer:
As temperature increases, particles gain kinetic energy and their average separation increases, causing the material to expand (linear, area, volume expansion).
Q5 (conceptual)
Why does water have maximum density at 4°C?
Answer:
Hydrogen bonding creates an open crystalline structure below 4°C. At 4°C, the structure is most compact; below, it expands (ice floats).
Q6 (conceptual)
Explain why pressure of a gas increases when compressed at constant temperature.
Answer:
Boyle’s law: PV = constant. Decreasing volume increases collisions per unit area, so pressure rises.
Q7 (conceptual)
Why does the ideal gas law fail at high pressure?
Answer:
At high pressure, gas molecules occupy a significant fraction of the volume, and intermolecular forces become significant, so PV ≠ nRT.
Q8 (conceptual)
Explain why a refrigerator cools its contents.
Answer:
Compressor increases pressure of refrigerant, which then condenses and rejects heat outside. Expansion valve lowers pressure, causing rapid cooling, and the cold refrigerant absorbs heat from inside.
Q9 (conceptual)
Why is internal energy of an ideal gas only kinetic?
Answer:
Ideal gas assumes no intermolecular forces, so potential energy is zero. Internal energy comes only from translational (and rotational/vibrational for polyatomic) kinetic energy.
Q10 (conceptual)
Explain why work done by a gas is the area under the PV diagram.
Answer:
Work dW = P dV. Integrating from initial to final volume gives total work, which equals the area under the P‑V curve.
Q11 (conceptual)
Why does an adiabatic process cool a gas faster than an isothermal expansion?
Answer:
In adiabatic expansion, no heat is added, so the gas does work at the expense of its internal energy, causing temperature to drop. In isothermal, heat is supplied to maintain constant temperature.
Q12 (conceptual)
Explain why specific heat at constant volume differs from constant pressure.
Answer:
At constant pressure, gas expands and does work, so extra heat is needed compared to constant volume. Cp = Cv + nR for ideal gas.
Q13 (conceptual)
Why is latent heat independent of mass?
Answer:
Latent heat is energy per kilogram to change phase. It is a material property, so total Q = m L, but L itself does not depend on mass.
Q14 (conceptual)
Explain why steam burns more than boiling water at the same temperature.
Answer:
Steam releases latent heat of vaporization when it condenses on the skin, transferring much more energy than just hot water at 100°C.
Q15 (conceptual)
Why is thermodynamic efficiency always less than 100%?
Answer:
Second law: some heat must be rejected to a cold reservoir. Carnot efficiency η = 1 – Tc/Th is always < 1 unless Tc = 0 K (unattainable).
Q16 (conceptual)
Explain why heat flows spontaneously from hot to cold.
Answer:
This increases total entropy, following the second law of thermodynamics.
Q17 (conceptual)
Why does pressure remain constant during phase change at boiling point?
Answer:
During phase change, temperature is constant and pressure equals the vapor pressure at that temperature (e.g., atmospheric pressure for boiling water).
Q18 (conceptual)
Explain why Maxwell’s demon violates classical thermodynamics.
Answer:
The demon would sort fast and slow molecules, decreasing entropy without doing work, contradicting the second law.
Q19 (conceptual)
Why does gas expand in Joule‑Thomson effect cool or heat?
Answer:
For real gases, intermolecular forces cause energy redistribution during expansion. Ideal gases would have ΔT = 0.
Q20 (conceptual)
Why does conduction depend on cross‑sectional area and length?
Answer:
Q/t = k A ΔT / L: more area → more heat flow; longer path → slower transfer.
Q21 (conceptual)
Explain why black surfaces emit and absorb heat efficiently.
Answer:
High emissivity and absorptivity. They are good emitters (cooling) and good absorbers (heating) for thermal radiation.
Q22 (conceptual)
Why do gases exert pressure on container walls?
Answer:
Gas molecules collide with the walls, transferring momentum, which results in force per unit area – pressure.
Q23 (conceptual)
Explain why temperature rises slower for high‑specific‑heat liquids.
Answer:
Q = mcΔT, so for the same Q and m, larger c means smaller ΔT.
Q24 (conceptual)
Why is water used as coolant in engines?
Answer:
High specific heat capacity allows water to absorb large amounts of heat with moderate temperature rise. It is also abundant and flows easily.
Q25 (conceptual)
Explain why phase diagrams are important.
Answer:
They show stable phases at different T and P, and predict conditions for melting, boiling, sublimation, and triple point.
Q26 (conceptual)
Why is real gas compressibility factor > 1 at high P?
Answer:
Molecules occupy significant volume, making effective volume larger than ideal; repulsive forces dominate.
Q27 (conceptual)
Explain why thermometers use liquid expansion.
Answer:
Liquids expand linearly with temperature over a range, allowing calibration of a scale.
Q28 (conceptual)
Why does ice float on water?
Answer:
Ice density is less than water because of the open crystalline structure from hydrogen bonding, so buoyant force exceeds weight.
Q29 (conceptual)
Explain why heat engines use cyclic processes.
Answer:
The engine returns to its initial state after each cycle, so it can produce work repeatedly. Efficiency is defined over one cycle.
Q30 (conceptual)
Why is understanding thermodynamics critical for students and engineers?
Answer:
It governs heat, energy, and work in engines, refrigeration, power plants, and climate systems. Optimizing efficiency and predicting system behaviour are essential in engineering.
Q31 (calculation)
1 kg of water is heated from 20°C to 80°C. Specific heat c = 4200 J/kg°C. Find the heat absorbed.
Step‑by‑step:
Q = m c ΔT = 1 × 4200 × (80 – 20) = 4200 × 60 = 252,000 J.
✅ Answer: 252 kJ
Q32 (calculation)
0.5 kg of ice at 0°C melts completely. Latent heat Lf = 334 kJ/kg. Find the heat required.
Step‑by‑step:
Q = m L = 0.5 × 334,000 = 167,000 J.
✅ Answer: 167 kJ
Q33 (calculation)
1 kg of steam condenses at 100°C. Latent heat Lv = 2.26×10⁶ J/kg. Heat released?
Step‑by‑step:
Q = m L = 1 × 2.26×10⁶ = 2.26×10⁶ J.
✅ Answer: 2.26 MJ
Q34 (calculation)
1 mol of gas expands isothermally at 300 K from V₁ = 0.01 m³ to V₂ = 0.03 m³. Find work done (R = 8.314 J/mol·K).
Step‑by‑step:
W = n R T ln(V₂ / V₁) = 1 × 8.314 × 300 × ln(3) ≈ 8.314 × 300 × 1.0986 ≈ 2739 J.
✅ Answer: about 2740 J
Q35 (calculation)
1 mol of gas has Cv = 12.5 J/mol·K. If temperature rises by 20 K, find ΔU.
Step‑by‑step:
ΔU = n Cv ΔT = 1 × 12.5 × 20 = 250 J.
✅ Answer: 250 J
Q36 (calculation)
Gas at 2 atm expands adiabatically, γ = 1.4, V₂ / V₁ = 2. Find P₂.
Step‑by‑step:
P₂ = P₁ (V₁ / V₂)^γ = 2 × (1/2)^1.4 = 2 × 0.5^1.4. 0.5^1.4 ≈ 0.378. P₂ ≈ 0.756 atm.
✅ Answer: about 0.76 atm
Q37 (calculation)
An ideal engine absorbs 500 J at 600 K and rejects to 300 K. Find its efficiency.
Step‑by‑step:
η = 1 – Tc/Th = 1 – 300/600 = 0.5 = 50%.
✅ Answer: 50%
Q38 (calculation)
1 kg of air (Cp ≈ 1005 J/kg·K) is heated at constant pressure from 300 K to 600 K. Find Q.
Step‑by‑step:
Q = m Cp ΔT = 1 × 1005 × (600 – 300) = 1005 × 300 = 301,500 J = 301.5 kJ.
✅ Answer: 301.5 kJ
Q39 (calculation)
A heat engine does 400 J of work and rejects 600 J. Find its efficiency.
Step‑by‑step:
Q_h = W + Q_c = 400 + 600 = 1000 J. η = W / Q_h = 400 / 1000 = 0.4 = 40%.
✅ Answer: 40%
Q40 (calculation)
1 kg of water vapor at 100°C occupies 0.1 m³. Find its pressure (M = 18 g/mol, R = 8.314).
Step‑by‑step:
n = mass / molar mass = 1000 g / 18 g/mol ≈ 55.56 mol.
P = n R T / V = 55.56 × 8.314 × 373 / 0.1 ≈ 55.56 × 8.314 × 3730 ≈ 55.56 × 31000 ≈ 1.72×10⁶ Pa.
✅ Answer: about 1.72 MPa
Q41 (calculation)
Gas expands isobarically at P = 2×10⁵ Pa from V₁ = 1 L to V₂ = 3 L. Find work done.
Step‑by‑step:
ΔV = 3 L – 1 L = 2 L = 2×10⁻³ m³. W = P ΔV = 2×10⁵ × 2×10⁻³ = 400 J.
✅ Answer: 400 J
Q42 (calculation)
0.5 kg of ice at –10°C is warmed to 0°C. c_ice = 2100 J/kg·K. Find heat required.
Step‑by‑step:
Q = m c ΔT = 0.5 × 2100 × 10 = 10,500 J.
✅ Answer: 10.5 kJ
Q43 (calculation)
2 mol of gas, Cp = 29 J/mol·K, heated at constant pressure by 50 K. Find Q.
Step‑by‑step:
Q = n Cp ΔT = 2 × 29 × 50 = 2,900 J.
✅ Answer: 2900 J
Q44 (calculation)
A cylinder contains gas at P₁ = 10⁵ Pa, V₁ = 0.01 m³. It expands adiabatically (γ = 1.4) to V₂ = 0.02 m³. Find work done.
Step‑by‑step:
P₂ = P₁ (V₁/V₂)^γ = 10⁵ × (0.5)^1.4 ≈ 10⁵ × 0.378 = 3.78×10⁴ Pa.
W = (P₂V₂ – P₁V₁) / (1 – γ) = (3.78×10⁴×0.02 – 10⁵×0.01) / (1 – 1.4) = (756 – 1000) / (–0.4) = (–244) / (–0.4) = 610 J.
✅ Answer: about 610 J
Q45 (calculation)
A steam engine absorbs 1×10⁶ J and rejects 4×10⁵ J. Find work done and efficiency.
Step‑by‑step:
W = Q_h – Q_c = 1×10⁶ – 4×10⁵ = 6×10⁵ J. η = W / Q_h = 6×10⁵ / 1×10⁶ = 0.6 = 60%.
✅ Answer: 600 kJ, 60%
Q46 (calculation)
Air at P₁ = 10⁵ Pa, V₁ = 0.01 m³ is compressed isothermally so that volume halves. Find work done on the gas.
Step‑by‑step:
Work done by gas: W = nRT ln(V₂/V₁) = P₁V₁ ln(0.5) = 10⁵ × 0.01 × (–0.693) = –693 J. Work done on gas = +693 J.
✅ Answer: 693 J (on the gas)
Q47 (calculation)
A gas absorbs 500 J of heat and does 200 J of work. Find ΔU.
Step‑by‑step:
ΔU = Q – W = 500 – 200 = 300 J.
✅ Answer: 300 J
Q48 (calculation)
1 mol of monatomic gas (Cv = 12.5 J/mol·K) is heated by 100 K. Find ΔU.
Step‑by‑step:
ΔU = n Cv ΔT = 1 × 12.5 × 100 = 1250 J.
✅ Answer: 1250 J
Q49 (calculation)
1 kg of water cools from 80°C to 20°C. Find heat released (c = 4200 J/kg°C).
Step‑by‑step:
Q = m c ΔT = 1 × 4200 × (80 – 20) = 4200 × 60 = 252,000 J.
✅ Answer: 252 kJ
Q50 (calculation)
0.1 m³ of ideal gas at 10⁵ Pa and 300 K. Find number of moles (R = 8.314).
Step‑by‑step:
n = P V / (R T) = 10⁵ × 0.1 / (8.314 × 300) = 10⁴ / 2494.2 ≈ 4.01 mol.
✅ Answer: about 4.01 mol
Q51 (calculation)
Latent heat of fusion is 334 kJ/kg. How much heat to melt 0.2 kg of ice at 0°C?
Step‑by‑step:
Q = m L = 0.2 × 334,000 = 66,800 J.
✅ Answer: 66.8 kJ
Q52 (calculation)
1 mol gas, γ = 1.4, P₁ = 10⁵ Pa, V₁ = 0.01 m³, expands adiabatically to V₂ = 0.02 m³. Find P₂.
Step‑by‑step:
P₂ = P₁ (V₁/V₂)^γ = 10⁵ × (0.5)^1.4 ≈ 10⁵ × 0.378 = 3.78×10⁴ Pa.
✅ Answer: 3.78×10⁴ Pa
Q53 (calculation)
1 kg of water at 0°C is heated to 100°C and then vaporized. Total heat? (c = 4200, Lv = 2.26×10⁶ J/kg)
Step‑by‑step:
Sensible: Q₁ = 1×4200×100 = 420,000 J. Latent: Q₂ = 1×2.26×10⁶ = 2,260,000 J. Total = 2,680,000 J = 2.68 MJ.
✅ Answer: 2.68 MJ
Q54 (calculation)
Ideal gas: P = 2×10⁵ Pa, V = 0.05 m³, n = 2 mol. Find T (R = 8.314).
Step‑by‑step:
T = P V / (n R) = 2×10⁵ × 0.05 / (2 × 8.314) = 10⁴ / 16.628 ≈ 601 K.
✅ Answer: about 601 K
Q55 (calculation)
A heat engine absorbs 1 MJ and rejects 300 kJ. Find work and efficiency.
Step‑by‑step:
W = 1,000,000 – 300,000 = 700,000 J. η = 700,000 / 1,000,000 = 0.7 = 70%.
✅ Answer: 700 kJ, 70%
Q56 (calculation)
1 mol diatomic gas, Cv = 20.8 J/mol·K, heated by 50 K. Find ΔU.
Step‑by‑step:
ΔU = n Cv ΔT = 1 × 20.8 × 50 = 1040 J.
✅ Answer: 1040 J
Q57 (calculation)
0.5 kg steam at 100°C condenses and then cools to 50°C. Total heat released? (Lv = 2.26×10⁶, c = 4200)
Step‑by‑step:
Condensation: Q₁ = 0.5 × 2.26×10⁶ = 1,130,000 J. Cooling: Q₂ = 0.5 × 4200 × (100 – 50) = 0.5 × 4200 × 50 = 105,000 J. Total = 1,235,000 J = 1.235 MJ.
✅ Answer: 1.235 MJ
Q58 (calculation)
A gas expands isothermally, doing 500 J of work. Find ΔU and Q.
Step‑by‑step:
Isothermal: ΔU = 0. Q = W = 500 J.
✅ Answer: ΔU = 0, Q = 500 J
Q59 (calculation)
1 kg of ice at –10°C is turned into steam at 100°C. Total heat? (c_ice = 2100, Lf = 334,000, c_water = 4200, Lv = 2.26×10⁶)
Step‑by‑step:
Warm ice to 0°C: Q₁ = 1×2100×10 = 21,000 J. Melt ice: Q₂ = 1×334,000 = 334,000 J. Heat water to 100°C: Q₃ = 1×4200×100 = 420,000 J. Vaporize: Q₄ = 1×2.26×10⁶ = 2,260,000 J. Total = 3,035,000 J = 3.035 MJ.
✅ Answer: 3.035 MJ
Q60 (conceptual)
Explain why understanding thermodynamics is crucial for modern energy systems.
Answer:
Thermodynamics predicts energy conversion efficiency, designs engines, refrigeration, power plants, and climate systems. It is essential for sustainable technology and real‑world applications.
