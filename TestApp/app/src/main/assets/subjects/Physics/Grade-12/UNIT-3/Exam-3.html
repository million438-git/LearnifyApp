📘 Fluid mechanics – key ideas

Fluid mechanics studies the behavior of liquids and gases at rest and in motion. Key principles: pressure variation with depth (P = ρ g h), Pascal's principle (pressure transmitted equally), Archimedes' principle (buoyancy), equation of continuity (A₁v₁ = A₂v₂), and Bernoulli's equation (P + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant). These concepts explain why ships float, how hydraulic lifts work, and how airplanes fly.

📌 REMEMBER: Pressure at a point in a static fluid depends only on depth and fluid density – not on the shape of the container.
✨ WOW: The same principle that lets a submarine dive also keeps a hot‑air balloon floating!
Q1 (conceptual)
Explain why fluid pressure increases with depth.
Answer:
Pressure P = ρ g h. Greater depth means more fluid above, so the weight per unit area (pressure) is larger.
Q2 (conceptual)
Why is pressure transmitted equally in all directions in a static fluid?
Answer:
This is Pascal’s principle. Fluid molecules are free to move and push equally in all directions, so pressure is uniform at any point.
Q3 (conceptual)
Explain why buoyant force acts upward on submerged objects.
Answer:
Archimedes’ principle: the fluid exerts an upward force equal to the weight of the displaced fluid. The net result is a buoyant force upward.
Q4 (conceptual)
Why does a ship float even if made of steel?
Answer:
The ship’s average density (including air inside) is less than the density of water. The weight of displaced water equals the ship’s weight, so it floats.
Q5 (conceptual)
Explain why pressure at the bottom of a wide tank is independent of tank width.
Answer:
Pressure depends only on depth and fluid density (P = ρ g h). Lateral dimensions do not affect vertical pressure.
Q6 (conceptual)
Why does atmospheric pressure decrease with altitude?
Answer:
Less air above means smaller weight per unit area, so pressure decreases.
Q7 (conceptual)
Explain why liquids in connected vessels reach the same height.
Answer:
Hydrostatic equilibrium: pressure at the same horizontal level must be equal. If heights differed, pressure differences would cause flow until levels equalize.
Q8 (conceptual)
Why is Bernoulli’s principle applied in airplane lift?
Answer:
Faster air over the curved top of the wing creates lower pressure. The pressure difference between top and bottom generates lift.
Q9 (conceptual)
Why does a venturi meter measure fluid speed?
Answer:
At a constriction, velocity increases (continuity) and pressure decreases (Bernoulli). Measuring the pressure difference gives the speed.
Q10 (conceptual)
Explain why blood pressure drops in capillaries.
Answer:
Total cross‑sectional area of capillaries is large, so velocity decreases (continuity). Lower velocity means lower dynamic pressure, enabling nutrient exchange.
Q11 (conceptual)
Why is surface tension stronger in liquids with high intermolecular forces?
Answer:
High cohesive forces pull molecules inward more strongly, creating a tighter surface “skin” – higher surface tension.
Q12 (conceptual)
Explain why a bubble rises in a liquid.
Answer:
The bubble’s density is much lower than the liquid’s, so the buoyant force exceeds its weight, causing it to rise.
Q13 (conceptual)
Why does an object sink if its density is greater than the fluid’s density?
Answer:
Buoyant force equals weight of displaced fluid. If object density > fluid density, the object’s weight exceeds buoyant force, so it sinks.
Q14 (conceptual)
Explain why hydraulic lifts multiply force.
Answer:
Pascal’s principle: pressure is the same throughout. F₁/A₁ = F₂/A₂, so a small force on a small area produces a large force on a large area.
Q15 (conceptual)
Why is viscosity important in fluid flow?
Answer:
Viscosity measures internal friction. It determines flow rate, pressure drop, and energy loss – essential in lubrication, pipelines, and blood flow.
Q16 (calculation)
Water density 1000 kg/m³, depth 10 m. Find pressure at the bottom (g = 9.8 m/s²).
Step‑by‑step:
P = ρ g h = 1000 × 9.8 × 10 = 98,000 Pa.
✅ Answer: 98,000 Pa (or 98 kPa)
Q17 (calculation)
An object of volume 0.02 m³ is submerged in water. Find the buoyant force. (ρ_water = 1000 kg/m³, g = 9.8 m/s²)
Step‑by‑step:
F_b = ρ g V = 1000 × 9.8 × 0.02 = 196 N.
✅ Answer: 196 N
Q18 (calculation)
A steel block of volume 0.005 m³ and density 7800 kg/m³ is submerged in water. Find the net downward force. (ρ_water = 1000 kg/m³, g = 9.8 m/s²)
Step‑by‑step:
Weight = ρ_block × V × g = 7800 × 0.005 × 9.8 = 382.2 N.
Buoyant force = ρ_water × V × g = 1000 × 0.005 × 9.8 = 49 N.
Net downward force = 382.2 – 49 = 333.2 N.
✅ Answer: 333.2 N
Q19 (calculation)
A pipe narrows from cross‑sectional area 0.1 m² to 0.02 m². Water speed in the wide section is 2 m/s. Find the speed in the narrow section.
Step‑by‑step:
Continuity: A₁ v₁ = A₂ v₂ → v₂ = (A₁ v₁) / A₂ = (0.1 × 2) / 0.02 = 10 m/s.
✅ Answer: 10 m/s
Q20 (calculation)
For the same pipe, find the pressure difference between wide and narrow sections (use Bernoulli). ρ = 1000 kg/m³.
Step‑by‑step:
Bernoulli: P₁ + ½ρv₁² = P₂ + ½ρv₂² → ΔP = ½ρ(v₂² – v₁²) = ½×1000×(10² – 2²) = 500 × (100 – 4) = 500 × 96 = 48,000 Pa.
✅ Answer: 48,000 Pa (pressure is lower in the narrow section)
Q21 (calculation)
A hydraulic lift has small piston area 0.01 m² and large piston area 1 m². An input force of 100 N is applied. Find the output force.
Step‑by‑step:
F₂ = F₁ × (A₂ / A₁) = 100 × (1 / 0.01) = 100 × 100 = 10,000 N.
✅ Answer: 10,000 N
Q22 (calculation)
A 50 kg diver jumps into a 5 m deep pool. Find the average upward force exerted by the water if he comes to rest in 2 s after entering. (g = 9.8 m/s²)
Step‑by‑step:
Speed at entry: v = √(2 g h) = √(2 × 9.8 × 5) = √98 ≈ 9.9 m/s.
Deceleration: a = Δv / t = 9.9 / 2 ≈ 4.95 m/s² upward.
Net upward force = m (g + a) = 50 × (9.8 + 4.95) = 50 × 14.75 = 737.5 N.
✅ Answer: about 738 N
Q23 (calculation)
A venturi meter has A₁ = 0.05 m², A₂ = 0.02 m², v₁ = 2 m/s, P₁ = 2×10⁵ Pa. Find P₂ (ρ = 1000 kg/m³).
Step‑by‑step:
v₂ = A₁ v₁ / A₂ = 0.05×2 / 0.02 = 5 m/s.
Bernoulli: P₂ = P₁ + ½ρ(v₁² – v₂²) = 2×10⁵ + 500×(4 – 25) = 200,000 – 10,500 = 189,500 Pa.
✅ Answer: 1.895×10⁵ Pa
Q24 (calculation)
A bubble rises 2 m in a liquid of density 1200 kg/m³. Find the change in pressure it experiences. (g = 9.8 m/s²)
Step‑by‑step:
ΔP = ρ g h = 1200 × 9.8 × 2 = 23,520 Pa.
✅ Answer: 23,520 Pa (decrease as it rises)
Q25 (calculation)
A cylindrical container has radius 0.5 m and height 1 m. It is filled with a fluid of density 850 kg/m³. Find the force on the bottom. (g = 9.8 m/s²)
Step‑by‑step:
Area A = π r² = 3.1416 × 0.25 ≈ 0.7854 m².
Pressure P = ρ g h = 850 × 9.8 × 1 = 8330 Pa.
Force F = P × A = 8330 × 0.7854 ≈ 6540 N.
✅ Answer: about 6540 N
Q26 (calculation)
Oil flows in a pipe of diameter 0.1 m at speed 0.5 m/s. The kinematic viscosity is 1×10⁻⁵ m²/s. Find the Reynolds number and determine if the flow is laminar or turbulent.
Step‑by‑step:
Re = v D / ν = 0.5 × 0.1 / 1×10⁻⁵ = 0.05 / 10⁻⁵ = 5000.
Since Re > 4000, flow is turbulent.
✅ Answer: Re = 5000, turbulent
Q27 (calculation)
Water flows over a dam of height 2 m. Find the pressure at the base of the dam (ρ = 1000 kg/m³, g = 9.8 m/s²).
Step‑by‑step:
P = ρ g h = 1000 × 9.8 × 2 = 19,600 Pa.
✅ Answer: 19,600 Pa
Q28 (calculation)
A ship displaces 2000 m³ of water. Find the buoyant force acting on it. (ρ_water = 1000 kg/m³, g = 9.8 m/s²)
Step‑by‑step:
F_b = ρ g V = 1000 × 9.8 × 2000 = 19,600,000 N = 1.96×10⁷ N.
✅ Answer: 1.96×10⁷ N
Q29 (calculation)
Find the speed of efflux from a hole at depth 10 m below the surface of a liquid (Torricelli’s law, g = 9.8 m/s²).
Step‑by‑step:
v = √(2 g h) = √(2 × 9.8 × 10) = √196 = 14 m/s.
✅ Answer: 14 m/s
Q30 (conceptual)
Explain why understanding fluid mechanics is essential in engineering and medicine.
Answer:
Fluid mechanics predicts flow, pressure, and forces in systems like pipelines, pumps, blood circulation, and aerospace. It enables efficient design, safety, and optimization – from artificial hearts to aircraft wings.
