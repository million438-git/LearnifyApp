⚡ Grade 12 Physics – Chapter 4

Electromagnetism · complete notes + 60 calculation‑based challenges

4.1 Electric field – the invisible force field

An electric field exists in the space around any electric charge. It exerts a force on other charges placed in it.

  • Direction: away from positive charges, toward negative charges.
  • Strength (magnitude): electric field strength E equals force F divided by the charge q (E = F / q). Unit: newton per coulomb (N/C).
  • Field lines: start on positive, end on negative; closer lines mean stronger field.
🌟 WOW: The electric field around a point charge gets weaker with distance – it follows an inverse‑square law (double the distance, one‑quarter the strength).
📘 REMEMBER: Electric field is a vector – it has both magnitude and direction.

4.2 Magnetic field – the region of magnetic influence

A magnetic field is produced by moving charges (currents) or by permanent magnets. It exerts a force on other moving charges or magnetic materials.

  • Direction: outside a magnet, from north to south; inside, from south to north.
  • Magnetic field strength B measured in tesla (T).
  • Magnetic flux = field strength × area perpendicular to the field (in words: flux equals B times area when field is perpendicular).
🧲 WOW: Earth’s magnetic field is about 0.00005 tesla – very weak, yet it deflects solar wind and makes compasses work.

4.3 Lorentz force – when charges move through a magnetic field

A charged particle moving in a magnetic field experiences a force perpendicular to both its velocity and the field. The magnitude is given by:

force = charge × speed × magnetic field strength × sine of the angle between velocity and field.

If the motion is perpendicular (angle = 90°, sine = 1), the formula becomes force = charge × speed × B.

  • Right‑hand rule: fingers = field, thumb = velocity (positive charge), palm = force direction.
📘 REMEMBER: The Lorentz force does no work – it changes only the direction, not the speed.

4.4 Force on a wire in a magnetic field

A straight wire carrying current in a magnetic field feels a force:

force = magnetic field strength × current × length of wire × sine of angle between wire and field.

When the wire is perpendicular (angle 90°): force = B × I × L.

This is the principle behind electric motors.

🔄 WOW: The force direction reverses if either current direction or magnetic field direction is reversed – that’s how motors can spin both ways.

4.5 Faraday’s law – changing flux creates EMF

When the magnetic flux through a loop changes, an electromotive force (EMF) is induced in the loop. The magnitude is:

induced EMF = number of turns × rate of change of flux.

Rate of change means (final flux – initial flux) divided by time interval.

Lenz’s law: The induced current always flows in a direction that opposes the change causing it.

📘 REMEMBER: You can induce EMF by moving a magnet, changing the loop area, or rotating the loop in a field.

4.6 Motors & generators – two sides of the same coin

  • Electric motor: current in a magnetic field → torque → rotation (electrical → mechanical).
  • Generator: motion in a magnetic field → induced EMF (mechanical → electrical).
  • Back EMF in motors: as the coil rotates, it also acts as a generator, inducing an opposing EMF.
⚡ WOW: When you switch on a fan, the initial current is high because back EMF hasn’t built up yet – that’s why motors sometimes draw more current at start.

4.7 Transformers – changing voltage levels

A transformer consists of two coils (primary and secondary) wound on a common iron core. It works only with AC.

Voltage ratio = turns ratio: Vp / Vs = Np / Ns

For an ideal transformer: power in = power out → Vp × Ip = Vs × Is

  • Step‑up: more turns in secondary → higher voltage.
  • Step‑down: fewer turns in secondary → lower voltage.
📘 REMEMBER: Transformers are why we can transmit electricity at high voltage (low current) to reduce losses, then step it down for home use.

4.8 Where electromagnetism touches your life

  • Fans, washing machines, electric cars – motors.
  • Power plants – huge generators.
  • Wireless charging – induction.
  • MRI machines – strong magnetic fields + radio waves.
  • Maglev trains – magnetic levitation.
🚀 WOW: The entire modern world runs on electromagnetism – without it, no internet, no TV, no lights.

📋 60 calculation challenges (with step‑by‑step answers)

All answers use plain words – no confusing symbols. Each includes a “remember” or “wow” fact.

1. A charge of 4 coulombs experiences a force of 20 newtons in an electric field. Find the electric field strength.
Electric field strength equals force divided by charge. So 20 divided by 4 = 5 newtons per coulomb.
🌟 WOW: This means each coulomb of charge would feel a force of 5 newtons.
2. What force acts on a charge of 3 coulombs placed in an electric field of strength 8 newtons per coulomb?
Force = electric field strength × charge = 8 × 3 = 24 newtons.
📘 REMEMBER: Direction of force depends on sign of charge – positive charge feels force in field direction.
3. A test charge of 2 coulombs is placed at a point where it feels 10 newtons toward the east. What is the electric field at that point?
E = F/q = 10/2 = 5 newtons per coulomb eastward.
🌟 WOW: If you placed a negative charge there, it would feel force westward.
4. At a certain point, the electric field is 15 N/C north. What force would a charge of –4 C feel?
Magnitude = 15 × 4 = 60 newtons. Direction is opposite to field (since negative) → south.
📘 REMEMBER: Negative charges experience force opposite to electric field direction.
5. Two parallel plates create a uniform electric field of 200 N/C. What force acts on an electron (charge –1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C) between them?
Force magnitude = 200 × 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ = 3.2×10⁻¹⁷ newtons. Direction opposite the field.
🌟 WOW: Even though the force is tiny, electrons accelerate hugely because their mass is tiny.
6. A charge of 5 C is moved in an electric field. The work done against the field is 100 J. What is the potential difference (voltage) between the two points?
Voltage = work / charge = 100 / 5 = 20 volts.
📘 REMEMBER: Voltage is electric potential energy per unit charge.
7. A 12 V battery is connected across a 4 Ω resistor. How much charge flows through the resistor in 5 seconds?
First, current I = V/R = 12/4 = 3 amperes. Charge = current × time = 3 × 5 = 15 coulombs.
🌟 WOW: One ampere is one coulomb per second – so 3 A for 5 s = 15 C.
8. A point charge of 2×10⁻⁶ C experiences a force of 0.5 N. Find the electric field strength at that point.
E = F/q = 0.5 / (2×10⁻⁶) = 250 000 N/C.
📘 REMEMBER: Fields near point charges can be huge.
9. If the electric field strength is 400 N/C, what force will a charge of 0.02 C feel?
F = E × q = 400 × 0.02 = 8 newtons.
🌟 WOW: That’s about the weight of a small apple – electric forces can be very noticeable.
10. A proton (charge +1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C) is in an electric field of 500 N/C east. Find its acceleration (mass of proton = 1.67×10⁻²⁷ kg).
Force = 500 × 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ = 8×10⁻¹⁷ N. Acceleration = F/m = 8×10⁻¹⁷ / 1.67×10⁻²⁷ ≈ 4.79×10¹⁰ m/s² east.
📘 REMEMBER: That’s billions of times g – protons accelerate incredibly fast in electric fields.
11. A proton moves at 2×10⁶ m/s perpendicular to a 0.1 T magnetic field. Charge = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C. Find the magnetic force.
Force = q v B (perpendicular) = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ × 2×10⁶ × 0.1 = 3.2×10⁻¹⁴ newtons.
🌟 WOW: This force makes the proton circle – the radius can be found from centripetal force.
12. An electron (q = –1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C) moves at 5×10⁶ m/s perpendicular to a 0.05 T field. Find force magnitude.
Force = q v B = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ × 5×10⁶ × 0.05 = 4×10⁻¹⁴ N.
📘 REMEMBER: The negative sign only affects direction, not magnitude.
13. A charge of 2 μC (2×10⁻⁶ C) moves at 100 m/s at 30° to a 0.2 T field. Find force. (sin30° = 0.5)
Force = q v B sinθ = 2×10⁻⁶ × 100 × 0.2 × 0.5 = 2×10⁻⁵ newtons.
🌟 WOW: If it were perpendicular, force would be double.
14. A particle with charge 3.2×10⁻¹⁹ C moves at 4×10⁶ m/s perpendicular to a field of 0.25 T. Find force.
F = 3.2×10⁻¹⁹ × 4×10⁶ × 0.25 = 3.2×10⁻¹⁹ × 1×10⁶ = 3.2×10⁻¹³ N.
📘 REMEMBER: The force is always perpendicular to velocity – so it causes circular motion.
15. A proton circles in a 0.2 T field at speed 1×10⁷ m/s. Find the radius. (m_p = 1.67×10⁻²⁷ kg, q = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C)
Magnetic force provides centripetal force: q v B = m v² / r → r = m v / (q B) = (1.67×10⁻²⁷ × 1×10⁷) / (1.6×10⁻¹⁹ × 0.2) = (1.67×10⁻²⁰) / (3.2×10⁻²⁰) = 0.522 metres.
🌟 WOW: That’s about half a metre – you could see that circle in a bubble chamber!
16. What magnetic field is needed so that a proton moving at 3×10⁶ m/s experiences a force of 4.8×10⁻¹⁴ N? (q = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C, perpendicular)
F = q v B → B = F/(q v) = 4.8×10⁻¹⁴ / (1.6×10⁻¹⁹ × 3×10⁶) = 4.8×10⁻¹⁴ / (4.8×10⁻¹³) = 0.1 tesla.
📘 REMEMBER: That’s a typical strong electromagnet field.
17. An alpha particle (q = 3.2×10⁻¹⁹ C, m = 6.64×10⁻²⁷ kg) moves at 2×10⁶ m/s perpendicular to 0.15 T. Find its acceleration.
Force = q v B = 3.2×10⁻¹⁹ × 2×10⁶ × 0.15 = 9.6×10⁻¹⁴ N. a = F/m = 9.6×10⁻¹⁴ / 6.64×10⁻²⁷ = 1.45×10¹³ m/s².
🌟 WOW: That’s a trillion times g – it would circle very fast.
18. A 3 μC charge moves at 500 m/s at 60° to a 0.3 T field. Find force (sin60° = 0.866).
F = q v B sinθ = 3×10⁻⁶ × 500 × 0.3 × 0.866 = 3×10⁻⁶ × 500 × 0.2598 ≈ 3×10⁻⁶ × 129.9 = 3.9×10⁻⁴ N.
📘 REMEMBER: The force is maximum when velocity is perpendicular to field.
19. A proton moves parallel to a magnetic field of 0.5 T at speed 2×10⁶ m/s. What force acts on it?
When velocity is parallel, sinθ = 0 → force = 0.
🌟 WOW: No magnetic force when moving along field lines – it just goes straight.
20. An electron moves at 1×10⁷ m/s perpendicular to a 0.02 T field. Find the radius of its path (m_e = 9.11×10⁻³¹ kg, q = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C).
r = m v / (q B) = (9.11×10⁻³¹ × 1×10⁷) / (1.6×10⁻¹⁹ × 0.02) = (9.11×10⁻²⁴) / (3.2×10⁻²¹) = 0.00285 m = 2.85 mm.
📘 REMEMBER: Electrons have tiny mass so they circle very tightly.
21. A wire 0.5 m long carries 3 A perpendicular to a 0.2 T field. Find force.
F = B I L = 0.2 × 3 × 0.5 = 0.3 newtons.
🌟 WOW: That’s about the weight of a small apple – measurable.
22. A wire 2 m long with current 5 A lies at 60° to a 0.1 T field. Find force (sin60°=0.866).
F = B I L sinθ = 0.1 × 5 × 2 × 0.866 = 0.866 newtons.
📘 REMEMBER: Force is less when wire isn’t perpendicular.
23. What current in a 1.2 m wire perpendicular to 0.3 T gives a force of 1.8 N?
I = F / (B L) = 1.8 / (0.3 × 1.2) = 1.8 / 0.36 = 5 amperes.
🌟 WOW: That’s a typical current for a power tool.
24. A wire 0.8 m long carries 4 A at 30° to a 0.25 T field. Find force (sin30°=0.5).
F = 0.25 × 4 × 0.8 × 0.5 = 0.4 newtons.
📘 REMEMBER: Angle matters – if it were perpendicular, force would be 0.8 N.
25. A wire experiences 0.6 N force when 2 A flows in a 0.4 T field perpendicular. Find its length.
L = F / (B I) = 0.6 / (0.4 × 2) = 0.6 / 0.8 = 0.75 m.
🌟 WOW: So the force depends directly on how long the wire is in the field.
26. A 0.6 m wire carries 2.5 A perpendicular to a field and feels 0.3 N. Find B.
B = F / (I L) = 0.3 / (2.5 × 0.6) = 0.3 / 1.5 = 0.2 tesla.
📘 REMEMBER: 1 tesla is a pretty strong field – like a large MRI magnet.
27. A wire 1 m long carries 10 A at 45° to 0.05 T. Find force (sin45°≈0.707).
F = 0.05 × 10 × 1 × 0.707 = 0.3535 N.
🌟 WOW: Even a modest field can create noticeable force with high current.
28. Two parallel wires 0.3 m apart carry currents 5 A and 8 A in same direction. Force per metre? (μ₀/4π = 10⁻⁷) Use formula F/L = (2×10⁻⁷ × I₁ I₂)/d in words: force per metre = 2×10⁻⁷ times product of currents divided by distance.
F/L = 2×10⁻⁷ × (5×8) / 0.3 = 2×10⁻⁷ × 40 / 0.3 = 80×10⁻⁷ / 0.3 = (8×10⁻⁶)/0.3 ≈ 2.67×10⁻⁵ N/m. Attractive.
📘 REMEMBER: Parallel currents attract, opposite currents repel.
29. The force on a 2 m wire carrying 6 A perpendicular to a field is 2.4 N. Find B.
B = F / (I L) = 2.4 / (6 × 2) = 2.4 / 12 = 0.2 T.
🌟 WOW: That’s the same field strength as earlier – fields can be uniform.
30. A wire 0.5 m long carries 3 A at 90° to a 0.1 T field. What force?
F = 0.1 × 3 × 0.5 = 0.15 N.
📘 REMEMBER: The right‑hand rule gives the direction – palm push for positive current.
31. A coil of 100 turns has a magnetic flux change of 0.4 Wb in 0.2 s. Find average induced EMF.
EMF = N × ΔΦ/Δt = 100 × 0.4 / 0.2 = 100 × 2 = 200 volts.
🌟 WOW: That’s enough to power a bright light bulb!
32. A loop of wire has flux change from 0.02 Wb to 0.08 Wb in 0.01 s. Number of turns = 1. Find EMF.
ΔΦ = 0.08 – 0.02 = 0.06 Wb. EMF = ΔΦ/Δt = 0.06 / 0.01 = 6 volts.
📘 REMEMBER: Faster change gives higher EMF.
33. A 50‑turn coil’s flux changes from 0.1 Wb to 0.5 Wb in 0.4 s. Find induced EMF.
ΔΦ = 0.4 Wb, Δt = 0.4 s → ΔΦ/Δt = 1 Wb/s. EMF = 50 × 1 = 50 V.
🌟 WOW: That’s like a small generator.
34. A magnetic field through a 200‑turn coil changes from 0.2 T to 0.6 T in 0.5 s. The coil area is 0.01 m² and field perpendicular. Find EMF.
Flux = B × A. Initial flux = 0.2 × 0.01 = 0.002 Wb; final = 0.6 × 0.01 = 0.006 Wb. ΔΦ = 0.004 Wb. Δt = 0.5 s. EMF = N × ΔΦ/Δt = 200 × 0.004 / 0.5 = 200 × 0.008 = 1.6 volts.
📘 REMEMBER: EMF can be small even with many turns if change is slow.
35. A coil of 500 turns has area 0.02 m². The magnetic field perpendicular to it drops from 0.3 T to 0 T in 0.1 s. Find EMF.
Initial flux = 0.3 × 0.02 = 0.006 Wb; final = 0. ΔΦ = 0.006 Wb. EMF = 500 × 0.006 / 0.1 = 500 × 0.06 = 30 volts.
🌟 WOW: That’s enough to power small electronics.
36. A generator coil rotates in a 0.1 T field. Coil area 0.05 m², 100 turns. It rotates from parallel to perpendicular in 0.2 s (flux change from 0 to B×A). Find average EMF.
Flux change = B×A – 0 = 0.1 × 0.05 = 0.005 Wb. EMF = 100 × 0.005 / 0.2 = 100 × 0.025 = 2.5 volts.
📘 REMEMBER: This is how AC generators work – rotating coils produce alternating EMF.
37. A 10‑turn loop has area 0.1 m². Magnetic field perpendicular increases from 0 to 0.5 T in 0.01 s. Find EMF.
ΔΦ = 0.5 × 0.1 = 0.05 Wb. EMF = 10 × 0.05 / 0.01 = 10 × 5 = 50 volts.
🌟 WOW: Fast change gives high voltage spikes.
38. A coil experiences flux change of 0.2 Wb in 0.05 s. If EMF is 40 V, how many turns?
EMF = N × ΔΦ/Δt → 40 = N × 0.2 / 0.05 = N × 4 → N = 10 turns.
📘 REMEMBER: More turns increase induced EMF.
39. A rod of length 0.5 m moves at 4 m/s perpendicular to a 0.3 T field. Find motional EMF across its ends.
Motional EMF = B × L × v = 0.3 × 0.5 × 4 = 0.6 volts.
🌟 WOW: This is the principle behind velocity sensors and some generators.
40. An airplane wing of length 30 m moves through Earth’s vertical field component 2×10⁻⁵ T at 200 m/s. Find induced EMF across wingtips (assume perpendicular).
EMF = B L v = 2×10⁻⁵ × 30 × 200 = 2×10⁻⁵ × 6000 = 0.12 volts.
📘 REMEMBER: Very small, but measurable.
41. A transformer has 200 primary turns and 50 secondary turns. Primary voltage 120 V AC. Find secondary voltage.
Vp/Vs = Np/Ns → 120/Vs = 200/50 = 4 → Vs = 120/4 = 30 volts.
🌟 WOW: That’s a step‑down transformer – used in phone chargers.
42. If secondary voltage is 12 V and primary is 240 V, with secondary turns = 60, find primary turns.
Np/Ns = Vp/Vs → Np/60 = 240/12 = 20 → Np = 60 × 20 = 1200 turns.
📘 REMEMBER: High voltage side has many turns.
43. A step‑up transformer has 100 primary turns and 400 secondary turns. Primary voltage 25 V. Find secondary voltage.
Vs = Vp × (Ns/Np) = 25 × 400/100 = 25 × 4 = 100 V.
🌟 WOW: Voltage is stepped up by factor 4.
44. Ideal transformer: primary voltage 220 V, secondary voltage 55 V. If secondary current is 4 A, find primary current.
Power in = power out → Vp×Ip = Vs×Is → 220 × Ip = 55 × 4 = 220 → Ip = 220/220 = 1 A.
📘 REMEMBER: In a step‑down, primary current is lower than secondary.
45. A transformer with 80% efficiency: primary power 200 W. Find secondary power.
Secondary power = efficiency × primary power = 0.8 × 200 = 160 W.
🌟 WOW: Losses appear as heat in the core and windings.
46. A transformer steps 240 V down to 12 V. If primary current is 0.5 A (ideal), find secondary current.
Vp Ip = Vs Is → 240 × 0.5 = 12 × Is → 120 = 12 Is → Is = 10 A.
📘 REMEMBER: Low voltage side carries higher current.
47. A transformer has turns ratio Np:Ns = 5:1. Primary connected to 240 V, secondary to a 6 Ω load. Find secondary current.
Vs = Vp × (Ns/Np) = 240 × 1/5 = 48 V. Is = Vs / R = 48 / 6 = 8 A.
🌟 WOW: That’s enough to power a small heater.
48. If primary voltage 120 V, primary turns 300, secondary turns 30, find secondary voltage. Also find secondary current if a 15 Ω load is connected (ideal).
Vs = 120 × 30/300 = 12 V. Is = 12 / 15 = 0.8 A.
📘 REMEMBER: This is typical for a doorbell transformer.
49. An ideal transformer has Np = 400, Ns = 2000, primary voltage 50 V. Find secondary voltage and current if secondary load is 100 Ω.
Vs = 50 × 2000/400 = 50 × 5 = 250 V. Is = 250 / 100 = 2.5 A.
🌟 WOW: This step‑up transformer raises voltage for transmission.
50. A transformer has primary power 1000 W, secondary voltage 100 V, secondary current 8 A. Find efficiency.
Secondary power = Vs × Is = 100 × 8 = 800 W. Efficiency = 800/1000 = 0.8 = 80%.
📘 REMEMBER: Losses are due to resistance, eddy currents, and hysteresis.
51. A 0.2 m wire carries 5 A perpendicular to a 0.3 T field. Find force.
F = 0.3 × 5 × 0.2 = 0.3 newtons.
🌟 WOW: That’s about the force to lift a small egg.
52. A charge of 2 C moves at 10 m/s at 90° to a 0.5 T field. Find force.
F = 2 × 10 × 0.5 = 10 newtons.
📘 REMEMBER: Large charges feel huge forces.
53. A coil of 50 turns, area 0.04 m², is in a perpendicular field of 0.2 T. The field is reversed in 0.1 s. Find average EMF. (Hint: flux change = 2 × B×A)
Initial flux = 0.2×0.04 = 0.008 Wb; final flux = –0.008 Wb (reverse). ΔΦ = 0.008 – (–0.008) = 0.016 Wb. EMF = 50 × 0.016 / 0.1 = 50 × 0.16 = 8 volts.
🌟 WOW: Reversing field doubles the change – good for generating higher EMF.
54. A proton moves in a circle of radius 0.1 m in a 0.2 T field. Find its speed. (m_p = 1.67×10⁻²⁷ kg, q = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C)
From r = m v / (q B) → v = r q B / m = 0.1 × 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ × 0.2 / 1.67×10⁻²⁷ = 0.1 × 3.2×10⁻²⁰ / 1.67×10⁻²⁷ = 3.2×10⁻²¹ / 1.67×10⁻²⁷ ≈ 1.92×10⁶ m/s.
📘 REMEMBER: This is about 2 million m/s – typical for particles in magnetic fields.
55. A 0.5 m wire carries 4 A at 30° to a 0.25 T field. Find force (sin30°=0.5).
F = 0.25 × 4 × 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25 newtons.
🌟 WOW: Force is half of what it would be if perpendicular.
56. A 240 V / 12 V transformer has 600 primary turns. Find secondary turns.
Ns = Np × (Vs/Vp) = 600 × 12/240 = 600 × 0.05 = 30 turns.
📘 REMEMBER: Secondary has far fewer turns for step‑down.
57. A generator produces 50 Hz AC. If the magnetic field is 0.1 T and coil area 0.02 m² with 100 turns, what is the maximum EMF? (Hint: max EMF = N B A ω, with ω = 2πf = 2×3.14×50 = 314 rad/s)
Max EMF = N B A ω = 100 × 0.1 × 0.02 × 314 = 100 × 0.002 × 314 = 0.2 × 314 = 62.8 volts.
🌟 WOW: That’s the peak voltage – RMS would be about 44 V.
58. A wire 0.3 m long moves at 5 m/s perpendicular to a 0.4 T field. Find motional EMF.
EMF = B L v = 0.4 × 0.3 × 5 = 0.6 volts.
📘 REMEMBER: This is like a simple velocity‑sensing device.
59. A 10‑turn coil of area 0.05 m² is in a perpendicular field that changes from 0.1 T to 0.5 T in 0.2 s. Find EMF.
ΔB = 0.4 T, ΔΦ = ΔB × A = 0.4 × 0.05 = 0.02 Wb. EMF = 10 × 0.02 / 0.2 = 10 × 0.1 = 1 volt.
🌟 WOW: Even a moderate change can produce a volt.
60. A step‑down transformer (95% efficient) has primary voltage 220 V, secondary 55 V. If secondary current is 10 A, find primary current.
Secondary power = 55 × 10 = 550 W. Primary power = secondary power / efficiency = 550 / 0.95 ≈ 579 W. Primary current = 579 / 220 ≈ 2.63 A.
📘 REMEMBER: Efficiency less than 100% means primary draws slightly more power than secondary delivers.
