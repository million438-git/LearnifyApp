📘 2D motion – key ideas

Two‑dimensional motion combines horizontal and vertical components independently. Projectile motion is the classic example: horizontal velocity constant, vertical acceleration constant (g). Circular motion involves centripetal acceleration toward the center. Understanding vectors, components, and independence is essential for solving real‑world problems like satellite orbits, sports, and vehicle dynamics.

📌 REMEMBER: Horizontal and vertical motions are independent. Use vₓ = v cosθ, vᵧ = v sinθ.
✨ WOW: The same physics that lets you throw a ball also lets you aim a rocket to the Moon!
Q1 (conceptual)
Explain why projectile motion can be analyzed independently in horizontal and vertical directions.
Answer:
Horizontal motion has no force (ignoring air resistance) → constant velocity. Vertical motion has constant downward acceleration due to gravity. These two motions are perpendicular and do not influence each other, so they can be solved separately.
Q2 (conceptual)
Why is the trajectory of a projectile parabolic?
Answer:
Horizontal displacement is linear in time (x = v₀ₓ t). Vertical displacement is quadratic in time (y = v₀ᵧ t – ½ g t²). Eliminating t gives y as a quadratic function of x → a parabola.
Q3 (conceptual)
Explain why maximum range occurs at 45° launch angle (ignoring air resistance).
Answer:
The range formula is R = (v₀² sin 2θ) / g. sin 2θ reaches its maximum value of 1 when 2θ = 90°, so θ = 45°. Any other angle gives a smaller range.
Q4 (conceptual)
Why does horizontal velocity remain constant in ideal projectile motion?
Answer:
No horizontal force acts on the projectile (neglecting air resistance). By Newton’s first law, velocity in the horizontal direction remains constant.
Q5 (conceptual)
Explain why vertical velocity changes during projectile motion.
Answer:
Gravity acts downward, producing a constant acceleration g. This changes the vertical component: upward velocity decreases by g each second, becomes zero at the top, then increases downward.
Q6 (conceptual)
Why is the time of flight the same for symmetric launch and landing heights?
Answer:
The upward and downward paths are mirror images. Time up equals time down, so total time = 2 × (vertical component / g).
Q7 (conceptual)
Explain why centripetal acceleration is perpendicular to velocity in circular motion.
Answer:
Centripetal acceleration points toward the center of the circle, always perpendicular to the instantaneous velocity (which is tangent to the circle). This changes the direction of velocity without changing its magnitude.
Q8 (conceptual)
Why does angular velocity remain constant in uniform circular motion?
Answer:
In uniform circular motion, the speed is constant, so the rate of change of angle (angular velocity = v / r) is constant. Only the direction of linear velocity changes.
Q9 (conceptual)
Explain why banking of roads helps vehicles turn safely.
Answer:
Banking provides a component of the normal force toward the center of the curve, contributing to the required centripetal force. This reduces reliance on friction and allows higher safe speeds.
Q10 (conceptual)
Why does horizontal range decrease if launch height is above ground?
Answer:
With a positive launch height, the time of flight increases because the projectile falls farther. However, the optimal angle for maximum range becomes less than 45° because the extra flight time favors a larger horizontal component.
Q11 (conceptual)
Explain the effect of air resistance on 2D motion.
Answer:
Air resistance opposes motion, reducing both horizontal and vertical velocities. It decreases maximum height, range, and the trajectory becomes non‑parabolic (asymmetric).
Q12 (conceptual)
Why does a satellite in orbit undergo “free‑fall” motion?
Answer:
A satellite is continuously falling toward Earth due to gravity, but its tangential speed is so high that Earth’s surface curves away at the same rate. It is in constant free fall, hence weightlessness.
Q13 (conceptual)
Why is relative velocity important in projectile motion in moving reference frames?
Answer:
Observers in different frames see different horizontal velocities. For example, a person on a moving platform sees a different horizontal component, while vertical motion remains the same because gravity is unaffected.
Q14 (conceptual)
Explain why trajectory curvature depends on initial speed and launch angle.
Answer:
The shape of the parabola is determined by the ratio v₀ᵧ / v₀ₓ. A larger vertical component gives a higher arc; a larger horizontal component stretches the parabola horizontally. Speed scales both components equally, increasing size but preserving shape if angle fixed.
Q15 (conceptual)
Why does centripetal force not do work on an object in circular motion?
Answer:
Centripetal force is always perpendicular to the displacement (direction of motion). Work = force × displacement × cos φ, and cos 90° = 0, so no work is done; the object’s speed remains constant.
Q16 (calculation)
A projectile is launched at 30 m/s at 60° above horizontal. Find: (a) maximum height, (b) time of flight, (c) horizontal range. (g = 9.8 m/s²)
Step‑by‑step:
v₀ₓ = 30 × cos60° = 30 × 0.5 = 15 m/s.
v₀ᵧ = 30 × sin60° = 30 × 0.8660 ≈ 25.98 m/s.
(a) H = v₀ᵧ² / (2g) = (25.98)² / (2×9.8) = 675 / 19.6 ≈ 34.4 m.
(b) Time of flight T = 2 v₀ᵧ / g = 2 × 25.98 / 9.8 ≈ 5.30 s.
(c) Range R = v₀ₓ × T = 15 × 5.30 ≈ 79.5 m.
✅ Answers: 34.4 m, 5.30 s, 79.5 m
Q17 (calculation)
A ball is thrown horizontally from a height of 20 m with a speed of 10 m/s. Find the time to hit the ground and the horizontal distance traveled. (g = 9.8 m/s²)
Step‑by‑step:
Time to fall: t = √(2h / g) = √(40 / 9.8) ≈ √4.082 ≈ 2.02 s.
Horizontal distance: x = vₓ × t = 10 × 2.02 ≈ 20.2 m.
✅ Answers: 2.02 s, 20.2 m
Q18 (calculation)
A car of mass 1200 kg goes around a curve of radius 50 m at 15 m/s. Find the centripetal acceleration and the centripetal force.
Step‑by‑step:
a_c = v² / r = 15² / 50 = 225 / 50 = 4.5 m/s².
F_c = m a_c = 1200 × 4.5 = 5400 N.
✅ Answers: 4.5 m/s², 5400 N
Q19 (calculation)
A projectile lands after 5 s and its vertical displacement is zero (launch and landing at same height). Find its initial vertical speed. (g = 9.8 m/s²)
Step‑by‑step:
Time of flight T = 2 v₀ᵧ / g → v₀ᵧ = (g T) / 2 = (9.8 × 5) / 2 = 24.5 m/s.
✅ Answer: 24.5 m/s
Q20 (calculation)
A satellite orbits Earth at a radius of 7000 km. Earth’s mass M = 5.97×10²⁴ kg, G = 6.67×10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg². Find its orbital speed.
Step‑by‑step:
v = √(GM / r) = √(6.67×10⁻¹¹ × 5.97×10²⁴ / 7×10⁶) = √(3.98×10¹⁴ / 7×10⁶) = √(5.686×10⁷) ≈ 7540 m/s = 7.54 km/s.
✅ Answer: about 7.54 km/s
Q21 (calculation)
A projectile launched at 40 m/s at 30° from the horizontal. Find its velocity components at t = 2 s. (g = 9.8 m/s²)
Step‑by‑step:
v₀ₓ = 40 cos30° = 40 × 0.8660 = 34.64 m/s (constant).
v₀ᵧ = 40 sin30° = 40 × 0.5 = 20 m/s.
vᵧ(t) = v₀ᵧ – g t = 20 – 9.8×2 = 20 – 19.6 = 0.4 m/s.
✅ Answer: vₓ = 34.64 m/s, vᵧ = 0.4 m/s
Q22 (calculation)
A ball is thrown with 20 m/s at 45°. Find its speed at half the maximum height. (g = 9.8 m/s²)
Step‑by‑step:
v₀ = 20 m/s, θ = 45° → v₀ₓ = 20 cos45° = 14.14 m/s, v₀ᵧ = 14.14 m/s.
Max height H = v₀ᵧ² / (2g) = 200 / 19.6 ≈ 10.2 m.
Half height h = H/2 = 5.1 m.
vᵧ² = v₀ᵧ² – 2g h = 200 – 2×9.8×5.1 = 200 – 99.96 = 100.04 → vᵧ ≈ 10.0 m/s.
Speed = √(vₓ² + vᵧ²) = √(14.14² + 10²) = √(200 + 100) = √300 ≈ 17.32 m/s.
✅ Answer: about 17.3 m/s
Q23 (calculation)
A stone is thrown across a river 50 m wide with a speed of 10 m/s. What minimum launch angle is needed to just cross the river? (Assume launch and landing at same height, g = 9.8 m/s²)
Step‑by‑step:
Range R = (v₀² sin 2θ) / g = 50 m → sin 2θ = (R g) / v₀² = (50 × 9.8) / 100 = 490 / 100 = 4.9. But sin 2θ cannot exceed 1. Therefore impossible at 10 m/s; need higher speed. Actually for given v₀, maximum range is v₀²/g = 100/9.8 ≈ 10.2 m. So even at 45°, range is only ~10.2 m, far less than 50 m. So with v₀ = 10 m/s, it's impossible. The problem is a trick: minimum angle does not exist because the stone cannot cross. So the answer: Not possible with v₀ = 10 m/s; required v₀ must be at least √(gR) = √(9.8×50) ≈ 22.1 m/s.
Q24 (calculation)
A car takes a banked curve of radius 60 m with banking angle 20°. If friction is negligible, find the maximum safe speed. (g = 9.8 m/s²)
Step‑by‑step:
For a banked curve with no friction, the required speed is v = √(r g tanθ) = √(60 × 9.8 × tan20°). tan20° ≈ 0.3640.
v = √(60 × 9.8 × 0.364) = √(60 × 3.5672) = √214.03 ≈ 14.63 m/s.
✅ Answer: about 14.6 m/s
Q25 (calculation)
A projectile is launched at 25 m/s at 45° onto a slope inclined at 30° upward from the horizontal. Find the distance along the slope from launch point to impact. (g = 9.8 m/s²)
Step‑by‑step:
This is an advanced problem requiring parametric equations and solving for intersection with the slope. Let the slope have equation y = x tan30° = 0.5774 x.
Projectile: x = v₀ cosθ t = 25×0.7071 t = 17.678 t; y = v₀ sinθ t – ½ g t² = 17.678 t – 4.9 t².
Set y = 0.5774 x → 17.678 t – 4.9 t² = 0.5774 × 17.678 t = 10.207 t.
Rearr: 17.678 t – 4.9 t² – 10.207 t = 0 → 7.471 t – 4.9 t² = 0 → t (7.471 – 4.9 t) = 0 → t = 0 (launch) or t = 7.471 / 4.9 ≈ 1.524 s.
Then x = 17.678 × 1.524 ≈ 26.94 m. Distance along slope = x / cos30° = 26.94 / 0.8660 ≈ 31.1 m.
✅ Answer: about 31.1 m
Q26 (calculation)
A particle moves in a circle of radius 2 m with constant angular velocity ω = 5 rad/s. Find its centripetal acceleration.
Step‑by‑step:
a_c = r ω² = 2 × (5)² = 2 × 25 = 50 m/s².
✅ Answer: 50 m/s²
Q27 (calculation)
A cannon fires at 100 m/s at 60°. The target is 50 m above the cannon. Find the horizontal distance to the target. (g = 9.8 m/s²)
Step‑by‑step:
v₀ₓ = 100 cos60° = 50 m/s, v₀ᵧ = 100 sin60° = 86.6 m/s.
Equation: y = v₀ᵧ t – ½ g t² = 86.6 t – 4.9 t² = 50.
Rearr: 4.9 t² – 86.6 t + 50 = 0. Solve quadratic: t = [86.6 ± √(86.6² – 4×4.9×50)] / (9.8).
Discriminant: 7500 – 980 = 6520, √6520 ≈ 80.75.
t = (86.6 – 80.75)/9.8 ≈ 0.597 s (other root larger).
Then x = v₀ₓ t = 50 × 0.597 ≈ 29.85 m.
✅ Answer: about 29.9 m
Q28 (calculation)
A plane flying horizontally at 60 m/s drops a package from 200 m altitude. Find the impact point horizontally from the release point. (g = 9.8 m/s²)
Step‑by‑step:
Time to fall: t = √(2h/g) = √(400/9.8) ≈ √40.82 ≈ 6.39 s.
Horizontal distance: x = vₓ t = 60 × 6.39 ≈ 383.4 m.
✅ Answer: about 383 m
Q29 (calculation)
A cyclist goes around a turn of radius 30 m at speed 10 m/s. The coefficient of friction between tyres and road is 0.3. Can they safely turn without skidding? (g = 9.8 m/s²)
Step‑by‑step:
Required centripetal force (per kg) = v² / r = 100 / 30 ≈ 3.33 N/kg.
Maximum friction per kg = μ g = 0.3 × 9.8 = 2.94 N/kg.
Since 3.33 > 2.94, the required force exceeds maximum friction → they will skid. Not safe.
✅ Answer: No, they cannot turn safely.
Q30 (conceptual)
Explain why 2D motion problems require vector decomposition.
Answer:
In two dimensions, forces and velocities act in different directions. Breaking them into perpendicular components (usually horizontal and vertical) allows us to apply Newton’s laws independently in each direction. This simplifies calculations and enables accurate predictions of motion, especially when forces are not aligned.
