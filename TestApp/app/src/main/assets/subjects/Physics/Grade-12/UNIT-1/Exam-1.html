📘 Physics in the real world

Physics is not just a classroom subject – its principles are applied everywhere: from engineering and medicine to transportation and energy production. Mechanics helps design safe structures and vehicles; electromagnetism powers motors and MRI machines; optics enables communication and laser cutting; nuclear physics generates electricity. Understanding these applications shows how physics shapes modern life and technology.

📌 REMEMBER: Real‑world problems often combine multiple physics concepts – energy, forces, waves, and electricity work together.
✨ WOW: The same physics that explains why a roller coaster loops also helps design earthquake‑resistant buildings!
Q1 (conceptual)
Explain how the principles of mechanics are applied in designing roller coasters.
Answer:
Energy conservation: potential energy converts to kinetic energy, determining speed. Centripetal force is needed in loops to maintain forces on riders. Friction is minimized but controlled to ensure a smooth, safe ride.
Q2 (conceptual)
How does projectile motion help engineers in ballistics?
Answer:
Projectile motion equations predict trajectory, maximum height, and range. Engineers adjust launch angle and initial velocity to achieve precision targeting.
Q3 (conceptual)
Why do bridges use trusses instead of solid beams?
Answer:
Trusses distribute forces efficiently using tension and compression members, reducing material while preventing bending. This makes bridges both strong and lightweight.
Q4 (conceptual)
Explain why aerodynamics is critical in car and airplane design.
Answer:
Aerodynamics reduces drag, increasing fuel efficiency. For airplanes, shape creates lift via Bernoulli’s principle. Stability and control are also achieved through careful design.
Q5 (conceptual)
How are principles of electricity used in MRI machines?
Answer:
Strong magnetic fields align protons in the body. Radio waves (electric currents) disturb them; as they relax, they emit signals that are detected and converted into detailed images.
Q6 (conceptual)
Explain why conservation of momentum is crucial in collision safety design.
Answer:
Airbags and crumple zones increase the time over which momentum changes, reducing the average force (F = Δp/Δt). This protects passengers by spreading the impact.
Q7 (conceptual)
How is heat transfer principle used in cooking appliances?
Answer:
Conduction (pans on a stove), convection (oven fans circulate hot air), and radiation (broilers) ensure efficient energy delivery. Material choice affects heat distribution.
Q8 (conceptual)
Why is knowledge of fluid mechanics important for blood flow analysis?
Answer:
Viscosity and pressure gradients determine flow rates. Turbulence analysis helps diagnose cardiovascular risks like aneurysms or blockages.
Q9 (conceptual)
How is nuclear physics applied in power generation?
Answer:
Fission of heavy nuclei releases heat, which boils water to steam, driving turbines that generate electricity. Chain reactions are controlled to extract energy safely.
Q10 (conceptual)
Explain why optical physics is important in fiber optic communication.
Answer:
Total internal reflection confines light within the fiber, allowing data transmission over long distances with minimal loss. High bandwidth makes it ideal for internet.
Q11 (conceptual)
How do principles of electromagnetism improve electric motors?
Answer:
Force on a current‑carrying conductor in a magnetic field produces torque, causing rotation. Optimizing magnetic field design and current control increases efficiency.
Q12 (conceptual)
Why is Newton’s law applied in spacecraft orbit calculations?
Answer:
Gravitational force provides the centripetal force needed for circular orbits. Newton’s law of gravitation allows precise prediction of orbit paths and fuel requirements.
Q13 (conceptual)
Explain why thermodynamics is critical in car engine design.
Answer:
Engines convert heat from fuel combustion into mechanical work. Thermodynamic cycles (e.g., Otto cycle) determine efficiency, and engineers minimize heat loss to improve fuel economy.
Q14 (conceptual)
How is physics used in earthquake‑resistant building design?
Answer:
Damping systems absorb vibrational energy. Resonance is avoided by tuning building frequencies away from earthquake frequencies. Flexible materials and force distribution reduce damage.
Q15 (conceptual)
Explain why optics and photonics are applied in laser cutting tools.
Answer:
Lasers concentrate light into a tiny, intense beam. Photonics controls the beam’s focus and intensity, enabling precise cutting with minimal material waste.
Q16 (application)
A roller coaster car of mass 500 kg starts from rest at height 40 m. Find its speed at the bottom (ignore friction). (g = 9.8 m/s²)
Step‑by‑step:
Conservation of energy: m g h = ½ m v² → v = √(2 g h) = √(2 × 9.8 × 40) = √(784) = 28 m/s.
✅ Answer: 28 m/s
Q17 (application)
A car travels at 30 m/s around a curve of radius 50 m. Find the centripetal acceleration and the force if the mass is 1000 kg.
Step‑by‑step:
a_c = v² / r = (30)² / 50 = 900 / 50 = 18 m/s².
Force F = m a_c = 1000 × 18 = 18,000 N.
✅ Answer: 18 m/s², 18,000 N
Q18 (application)
Water flows through a pipe of radius 0.05 m at 2 m/s. Find the volumetric flow rate.
Step‑by‑step:
Q = A v = π r² v = 3.1416 × (0.05)² × 2 = 3.1416 × 0.0025 × 2 = 0.0157 m³/s.
✅ Answer: about 0.0157 m³/s
Q19 (application)
A motor delivers 10 kW of power to raise a 500 kg elevator 20 m in 10 s. Find the efficiency. (g = 9.8 m/s²)
Step‑by‑step:
Useful work = m g h = 500 × 9.8 × 20 = 98,000 J.
Input energy = power × time = 10,000 W × 10 s = 100,000 J.
Efficiency = (useful / input) × 100 = (98,000 / 100,000) × 100 = 98%.
✅ Answer: 98%
Q20 (application)
A laser emits 2 mW of power. Each photon has energy 3.2×10⁻¹⁹ J. Find the number of photons emitted per second.
Step‑by‑step:
Power = 2×10⁻³ J/s.
Number of photons per second = power / energy per photon = 2×10⁻³ / 3.2×10⁻¹⁹ ≈ 6.25×10¹⁵ photons/s.
✅ Answer: 6.25×10¹⁵ photons/s
Q21 (application)
A spacecraft of mass 1000 kg orbits Earth at a radius of 6.7×10⁶ m. Find its orbital speed (g = 9.8 m/s², GM = g R² with R = 6.4×10⁶ m).
Step‑by‑step:
GM = g R² = 9.8 × (6.4×10⁶)² ≈ 9.8 × 4.096×10¹³ = 4.014×10¹⁴ m³/s².
Orbital speed v = √(GM / r) = √(4.014×10¹⁴ / 6.7×10⁶) = √(5.99×10⁷) ≈ 7740 m/s.
✅ Answer: about 7740 m/s
Q22 (application)
A patient of mass 70 kg absorbs a radiation dose of 2 Gy. Find the energy absorbed.
Step‑by‑step:
Energy = dose × mass = 2 Gy × 70 kg = 140 J (since 1 Gy = 1 J/kg).
✅ Answer: 140 J
Q23 (application)
A light beam passes from water (n = 1.33) to air. Find the critical angle.
Step‑by‑step:
sin θ_c = n₂ / n₁ = 1 / 1.33 ≈ 0.7519.
θ_c = sin⁻¹(0.7519) ≈ 48.75°.
✅ Answer: about 48.8°
Q24 (application)
An electric car operates at 360 V and draws 120 A. Find the power.
Step‑by‑step:
Power = V I = 360 × 120 = 43,200 W = 43.2 kW.
✅ Answer: 43.2 kW
Q25 (application)
A bridge cable can withstand 2×10⁶ N. With a safety factor of 2, what is the maximum allowed load?
Step‑by‑step:
Max allowed load = breaking strength / safety factor = 2×10⁶ / 2 = 1×10⁶ N.
✅ Answer: 1×10⁶ N
Q26 (application)
In an MRI scanner, the magnetic field is 3 T. The gyromagnetic ratio for protons is γ = 2.675×10⁸ rad/(s·T). Find the precession frequency in Hz.
Step‑by‑step:
Angular frequency ω = γ B = 2.675×10⁸ × 3 = 8.025×10⁸ rad/s.
Frequency f = ω / (2π) = 8.025×10⁸ / 6.283 ≈ 1.277×10⁸ Hz.
✅ Answer: about 1.28×10⁸ Hz
Q27 (application)
An airplane wing generates a lift force of 5×10⁵ N at a speed of 70 m/s. Air density ρ = 1.225 kg/m³, lift coefficient C_L = 1.2. Find the wing area using lift formula: F = ½ ρ v² C_L A.
Step‑by‑step:
Rearrange: A = F / (½ ρ v² C_L) = 5×10⁵ / (0.5 × 1.225 × 70² × 1.2) = 5×10⁵ / (0.5 × 1.225 × 4900 × 1.2).
0.5 × 1.225 = 0.6125; 0.6125 × 4900 = 3001.25; ×1.2 = 3601.5.
A = 5×10⁵ / 3601.5 ≈ 138.8 m².
✅ Answer: about 139 m²
Q28 (application)
A satellite of mass 2000 kg orbits at a height of 500 km above Earth’s surface. Earth’s radius R = 6.37×10⁶ m, g = 9.8 m/s². Find the gravitational potential energy change relative to the surface. (Use ΔU = m g h approximation? But for precise: U = –GMm/r. We need difference.)
Step‑by‑step:
GM = g R² = 9.8 × (6.37×10⁶)² = 9.8 × 4.057×10¹³ = 3.976×10¹⁴ m³/s².
r_surface = R = 6.37×10⁶ m; r_orbit = R + h = 6.37×10⁶ + 0.5×10⁶ = 6.87×10⁶ m.
U_surface = –GMm / R = –3.976×10¹⁴ × 2000 / 6.37×10⁶ = –7.952×10¹⁷ / 6.37×10⁶ ≈ –1.248×10¹¹ J.
U_orbit = –GMm / r_orbit = –3.976×10¹⁴ × 2000 / 6.87×10⁶ = –7.952×10¹⁷ / 6.87×10⁶ ≈ –1.158×10¹¹ J.
Change ΔU = U_orbit – U_surface = (–1.158 + 1.248)×10¹¹ = 0.090×10¹¹ = 9.0×10⁹ J.
✅ Answer: about 9.0×10⁹ J (positive, meaning energy added)
Q29 (application)
A car experiences an air resistance force of 1000 N while moving 20 m. Find the work done against drag.
Step‑by‑step:
Work = force × distance = 1000 × 20 = 20,000 J.
✅ Answer: 20,000 J
Q30 (conceptual)
Explain why advanced physics applications require multi‑disciplinary understanding.
Answer:
Real‑world systems combine mechanics, electromagnetism, thermodynamics, nuclear physics, optics, and fluid dynamics. Design, safety, and efficiency depend on interlinked principles – for example, a car engine uses thermodynamics, materials science, and fluid flow. Engineers must integrate multiple physics areas to innovate.
