📘 Electrostatics and circuits – key ideas

Electrostatics deals with stationary charges: Coulomb's law (F = k q₁ q₂ / r²), electric field (E = F/q), potential (V = kq/r), and capacitance (C = Q/V). Electric circuits involve current (I = dQ/dt), resistance (R = ρL/A), Ohm's law (V = IR), series/parallel combinations, and Kirchhoff's laws. Energy in capacitors (U = ½CV²) and resistors (P = I²R) are essential. Understanding these concepts is fundamental to electronics, power systems, and everyday devices.

📌 REMEMBER: For point charges, forces add as vectors. In equilibrium, conductors have zero internal field. For resistors in series: Req = R₁ + R₂ + …; in parallel: 1/Req = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + …
✨ WOW: The same principles that make a lightning bolt also make your phone's touchscreen work!
Q1 (conceptual)
Explain why electric charge is quantized.
Answer:
Charges exist in multiples of elementary charge e = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C. No fractional charges exist naturally. Basis: electron & proton carry ±e.
Q2 (conceptual)
Why are like charges repelled and opposite charges attracted?
Answer:
Coulomb’s law: F = k q₁ q₂ / r². Force direction: same charges → repel, opposite → attract.
Q3 (conceptual)
Explain why conductors allow charges to move freely but insulators do not.
Answer:
Conductors have free electrons that can move under an electric field. Insulators have electrons tightly bound to atoms → minimal conduction.
Q4 (conceptual)
Why is Coulomb’s law valid only for point charges or spherically symmetric charge distributions?
Answer:
The law assumes distance much greater than charge size. For extended charges, one must integrate over the distribution to find the net force.
Q5 (conceptual)
Explain the principle of superposition of electric forces.
Answer:
The net force on a charge is the vector sum of the individual forces from all other charges. This allows solving multiple-charge systems.
Q6 (conceptual)
Why does electric field inside a conductor in electrostatic equilibrium become zero?
Answer:
Charges redistribute until they cancel any internal field. If there were a field, charges would move; equilibrium means no net motion → E = 0 inside.
Q7 (conceptual)
Explain electric potential and its relation to electric field.
Answer:
Electric potential V is work done per unit charge to move a charge from a reference point to that point. E = –dV/dx (in one dimension) → field points in the direction of decreasing potential.
Q8 (conceptual)
Why is potential difference required to move charges in a circuit?
Answer:
Potential difference provides the energy to push charges through the circuit. Without V, charges would not have a net motion (no current).
Q9 (conceptual)
Explain why capacitors store energy.
Answer:
Separating opposite charges creates an electric field. The energy stored in that field is U = ½ C V².
Q10 (conceptual)
Why does current exist in metals?
Answer:
Free electrons move under an applied electric field. Their drift velocity produces current: I = n e v_d A.
Q11 (conceptual)
Explain Ohm’s law.
Answer:
V = I R. The current through a conductor is proportional to the voltage across it. Resistance R depends on material, length, cross‑section, and temperature.
Q12 (conceptual)
Why does resistance of a wire increase with temperature?
Answer:
Thermal vibrations of lattice atoms increase, causing more collisions for electrons → higher resistivity → higher resistance.
Q13 (conceptual)
Explain the difference between series and parallel circuits.
Answer:
Series: same current through all components, total resistance = sum of individual resistances. Parallel: same voltage across each branch, total resistance found from reciprocals.
Q14 (conceptual)
Why is the electric field stronger near point charges?
Answer:
E = k q / r², so field strength is inversely proportional to the square of distance. Closer to the charge → smaller r → larger E.
Q15 (conceptual)
Explain why charges reside on the surface of conductors.
Answer:
Electrostatic repulsion forces charges to move as far apart as possible, which is on the surface. Inside the conductor, the field is zero.
Q16 (conceptual)
Why is grounding important in circuits?
Answer:
Grounding provides a low‑resistance path to Earth, preventing excess charge buildup and reducing shock risk. It also serves as a common reference point (0 V).
Q17 (conceptual)
Explain why a capacitor charges rapidly at first and then slowly.
Answer:
Initially, the voltage across the capacitor is zero, so the voltage across the resistor is large → high current. As charge builds up, the capacitor voltage increases, reducing the net voltage and current → slower charging.
Q18 (conceptual)
Why does current split in parallel branches?
Answer:
Current divides inversely proportional to the resistance of each branch. Total current equals the sum of branch currents (Kirchhoff’s current law).
Q19 (conceptual)
Explain why energy is dissipated in resistors.
Answer:
Electrons collide with lattice atoms, transferring kinetic energy as heat. Power dissipated = I² R = V I = V² / R.
Q20 (conceptual)
Why is the concept of electric potential energy useful?
Answer:
Potential energy (scalar) simplifies calculations; energy differences drive charges and determine work done by electric fields.
Q21 (conceptual)
Explain the principle of a voltmeter.
Answer:
A voltmeter measures potential difference between two points. It is connected in parallel and has very high resistance to avoid drawing significant current that would alter the circuit.
Q22 (conceptual)
Why is an ammeter connected in series?
Answer:
An ammeter measures the current flowing through a branch. Connecting it in series ensures the entire current passes through it. Its resistance is very low to minimize voltage drop.
Q23 (conceptual)
Explain Kirchhoff’s laws.
Answer:
Kirchhoff’s current law (KCL): sum of currents entering a junction equals sum leaving. Kirchhoff’s voltage law (KVL): sum of voltage gains and drops around any closed loop is zero.
Q24 (conceptual)
Why are high‑resistance wires used in voltmeters?
Answer:
High resistance ensures that the voltmeter draws negligible current from the circuit, so the measured voltage is very close to the actual potential difference without loading the circuit.
Q25 (conceptual)
Why does resistance depend on wire length and area?
Answer:
R = ρ L / A. Longer wire → more collisions → higher R. Larger cross‑sectional area → more paths for electrons → lower R.
Q26 (conceptual)
Explain why power dissipated in a resistor is given by P = I V = I² R = V² / R.
Answer:
Joule heating: energy converted to heat per unit time. From Ohm’s law (V = I R), all three forms are equivalent.
Q27 (conceptual)
Why is a capacitor used in smoothing DC after rectification?
Answer:
The capacitor charges when the voltage rises and discharges when it dips, reducing the ripple and providing a smoother DC output.
Q28 (conceptual)
Explain the role of a dielectric in a capacitor.
Answer:
A dielectric increases capacitance (C = κ C₀) by reducing the effective electric field for the same charge, allowing more energy storage. It also provides mechanical spacing and insulation.
Q29 (conceptual)
Why does a resistor oppose current flow?
Answer:
Electrons moving through the resistor collide with the lattice atoms, losing energy and causing resistance to flow.
Q30 (conceptual)
Explain why electrostatics is important in electronics and real‑world devices.
Answer:
Electric fields, potential, and charge control circuits, sensors, capacitors, insulation, and high‑voltage systems. Understanding these is essential for designing and troubleshooting electronic devices.
Q31 (calculation)
Two point charges q₁ = 5 μC and q₂ = –3 μC are separated by 2 m. Find the force between them (k = 9×10⁹ N·m²/C²).
Step‑by‑step:
F = k |q₁ q₂| / r² = 9×10⁹ × (5×10⁻⁶ × 3×10⁻⁶) / 4 = 9×10⁹ × 15×10⁻¹² / 4 = (135×10⁻³) / 4 = 0.03375 N ≈ 33.8 mN (attractive).
✅ Answer: 33.8 mN
Q32 (calculation)
A charge of 2 μC is placed in an electric field of 1000 N/C. Find the force on the charge.
Step‑by‑step:
F = q E = 2×10⁻⁶ × 1000 = 0.002 N = 2 mN.
✅ Answer: 2 mN
Q33 (calculation)
A capacitor of 10 μF is charged to 12 V. Find the stored energy.
Step‑by‑step:
U = ½ C V² = 0.5 × 10×10⁻⁶ × 144 = 0.5 × 1.44×10⁻³ = 7.2×10⁻⁴ J = 0.72 mJ.
✅ Answer: 0.72 mJ
Q34 (calculation)
Two resistors, 100 Ω and 200 Ω, are in series with a 12 V battery. Find the total current.
Step‑by‑step:
R_total = 100 + 200 = 300 Ω. I = V / R_total = 12 / 300 = 0.04 A = 40 mA.
✅ Answer: 40 mA
Q35 (calculation)
Two resistors, 100 Ω and 200 Ω, are in parallel with a 12 V battery. Find the total current.
Step‑by‑step:
1/R = 1/100 + 1/200 = 3/200 → R = 200/3 ≈ 66.67 Ω. I = V / R = 12 / 66.67 ≈ 0.18 A.
✅ Answer: 0.18 A
Q36 (calculation)
Two equal charges q = 4 μC are placed 3 m apart. Find the electric field at the midpoint between them.
Step‑by‑step:
Each charge produces field E₁ = E₂ = k q / (1.5)² = 9×10⁹ × 4×10⁻⁶ / 2.25 = 36×10³ / 2.25 = 16×10³ N/C. At the midpoint, the fields are equal and opposite (since both charges have same sign), so they cancel. Net E = 0.
✅ Answer: 0
Q37 (calculation)
A voltage divider uses R₁ = 2 kΩ and R₂ = 3 kΩ with input 12 V. Find the output voltage across R₂.
Step‑by‑step:
V_out = V_in × R₂ / (R₁ + R₂) = 12 × 3000 / 5000 = 12 × 0.6 = 7.2 V.
✅ Answer: 7.2 V
Q38 (calculation)
A resistor of 50 Ω has a voltage of 10 V across it. Find the current.
Step‑by‑step:
I = V / R = 10 / 50 = 0.2 A = 200 mA.
✅ Answer: 200 mA
Q39 (calculation)
A 100 μF capacitor is connected to a 12 V battery. Find the charge stored.
Step‑by‑step:
Q = C V = 100×10⁻⁶ × 12 = 1.2×10⁻³ C = 1.2 mC.
✅ Answer: 1.2 mC
Q40 (calculation)
A 50 Ω resistor carries 0.2 A. Find the power dissipated.
Step‑by‑step:
P = I² R = (0.2)² × 50 = 0.04 × 50 = 2 W.
✅ Answer: 2 W
Q41 (calculation)
Two charges +3 μC and –2 μC are 1 m apart. Find the electric potential at the midpoint (k = 9×10⁹).
Step‑by‑step:
r₁ = r₂ = 0.5 m. V = k (q₁/r₁ + q₂/r₂) = 9×10⁹ × (3×10⁻⁶ / 0.5 + (–2×10⁻⁶) / 0.5) = 9×10⁹ × (6×10⁻⁶ – 4×10⁻⁶) = 9×10⁹ × 2×10⁻⁶ = 18×10³ V = 18 kV.
✅ Answer: 18 kV
Q42 (calculation)
A capacitor of 20 μF is charged to 5 V. Find the stored energy.
Step‑by‑step:
U = ½ C V² = 0.5 × 20×10⁻⁶ × 25 = 0.5 × 500×10⁻⁶ = 250×10⁻⁶ J = 0.25 mJ.
✅ Answer: 0.25 mJ
Q43 (calculation)
Two parallel resistors, 100 Ω and 200 Ω, are connected to a 12 V battery. Find the current in each branch.
Step‑by‑step:
I₁ = V / R₁ = 12 / 100 = 0.12 A. I₂ = 12 / 200 = 0.06 A.
✅ Answer: 0.12 A, 0.06 A
Q44 (calculation)
A capacitor of 50 μF is connected to 10 V. Find the charge stored.
Step‑by‑step:
Q = C V = 50×10⁻⁶ × 10 = 500×10⁻⁶ C = 0.5 mC.
✅ Answer: 0.5 mC
Q45 (calculation)
A point charge of 5 μC is placed 2 m away. Find the electric field at that point.
Step‑by‑step:
E = k q / r² = 9×10⁹ × 5×10⁻⁶ / 4 = 45×10³ / 4 = 11,250 N/C.
✅ Answer: 11,250 N/C
Q46 (calculation)
A series circuit has a 100 Ω resistor and a 9 V battery. Find the power dissipated.
Step‑by‑step:
P = V² / R = 81 / 100 = 0.81 W.
✅ Answer: 0.81 W
Q47 (calculation)
A parallel circuit has a 50 Ω resistor and a 12 V battery. Find the power dissipated.
Step‑by‑step:
P = V² / R = 144 / 50 = 2.88 W.
✅ Answer: 2.88 W
Q48 (calculation)
An RC circuit has R = 1 kΩ and C = 10 μF. Find the time constant.
Step‑by‑step:
τ = R C = 1000 × 10×10⁻⁶ = 0.01 s = 10 ms.
✅ Answer: 10 ms
Q49 (calculation)
A resistor of 10 Ω carries a current of 2 A. Find the voltage across it.

V = I R = 2 × 10 = 20 V.
✅ Answer: 20 V
Q50 (calculation)
Two point charges each of 4 μC are separated by 2 m. Find the force between them.

F = k q² / r² = 9×10⁹ × (4×10⁻⁶)² / 4 = 9×10⁹ × 16×10⁻¹² / 4 = 144×10⁻³ / 4 = 0.036 N = 36 mN (repulsive).
✅ Answer: 36 mN

✅ Answer: 5 V, 10 V
