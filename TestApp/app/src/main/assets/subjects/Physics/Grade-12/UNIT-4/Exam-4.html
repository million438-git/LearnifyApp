📘 Electromagnetism – key ideas

Electromagnetism unites electricity and magnetism. Moving charges produce magnetic fields; changing magnetic fields induce electric fields. Key laws: Biot‑Savart, Ampère’s, Faraday’s, Lenz’s. Motors, generators, transformers, and all electronics rely on these principles. Understanding vectors, flux, and induction is essential.

📌 REMEMBER: F = q v B sinθ, F = I L B sinθ, ε = –N dΦ/dt, Φ = B A cosθ, B_solenoid = μ₀ n I.
✨ WOW: The same principles that light up cities also let us see inside the human body with MRI!
Q1 (conceptual)
Explain why a current‑carrying conductor produces a magnetic field.
Answer:
Moving electric charges generate a magnetic field. Field lines are circular around the conductor. Right‑hand rule gives direction of field.
Q2 (conceptual)
Why does a solenoid behave like a bar magnet?
Answer:
Circular loops of current combine to produce a uniform magnetic field along the axis. One end acts as north, the other as south – like a bar magnet.
Q3 (conceptual)
Explain why magnetic field lines never cross.
Answer:
At any point, magnetic field has a unique direction. Crossing would imply two directions at the same point – impossible.
Q4 (conceptual)
Why is Earth’s magnetic field important for navigation?
Answer:
A compass needle aligns with the magnetic field, providing direction (north‑south).
Q5 (conceptual)
Why does a moving charge experience a force in a magnetic field?
Answer:
Lorentz force: F = q (v × B). The force is perpendicular to both velocity and field.
Q6 (conceptual)
Explain why the force on a current‑carrying wire depends on wire orientation.
Answer:
F = I L B sinθ, where θ is the angle between current and field. Force is maximum when perpendicular, zero when parallel.
Q7 (conceptual)
Why does changing magnetic flux induce an emf?
Answer:
Faraday’s law: ε = –dΦ/dt. A time‑varying flux creates an induced electric field, producing emf.
Q8 (conceptual)
Explain Lenz’s Law in one sentence.
Answer:
Induced current opposes the change in magnetic flux that produced it – conserving energy.
Q9 (conceptual)
Why is alternating current (AC) preferred for power distribution?
Answer:
AC voltage can be easily stepped up or down with transformers, reducing power loss in transmission lines.
Q10 (conceptual)
Explain why transformers only work with AC.
Answer:
Transformers rely on changing magnetic flux to induce emf. DC produces constant flux → no induced emf.
Q11 (conceptual)
Why do parallel currents attract and antiparallel currents repel?
Answer:
Magnetic fields of each current interact. Parallel currents have fields that reinforce between them → attraction. Antiparallel → opposite → repulsion.
Q12 (conceptual)
Explain why magnetic field inside a long solenoid is uniform.
Answer:
Superposition of fields from all loops cancels outside and adds inside, producing nearly constant field parallel to axis.
Q13 (conceptual)
Why is magnetic flux quantized in superconducting loops?
Answer:
Flux takes integer multiples of Φ₀ = h/2e due to quantum mechanical requirement of wavefunction single‑valuedness.
Q14 (conceptual)
Explain why a cyclotron accelerates particles in a circular path.
Answer:
Magnetic field provides centripetal force. Electric field accelerates particles each half‑circle, increasing energy and radius.
Q15 (conceptual)
Why is induced emf negative in Faraday’s Law?
Answer:
The negative sign expresses Lenz’s law – the induced emf opposes the change in flux, ensuring energy conservation.
Q16 (conceptual)
Explain why moving a conductor in a magnetic field produces electricity.
Answer:
The conductor cuts magnetic field lines, changing flux through its circuit and inducing an emf (electromagnetic induction).
Q17 (conceptual)
Why does magnetic force do no work on a moving charge?
Answer:
The force is always perpendicular to velocity, so it changes direction but not speed → no work (W = F·d = 0).
Q18 (conceptual)
Explain why transformers have copper losses.
Answer:
Resistance of windings causes I²R heating (copper losses), reducing efficiency.
Q19 (conceptual)
Why does an AC motor rotate continuously?
Answer:
Alternating current reverses direction, causing magnetic poles to switch, so rotor is continuously pulled around.
Q20 (conceptual)
Explain why magnetic monopoles have never been observed.
Answer:
All magnetic phenomena arise from dipoles (north‑south). Isolated north or south poles (monopoles) have not been found in nature.
Q21 (conceptual)
Why does increasing turns in a coil increase induced emf?
Answer:
ε = –N dΦ/dt → more turns (N) multiply the flux linkage, so induced emf is larger.
Q22 (conceptual)
Explain why the Earth’s magnetic field is decreasing slowly.
Answer:
Changes in core fluid motion (dynamo effect) cause field fluctuations, and currently the dipole moment is weakening.
Q23 (conceptual)
Why does eddy current cause energy loss?
Answer:
Changing magnetic fields induce circulating currents (eddy currents) in conductors, which dissipate energy as heat.
Q24 (conceptual)
Explain why magnetic materials saturate at high field.
Answer:
All magnetic domains align; further increase in applied field cannot increase magnetization – saturation.
Q25 (conceptual)
Why is soft iron used in electromagnets?
Answer:
High permeability and low hysteresis (easily magnetized and demagnetized), ideal for temporary magnets.
Q26 (conceptual)
Why do inductors resist change in current?
Answer:
Self‑induced emf opposes any change in current (Lenz’s law), acting like electrical inertia.
Q27 (conceptual)
Explain why a moving coil galvanometer measures current.
Answer:
Current through a coil in a magnetic field produces torque, causing deflection proportional to current.
Q28 (conceptual)
Why is an AC generator preferred over DC generator for large‑scale generation?
Answer:
AC can be easily stepped up to high voltage for efficient transmission; DC is harder to transform.
Q29 (conceptual)
Explain why magnetic flux linkage depends on coil orientation.
Answer:
Φ = B·A·cosθ; angle between field and area vector changes the effective flux through the coil.
Q30 (conceptual)
Why is electromagnetism fundamental in modern technology?
Answer:
Powers motors, generators, transformers, wireless communication, electronics, and medical imaging – underpins nearly all technology.
Q31 (calculation)
A 2 m long wire carries 5 A perpendicular to a 0.2 T magnetic field. Find the force on the wire.
Step‑by‑step:
F = I L B = 5 × 2 × 0.2 = 2 N.
✅ Answer: 2 N
Q32 (calculation)
A current of 3 A flows in a 0.5 m wire at 60° to a 0.4 T field. Find the force.
Step‑by‑step:
F = I L B sinθ = 3 × 0.5 × 0.4 × sin60° = 3 × 0.5 × 0.4 × 0.866 ≈ 0.52 N.
✅ Answer: about 0.52 N
Q33 (calculation)
A coil has 50 turns, area 0.01 m², and is placed perpendicular to a 0.5 T field. Find the magnetic flux through the coil.
Step‑by‑step:
Φ = N B A = 50 × 0.5 × 0.01 = 0.25 Wb.
✅ Answer: 0.25 Wb
Q34 (calculation)
Magnetic flux through a 100‑turn coil changes from 0.2 Wb to 0.8 Wb in 0.05 s. Find the average induced emf.
Step‑by‑step:
ε = N ΔΦ/Δt = 100 × (0.8 – 0.2) / 0.05 = 100 × 0.6 / 0.05 = 100 × 12 = 1200 V.
✅ Answer: 1200 V
Q35 (calculation)
A charge of 1 C moves at 2 m/s perpendicular to a 0.3 T field. Find the magnetic force.
Step‑by‑step:
F = q v B = 1 × 2 × 0.3 = 0.6 N.
✅ Answer: 0.6 N
Q36 (calculation)
A solenoid has 1000 turns per metre and carries 2 A. Find the magnetic field inside. (μ₀ = 4π×10⁻⁷)
Step‑by‑step:
B = μ₀ n I = 4π×10⁻⁷ × 1000 × 2 = 8π×10⁻⁴ ≈ 2.51×10⁻³ T.
✅ Answer: 2.51×10⁻³ T
Q37 (calculation)
A coil of area 1 m², 10 turns, initially perpendicular to 0.1 T field, is rotated 90° in 0.02 s. Find average induced emf.
Step‑by‑step:
ΔΦ = B A (cos0° – cos90°) = 0.1×1×(1 – 0) = 0.1 Wb. ε = N ΔΦ/Δt = 10 × 0.1 / 0.02 = 50 V.
✅ Answer: 50 V
Q38 (calculation)
Two long parallel wires 0.5 m apart carry currents 4 A and 6 A in the same direction. Find the force per metre between them. (μ₀ = 4π×10⁻⁷)
Step‑by‑step:
F/L = μ₀ I₁ I₂ / (2π d) = 4π×10⁻⁷ × 4 × 6 / (2π × 0.5) = (4π×10⁻⁷ × 24) / (π) = 96×10⁻⁷ = 9.6×10⁻⁶ N/m.
✅ Answer: 9.6×10⁻⁶ N/m
Q39 (calculation)
An electron (m = 9.11×10⁻³¹ kg, q = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C) moves at 1×10⁶ m/s perpendicular to a 0.01 T field. Find the radius of its circular path.
Step‑by‑step:
r = m v / (q B) = 9.11×10⁻³¹ × 1×10⁶ / (1.6×10⁻¹⁹ × 0.01) = 9.11×10⁻²⁵ / 1.6×10⁻²¹ = 5.69×10⁻⁴ m = 0.569 mm.
✅ Answer: 5.69×10⁻⁴ m

Q45 (calculation)
An electron moves in a uniform magnetic field of 0.2 T at 2×10⁶ m/s perpendicular. Find its centripetal acceleration. (m = 9.11×10⁻³¹ kg, q = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C)
Step‑by‑step:
F = q v B = m a → a = q v B / m = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ × 2×10⁶ × 0.2 / 9.11×10⁻³¹ = (6.4×10⁻¹⁴) / 9.11×10⁻³¹ ≈ 7.02×10¹⁶ m/s².
✅ Answer: 7.02×10¹⁶ m/s²

Q50 (calculation)
A coil of area 0.05 m², N = 100, B = 0.2 T, rotates 60° in 0.01 s. Find average induced emf.
Step‑by‑step:
ΔΦ per turn = B A (cosθ₂ – cosθ₁). Initial θ₁ = 0°, cos0° = 1; final θ₂ = 60°, cos60° = 0.5. ΔΦ = 0.2×0.05×(0.5 – 1) = 0.01×(-0.5) = –0.005 Wb. |ΔΦ| = 0.005 Wb. ε = N |ΔΦ|/Δt = 100 × 0.005 / 0.01 = 50 V.
✅ Answer: 50 V
