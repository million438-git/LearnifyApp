Section 2.1

Motion, Distance, Displacement, Speed, Velocity & Acceleration

Uniformly accelerated motion is motion in a straight line where velocity changes by equal amounts in equal times. Before we can use the kinematic equations, we must be precise about what each quantity means.

ወጥ የተጣደፈ እንቅስቃሴ በቀጥተኛ መስመር ላይ ፍጥነት በእኩል ጊዜ ውስጥ በእኩል መጠን የሚለወጥበት እንቅስቃሴ ነው።

The crucial pairs:
Distance (scalar) = total path length travelled.
Displacement (vector) = straight-line change in position, with direction.
Average speed = distance ÷ time (scalar).
Average velocity = displacement ÷ time (vector).
Average acceleration = change in velocity ÷ time = (v − v₀)/t.
Instantaneous velocity = velocity at a single instant (slope of tangent on s-t graph). ወሳኝ ጥንድ፦ ርቀት (ስካላር) = አጠቃላይ የመንገድ ርዝመት። መፈናቀል (ቬክተር) = ከመነሻ እስከ መድረሻ ቀጥተኛ ለውጥ። አማካይ ፍጥነት = ርቀት ÷ ጊዜ። አማካይ ቬሎሲቲ = መፈናቀል ÷ ጊዜ።

Core formulas

Average velocity: v̄ = s / t   (s = displacement)
Average speed = total distance / t
Average acceleration: a = (v − v₀) / t
Instantaneous velocity = slope of tangent on position-time graph = ds/dt

Worked Examples

Example 2.1.1 — Distance vs displacement on a rectangular track (matric level)

A runner travels around a rectangular track 50 m × 20 m twice and returns to the start. Find distance and displacement.

  1. Perimeter of one lap = 2(50 + 20) = 140 m.
  2. Two laps = 2 × 140 = 280 m distance (scalar).
  3. Displacement: the runner returns to the starting point, so the straight-line change is 0 m.
  4. Insight: Distance is large, displacement is zero — this is exactly why we must never confuse them.
Final: Distance = 280 m; displacement = 0 m.

Example 2.1.2 — Average velocity with multiple legs

A cyclist rides 5.6 m/s for 4.0 s with acceleration 0.60 m/s². Find distance travelled and final velocity.

  1. Given: v₀ = 5.6 m/s, a = 0.60 m/s², t = 4.0 s.
  2. Displacement: s = v₀t + ½at² = 5.6(4.0) + ½(0.60)(16) = 22.4 + 4.8 = 27.2 m.
  3. Final velocity: v = v₀ + at = 5.6 + 0.60(4.0) = 8.0 m/s.
  4. Verify with v² = v₀² + 2as: 64 = 31.36 + 2(0.6)(27.2) = 31.36 + 32.64 = 64 ✓
Final: Distance = 27.2 m; final velocity = 8.0 m/s.

Example 2.1.3 — Average acceleration from velocity change

A car speeds up from 20 m/s to 50 m/s in 6 s. What is the average acceleration?

  1. Given: v₀ = 20 m/s, v = 50 m/s, t = 6 s.
  2. Apply formula: a = (v − v₀)/t = (50 − 20)/6.
  3. Compute: a = 30/6 = 5.0 m/s².
  4. Check direction: a is positive → same direction as velocity (speeding up) ✓.
Final: a = 5.0 m/s².

Common Mistakes

  • Mixing speed and velocity: Speed has no direction. Always state velocity with a direction.
  • Negative acceleration = slowing down? Not necessarily. If velocity is also negative, negative acceleration means speeding up (in the negative direction).
  • Using distance when asked for displacement: They differ whenever the path is not a single straight line.
  • Forgetting units: Always attach m/s² to acceleration, m/s to velocity, m to displacement.

Section 2.2

The Four Kinematic Equations

For constant acceleration, five quantities are linked: displacement s, initial velocity v₀, final velocity v, acceleration a, and time t. Any three knowns let you find the other two.

ለቋሚ ማጣደፍ አምስት መጠኖች ይገናኛሉ፦ መፈናቀል s፣ የመጀመሪያ ፍጥነት v₀፣ የመጨረሻ ፍጥነት v፣ ማጣደፍ a፣ ጊዜ t።

The four equations

① v = v₀ + at   (no s)
② s = v₀t + ½at²   (no v)
③ v² = v₀² + 2as   (no t)
④ s = ((v₀ + v) / 2) · t   (no a)
Choosing the right equation: Look at what is missing from the problem. If time is unknown, use ③. If final velocity is unknown, use ②. If displacement is unknown, use ①. ትክክለኛውን ቀመር መምረጥ፦ በችግሩ ውስጥ የማይታወቀውን ተመልከት። ጊዜ ከማይታወቅ ③ ተጠቀም።

Worked Examples

Example 2.2.1 — Car from rest (matric exam style)

A car starting from rest accelerates uniformly and covers 100 m in 5 s. Find its acceleration.

  1. Given: v₀ = 0, s = 100 m, t = 5 s.
  2. Find: a. The equation with v₀ = 0 is s = ½at².
  3. Rearrange: a = 2s / t² = 2(100) / 25.
  4. Compute: a = 200/25 = 8 m/s².
  5. Verify: v = at = 8 × 5 = 40 m/s; s = ½(8)(25) = 100 m ✓
Final: a = 8 m/s².

Example 2.2.2 — Airplane decelerating

An airplane lands at 70 m/s and decelerates at 1.5 m/s² for 40 s. Find its final velocity.

  1. Given: v₀ = 70 m/s, a = −1.5 m/s², t = 40 s.
  2. Equation: v = v₀ + at (no s needed).
  3. Substitute: v = 70 + (−1.5)(40) = 70 − 60.
  4. Compute: v = 10 m/s. Still positive → it's still moving forward, just slower.
Final: v = 10 m/s (still in the direction of motion).

Example 2.2.3 — Acceleration from rest to a target speed (no time given)

A car starts from rest and accelerates at 3.5 m/s². How far does it travel to reach 25 m/s?

  1. Given: v₀ = 0, v = 25 m/s, a = 3.5 m/s².
  2. Time is not given → use equation ③: v² = v₀² + 2as.
  3. Rearrange: s = (v² − v₀²) / (2a) = (625 − 0)/(7.0).
  4. Compute: s = 625/7 = 89.3 m.
  5. Check with time: t = v/a = 25/3.5 = 7.14 s; s = ½(3.5)(7.14²) = 89.3 m ✓
Final: s = 89.3 m.

Example 2.2.4 — Deceleration distance (matric challenge)

A truck at 22 m/s slows to 14 m/s over 125 m. Find (a) acceleration, (b) time taken.

  1. Given: v₀ = 22 m/s, v = 14 m/s, s = 125 m. Time unknown for part (a).
  2. (a) Use v² = v₀² + 2as: 196 = 484 + 2a(125) → 196 − 484 = 250a → −288 = 250a.
  3. a = −1.152 m/s². Negative because it's decelerating.
  4. (b) Use v = v₀ + at: 14 = 22 + (−1.152)t → t = 8/1.152 = 6.94 s.
  5. Verify s = ((v₀+v)/2)·t = (18)(6.94) ≈ 125 m ✓
Final: a ≈ −1.15 m/s²; t ≈ 6.94 s.

Common Mistakes

  • Using a = (v−v₀)/t when time is unknown: Switch to equation ③.
  • Forgetting the sign of a: Deceleration = negative a (if velocity is positive).
  • Dropping the square: The t² in s = ½at² is essential; students often forget it.
  • Mixing units: Convert everything to SI (m, s, m/s, m/s²) before substituting.

Section 2.3

Free Fall

Free fall is motion under the influence of gravity alone (neglecting air resistance). Every freely falling object experiences the same downward acceleration g ≈ 9.8 m/s², regardless of mass.

ነጻ ውድቀት በስበት ብቻ ተጽዕኖ የሚደረግበት እንቅስቃሴ ነው። እያንዳንዱ ነጻ የሚወድቅ አካል g ≈ 9.8 m/s² ወደታች ማጣደፍ ያገኛል።

Free-fall equations (replace a with g and s with h):
v = v₀ + gt
h = v₀t + ½gt²
v² = v₀² + 2gh
Sign convention: Usually downward is positive when studying falling objects (so g = +9.8 m/s² and h is positive downward). የነጻ ውድቀት ቀመሮች፦ a በ g፣ s በ h ተተካ። አብዛኛውን ጊዜ ወደታች አዎንታዊ ነው።

Worked Examples

Example 2.3.1 — Mango falling from a tree

A mango falls from rest and reaches the ground in 1 s. Find its velocity and height.

  1. Given: v₀ = 0, g = 9.8 m/s², t = 1 s. Take downward as positive.
  2. Velocity: v = v₀ + gt = 0 + 9.8(1) = 9.8 m/s (downward).
  3. Height: h = v₀t + ½gt² = 0 + ½(9.8)(1)² = 4.9 m.
  4. Verify: v² = 2gh → 96.04 = 2(9.8)(4.9) = 96.04 ✓
Final: v = 9.8 m/s; h = 4.9 m.

Example 2.3.2 — Stone dropped from a building (matric exam)

A stone falls for 8.0 s before hitting the ground. Find (a) its velocity on impact, (b) height of the building.

  1. Given: v₀ = 0, g = 9.8 m/s², t = 8.0 s. Take downward as positive.
  2. (a) Velocity: v = gt = 9.8 × 8.0 = 78.4 m/s.
  3. (b) Height: h = ½gt² = ½(9.8)(64) = 313.6 m.
  4. Sanity check: v² = 2gh = 2(9.8)(313.6) = 6146 → √6146 ≈ 78.4 ✓
Final: v = 78.4 m/s; h = 313.6 m.

Example 2.3.3 — Brick dropped from 4.9 m

A brick is dropped from rest from 4.9 m. How long does it take to reach the ground?

  1. Given: v₀ = 0, h = 4.9 m, g = 9.8 m/s². Find t.
  2. Equation: h = ½gt² → t² = 2h/g.
  3. Substitute: t² = 2(4.9)/9.8 = 9.8/9.8 = 1.
  4. t = 1.0 s. (Nice — matches the mango example backwards.)
Final: t = 1.0 s.

Example 2.3.4 — Object thrown upward (matric challenge)

A ball is thrown straight up at 20 m/s. Find (a) time to reach maximum height, (b) maximum height reached.

  1. Sign convention: Upward positive → g = −9.8 m/s².
  2. At maximum height, v = 0. Use v = v₀ + at: 0 = 20 − 9.8t → t = 20/9.8 = 2.04 s.
  3. Height: h = v₀t + ½at² = 20(2.04) + ½(−9.8)(2.04²) = 40.8 − 20.4 = 20.4 m.
  4. Shorter way: v² = v₀² + 2ah → 0 = 400 − 19.6h → h = 400/19.6 = 20.4 m ✓
Final: t = 2.04 s; h = 20.4 m.

Common Mistakes

  • Forgetting sign convention: Choose once — down-positive or up-positive — and stick with it.
  • Confusing g with G: g (9.8 m/s²) is acceleration at Earth's surface; G is the gravitational constant (6.67 × 10⁻¹¹).
  • Assuming g varies with mass: It doesn't. Feather and hammer fall at the same rate in vacuum.
  • Ignoring the "upward" phase: For a ball thrown up, use h = 0 at the starting point and let a = −g.

Section 2.4

Graphical Representation of Uniformly Accelerated Motion

Three graphs describe uniformly accelerated motion — position-time (s-t), velocity-time (v-t), and acceleration-time (a-t). Each reveals different information.

ሦስት ግራፎች ወጥ የተጣደፈ እንቅስቃሴን ይገልጻሉ፦ የቦታ-ጊዜ (s-t)፣ የቬሎሲቲ-ጊዜ (v-t)፣ የማጣደፍ-ጊዜ (a-t)።

The three graphs — what each shows

GraphShapeKey feature
Position-time (s-t)ParabolaSlope of tangent = instantaneous velocity
Velocity-time (v-t)Straight inclined lineSlope = acceleration; area under line = displacement
Acceleration-time (a-t)Horizontal line (parallel to t-axis)Area under line = change in velocity

Key formulas from graphs

Slope of s-t (tangent) = v (instantaneous velocity)
Slope of v-t = a (acceleration)
Area under v-t = s (displacement)
Area under a-t = Δv (change in velocity)

Worked Examples

Example 2.4.1 — Instantaneous velocity from s-t graph

A car accelerates from rest at 10 m/s² for 10 s. Find instantaneous velocity at t = 4 s.

  1. Position values: s = ½at² = 5t². At t=3, s=45 m; at t=5, s=125 m.
  2. Tangent method: Draw tangent at t=4; pick two nearby points, e.g., t=3 (s=45) and t=5 (s=125).
  3. Slope = (125 − 45)/(5 − 3) = 80/2 = 40 m/s.
  4. Verify with v = at: v = 10 × 4 = 40 m/s ✓
Final: Instantaneous velocity at t = 4 s is 40 m/s.

Example 2.4.2 — Displacement from v-t graph (composite area)

From a v-t graph with the following phases: acceleration from 0 to 4 m/s over 4 s, constant 4 m/s from t=4 to t=12 s, deceleration to 0 over 2 s, then negative motion to −2 m/s over 1 s. Find total distance and displacement.

  1. Triangle 1 (t=0 to 4): s₁ = ½ × 4 × 4 = 8 m.
  2. Rectangle (t=4 to 12): s₂ = 8 × 4 = 32 m.
  3. Triangle 2 (t=12 to 14): s₃ = ½ × 2 × 4 = 4 m.
  4. Triangle 3 (t=14 to 15, below axis): s₄ = ½ × 1 × 2 = 1 m (negative contribution).
  5. Distance = 8 + 32 + 4 + 1 = 45 m. Displacement = 8 + 32 + 4 − 1 = 43 m (positive).
Final: Distance = 45 m; displacement = 43 m.

Example 2.4.3 — Change in velocity from a-t graph

An a-t graph shows a constant 4 m/s² for 9 s. Find the change in velocity.

  1. Given: a = 4 m/s², t = 9 s. Area under a-t line = Δv.
  2. Shape: Rectangle: area = base × height = 9 × 4.
  3. Compute: Δv = 36 m/s.
  4. Check with a = Δv/Δt: 36/9 = 4 m/s² ✓
Final: Δv = 36 m/s.

Example 2.4.4 — Multi-phase motion (matric exam)

A truck starts from rest, accelerates at 2 m/s² to 20 m/s, then travels at constant speed for 20 s, then brakes uniformly to stop in 5 s. Find (a) total time in motion, (b) average speed.

  1. Phase 1 (accelerate): t₁ = v/a = 20/2 = 10 s; s₁ = ½(2)(100) = 100 m.
  2. Phase 2 (constant): t₂ = 20 s; s₂ = 20 × 20 = 400 m.
  3. Phase 3 (brake): t₃ = 5 s; s₃ = ½(20 + 0)(5) = 50 m.
  4. (a) Total time = 10 + 20 + 5 = 35 s.
  5. (b) Total distance = 100 + 400 + 50 = 550 m. Average speed = 550/35 = 15.7 m/s.
Final: t_total = 35 s; average speed = 15.7 m/s.

Common Mistakes

  • Confusing slope with area: Slope gives a rate (velocity from s-t, acceleration from v-t); area gives a total (displacement from v-t, Δv from a-t).
  • Ignoring sign of area: Area below the t-axis counts as negative displacement.
  • Reading instantaneous velocity as average: On a curved s-t graph, only a tangent gives the instantaneous value.
  • Thinking a-t is always zero when velocity is zero: An object momentarily at rest can still be accelerating (e.g., at the top of a thrown ball's path).

Section 2.5

Relative Velocity in One Dimension

Relative velocity is the velocity of one object measured from the reference frame of another. Every velocity must be stated relative to some frame — usually the ground.

አንጻራዊ ቬሎሲቲ የአንድ አካል ቬሎሲቲ ከሌላ አካል የማጣቀሻ ማዕቀፍ አንጻር የሚለካው ነው።

Key formulas

Relative velocity of A w.r.t. B: v_AB = v_A − v_B
Relative velocity of B w.r.t. A: v_BA = v_B − v_A = −v_AB
Same direction: |v_AB| = |v_A − v_B| (difference of magnitudes).
Opposite direction: |v_AB| = |v_A| + |v_B| (sum of magnitudes).
Sign convention: Choose one direction as positive; the opposite becomes negative. Then always use v_AB = v_A − v_B. ተመሳሳይ አቅጣጫ፦ |v_A − v_B|። ተቃራኒ አቅጣጫ፦ |v_A| + |v_B|።

Worked Examples

Example 2.5.1 — Two cars in opposite directions

Car A travels east at 100 km/h; car B travels west at 100 km/h. Find relative velocity of A w.r.t. B and B w.r.t. A.

  1. Sign convention: East positive. v_A = +100, v_B = −100 km/h.
  2. v_AB = v_A − v_B = 100 − (−100) = +200 km/h (A appears to move east at 200 km/h to B).
  3. v_BA = v_B − v_A = (−100) − 100 = −200 km/h (B appears to move west at 200 km/h to A).
  4. Magnitudes are equal (200 km/h), directions opposite.
Final: |v_AB| = |v_BA| = 200 km/h.

Example 2.5.2 — Two cars in the same direction

Both cars travel east at 100 km/h. Find v_AB.

  1. Signs: v_A = +100, v_B = +100 km/h.
  2. v_AB = 100 − 100 = 0 km/h.
  3. Interpretation: To B, A appears motionless. To an observer on the ground, both move at 100 km/h.
Final: v_AB = 0 km/h (relative rest).

Example 2.5.3 — Police chasing a thief (matric challenge)

A thief runs at 9 m/s on a jeep; a police officer chases at 10 m/s. The separation is 100 m. How long to catch?

  1. Sign convention: Both move in the direction of chase — call it positive. v_thief = +9, v_police = +10.
  2. Relative speed of approach = v_police − v_thief = 10 − 9 = 1 m/s.
  3. Time to close 100 m gap: t = distance / relative speed = 100 / 1 = 100 s.
  4. Check: In 100 s, police travels 1000 m, thief 900 m; gap closed = 100 m ✓
Final: t = 100 s.

Example 2.5.4 — Motorcycle passing a car (matric exam)

A motorcycle at 120 km/h passes a car at 90 km/h (same direction). What is the motorcycle's velocity relative to the car?

  1. Signs: v_motorcycle = +120, v_car = +90 km/h.
  2. v_relative = 120 − 90 = +30 km/h.
  3. Interpretation: To a passenger in the car, the motorcycle appears to move forward at 30 km/h.
Final: v = 30 km/h in the direction of travel.

Example 2.5.5 — Jet and hot air (matric exam)

A jet cruises at 1000 km/h and ejects hot air backward at 800 km/h relative to the jet. Find the hot air's speed relative to the ground.

  1. Sign convention: Forward positive. v_jet = +1000 km/h.
  2. Relative to jet, air speed is backward 800 km/h → v_air/jet = −800 km/h.
  3. v_air = v_jet + v_air/jet = 1000 + (−800) = +200 km/h (still moving forward, just slower than jet).
  4. Check: Relative to the jet, ground speed = 200 − 1000 = −800 km/h ✓ (backward 800 km/h).
Final: Air speed = 200 km/h forward relative to ground.

Common Mistakes

  • Forgetting sign convention: Always decide which direction is positive first, then apply signs to all velocities.
  • Adding magnitudes blindly: Only valid when velocities are opposite. For same direction, subtract.
  • Confusing "A relative to B" with "B relative to A": v_AB = −v_BA; they have opposite signs.
  • Forgetting units: Relative velocity keeps the same units as the individual velocities.
