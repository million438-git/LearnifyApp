Average Speed, Velocity and Acceleration

Unit 3: Motion in a Straight Line

Learning objectives

  • Explain the main concepts in your own words.
  • Use relevant scientific vocabulary correctly.
  • Apply the concepts to a practical or numerical example.

Lesson notes

Average speed is total distance divided by total time. Average velocity is displacement divided by elapsed time. Instantaneous speed describes how fast an object moves at a particular moment.

Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity. It may result from a change in speed, direction, or both. Use signs consistently when choosing a positive direction.

Key relationship

average speed = total distance ÷ total time; average velocity = displacement ÷ time; a = (v − u) ÷ t

Worked example

A car's velocity changes from 4 m/s to 16 m/s in 6 s. Acceleration = (16 − 4) ÷ 6 = 2 m/s².

Try it yourself

A cyclist travels 90 m in 15 s. Find average speed.

Reveal suggested answer

90 ÷ 15 = 6 m/s.
