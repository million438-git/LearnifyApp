Heat Transfer and Specific Heat Capacity

Unit 7: Temperature and Thermometry

Learning objectives

  • Explain the main concepts in your own words.
  • Use relevant scientific vocabulary correctly.
  • Apply the concepts to a practical or numerical example.

Lesson notes

Conduction transfers thermal energy through interactions within matter. Convection transfers energy by bulk movement of a fluid. Radiation transfers energy by electromagnetic waves and does not require a material medium.

Specific heat capacity is the energy needed to raise the temperature of 1 kg of a substance by 1 °C or 1 K. Water has a relatively high specific heat capacity. Insulation reduces unwanted thermal energy transfer.

Key relationship

thermal energy change Q = mass m × specific heat capacity c × temperature change ΔT

Worked example

For 1 kg of water, c = 4200 J/(kg·°C) and ΔT = 5 °C: Q = 1 × 4200 × 5 = 21,000 J.

Try it yourself

Calculate the energy needed to heat 2 kg of water by 3 °C using c = 4200 J/(kg·°C).

Reveal suggested answer

Q = 2 × 4200 × 3 = 25,200 J.
