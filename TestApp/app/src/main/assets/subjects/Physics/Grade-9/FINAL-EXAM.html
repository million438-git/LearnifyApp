Grade 9 Physics Final Examination

Course-wide revision assessment

Final revision examination

This revision assessment samples the seven course units. Answer first without opening the answer guides. For calculations, write the equation, substitute values, calculate, and include units.

  1. Unit 1: Physics and Human Society. Give two ways physics contributes to daily life.

    Show answer guide

    Examples: electricity and communication technology, vehicle motion, medical imaging, construction, refrigeration, and sound systems.

  2. Unit 1: Physics and Human Society. Match a refrigerator, a musical instrument, and a telescope to relevant branches of physics.

    Show answer guide

    Refrigerator: thermodynamics; musical instrument: acoustics; telescope: optics.

  3. Unit 1: Physics and Human Society. Name two physicists and describe one contribution from each.

    Show answer guide

    Examples: Newton—laws of motion and gravitation; Faraday—electromagnetism; Curie—radioactivity research.

  4. Unit 2: Physical Quantities. Convert 450 cm to metres.

    Show answer guide

    450 ÷ 100 = 4.5 m.

  5. Unit 2: Physical Quantities. State two ways to improve the reliability of a length measurement.

    Show answer guide

    Check zero, read at eye level, use a suitable ruler, and repeat measurements.

  6. Unit 2: Physical Quantities. Classify mass, force, time, and velocity as scalar or vector.

    Show answer guide

    Mass: scalar; force: vector; time: scalar; velocity: vector.

  7. Unit 3: Motion in a Straight Line. A student walks 10 m east and then 4 m west. Find distance and displacement.

    Show answer guide

    Distance = 10 + 4 = 14 m. Displacement = 6 m east.

  8. Unit 3: Motion in a Straight Line. A cyclist travels 90 m in 15 s. Find average speed.

    Show answer guide

    90 ÷ 15 = 6 m/s.

  9. Unit 3: Motion in a Straight Line. A velocity increases from 2 m/s to 14 m/s over 4 s. Find acceleration.

    Show answer guide

    a = (14 − 2) ÷ 4 = 3 m/s².

  10. Unit 4: Force, Work, Energy and Power. Find the resultant force needed to accelerate 3 kg at 4 m/s².

    Show answer guide

    F = 3 × 4 = 12 N.

  11. Unit 4: Force, Work, Energy and Power. Calculate the kinetic energy of a 2 kg object moving at 4 m/s.

    Show answer guide

    KE = ½ × 2 × 4² = 16 J.

  12. Unit 4: Force, Work, Energy and Power. A student does 240 J of work in 12 s. Find power.

    Show answer guide

    P = 240 ÷ 12 = 20 W.

  13. Unit 5: Simple Machines. Name three types of simple machine and give one use for each.

    Show answer guide

    Lever: crowbar; pulley: raising a flag; inclined plane: loading a box onto a truck.

  14. Unit 5: Simple Machines. A lever raises a 300 N load using a 75 N effort. Find mechanical advantage.

    Show answer guide

    MA = 300 ÷ 75 = 4.

  15. Unit 5: Simple Machines. A machine takes in 800 J and delivers 600 J of useful work. Find efficiency.

    Show answer guide

    600 ÷ 800 × 100% = 75%.

  16. Unit 6: Mechanical Oscillation and Sound Waves. A source completes 30 oscillations in 15 s. Find frequency and period.

    Show answer guide

    f = 30 ÷ 15 = 2 Hz; T = 1/2 = 0.5 s.

  17. Unit 6: Mechanical Oscillation and Sound Waves. A wave travels at 24 m/s and has frequency 6 Hz. Find wavelength.

    Show answer guide

    λ = v ÷ f = 24 ÷ 6 = 4 m.

  18. Unit 6: Mechanical Oscillation and Sound Waves. Why must the echo travel time be divided by two when finding distance to a wall?

    Show answer guide

    The measured time includes both the journey to the wall and the return journey.

  19. Unit 7: Temperature and Thermometry. Convert 30 °C to kelvin.

    Show answer guide

    30 + 273.15 = 303.15 K.

  20. Unit 7: Temperature and Thermometry. Why are expansion gaps used in bridges?

    Show answer guide

    They allow thermal expansion and contraction, reducing stress and the risk of damage.

  21. Unit 7: Temperature and Thermometry. Calculate the energy needed to heat 2 kg of water by 3 °C using c = 4200 J/(kg·°C).

    Show answer guide

    Q = 2 × 4200 × 3 = 25,200 J.
