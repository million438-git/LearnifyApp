Uniform Motion and Motion Graphs

Unit 3: Motion in a Straight Line

Learning objectives

  • Explain the main concepts in your own words.
  • Use relevant scientific vocabulary correctly.
  • Apply the concepts to a practical or numerical example.

Lesson notes

Uniform motion means constant velocity: equal displacements occur in equal time intervals in the same direction. On a position-time graph, the gradient represents velocity. A horizontal position-time line indicates no change in position.

On a velocity-time graph, gradient represents acceleration. The signed area between the graph and the time axis represents displacement. Label axes, units, and scales before interpreting a graph.

Key relationship

gradient = change in vertical quantity ÷ change in horizontal quantity

Worked example

A position-time graph changes from 5 m at 2 s to 25 m at 6 s. Average velocity = (25 − 5) ÷ (6 − 2) = 5 m/s.

Try it yourself

A velocity increases from 2 m/s to 14 m/s over 4 s. Find acceleration.

Reveal suggested answer

a = (14 − 2) ÷ 4 = 3 m/s².
