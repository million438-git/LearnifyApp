Force and Newton's Laws of Motion

Unit 4: Force, Work, Energy and Power

Learning objectives

  • Explain the main concepts in your own words.
  • Use relevant scientific vocabulary correctly.
  • Apply the concepts to a practical or numerical example.

Lesson notes

A force is a push or pull and has magnitude and direction. The resultant force is the vector sum of all forces on an object. Balanced forces give zero resultant force; unbalanced forces can cause acceleration.

Newton's first law describes inertia. Newton's second law relates resultant force to mass and acceleration. Newton's third law states that interacting objects exert equal and opposite forces on each other; these forces act on different objects.

Key relationship

resultant force F = mass m × acceleration a

Worked example

A 5 kg trolley accelerates at 2 m/s². Resultant force = 5 × 2 = 10 N.

Try it yourself

Find the resultant force needed to accelerate 3 kg at 4 m/s².

Reveal suggested answer

F = 3 × 4 = 12 N.
