Types of Cells and Levels of Organization

Grade 9 Biology • Ethiopian curriculum-oriented learning resource

Learning objectives

  • Compare prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells
  • Distinguish unicellular and multicellular organisms
  • Order levels of organization
  • Explain specialization

Study notes

Prokaryotic cells lack a membrane-bound nucleus; eukaryotic cells have one. Organisms may be unicellular or multicellular. In multicellular organisms, specialized cells form tissues, tissues form organs, organs work together in organ systems, and organ systems make an organism. Examples include muscle cells in muscle tissue and the heart as an organ. Specialization allows different parts of an organism to perform different tasks efficiently.

Practical or thinking activity

Arrange these from smallest to largest: organ, cell, organism, tissue, organ system.

Chapter review

1. What is the main distinction between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells?
Show answer

Eukaryotic cells have a membrane-bound nucleus; prokaryotic cells do not.

2. Put cell, tissue, organ, and organ system in order.
Show answer

Cell → tissue → organ → organ system.
