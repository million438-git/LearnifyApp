Introduction to Reproduction

Grade 9 Biology • Ethiopian curriculum-oriented learning resource

Learning objectives

  • Define reproduction
  • Compare sexual and asexual reproduction
  • Explain variation
  • Distinguish reproduction from growth

Study notes

Reproduction produces new individuals and helps maintain a species across generations. Asexual reproduction generally involves one parent and does not require fusion of gametes; offspring are often genetically similar to the parent. Sexual reproduction involves the fusion of gametes and usually produces genetic variation. Reproduction differs from growth: growth increases size or cell number, while reproduction produces new organisms. Different organisms reproduce in ways suited to their environments.

Practical or thinking activity

Compare two organisms that reproduce in different ways and identify one advantage and one limitation of each method.

Chapter review

1. What is one major difference between sexual and asexual reproduction?
Show answer

Sexual reproduction involves gamete fusion and usually creates more genetic variation; asexual reproduction generally does not.

2. Why is reproduction important to a species?
Show answer

It produces offspring and helps maintain the species across generations.
