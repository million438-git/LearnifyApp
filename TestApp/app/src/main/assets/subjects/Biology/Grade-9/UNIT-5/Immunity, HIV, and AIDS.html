Immunity, HIV, and AIDS

Grade 9 Biology • Ethiopian curriculum-oriented learning resource

Learning objectives

  • Describe body defences
  • Explain vaccination
  • Distinguish HIV from AIDS
  • Correct myths and reduce stigma

Study notes

The immune system helps defend the body against pathogens. Barriers such as skin and mucus help prevent entry; immune cells and antibodies help recognize and respond to infections. Vaccines train the immune system to recognize particular pathogens or their components. HIV attacks important immune cells; untreated HIV can progress to AIDS, a condition involving severe immune damage. HIV can be managed with effective antiretroviral treatment. It does not spread through casual contact, sharing food, or mosquito bites. Testing, confidential health services, treatment, and non-discrimination are important.

Practical or thinking activity

Prepare a myth-versus-fact table about infection prevention, using reliable public-health guidance.

Chapter review

1. What does vaccination help the immune system do?
Show answer

Recognize a pathogen or its components and respond more effectively to future exposure.

2. Can mosquitoes transmit HIV?
Show answer

No. HIV is not transmitted by mosquito bites.
