Microorganisms, Disease, and Prevention

Grade 9 Biology • Ethiopian curriculum-oriented learning resource

Learning objectives

  • Distinguish microorganism groups
  • Compare beneficial and harmful roles
  • Identify transmission routes
  • Explain prevention and responsible treatment

Study notes

Microorganisms include bacteria, microscopic fungi, protozoa, and microscopic algae; viruses require host cells to replicate and are not cells. Some microorganisms are beneficial in food production, decomposition, and normal body processes, while others cause disease. Pathogens may spread through air, contaminated food or water, contact, vectors, or blood, depending on the organism. Prevention can include sanitation, safe water, ventilation, handwashing, vaccination where available, vector control, and appropriate medical treatment. Antibiotics treat certain bacterial infections, not viral infections.

Practical or thinking activity

Choose one infectious disease and make a chart of its cause, transmission route, symptoms, and prevention using reliable health information.

Chapter review

1. Do antibiotics treat viral infections such as influenza?
Show answer

No. Antibiotics target bacteria and do not treat viruses.
