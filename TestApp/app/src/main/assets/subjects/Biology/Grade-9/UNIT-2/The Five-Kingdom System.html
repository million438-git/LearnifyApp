The Five-Kingdom System

Grade 9 Biology • Ethiopian curriculum-oriented learning resource

Learning objectives

  • Compare prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells
  • Distinguish fungi, plants, and animals
  • Recognize the traditional five kingdoms
  • Understand that classification systems change

Study notes

The traditional five-kingdom system groups organisms as Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, and Animalia. Monera includes prokaryotic organisms in this historical system; Protista includes diverse mostly simple eukaryotes; fungi absorb nutrients; plants generally photosynthesize; and animals obtain nutrients by ingestion. Modern classification recognizes additional domains and does not treat the five-kingdom model as the only possible system. Classification should be based on evidence, not on an organism's usefulness to humans.

Practical or thinking activity

Compare a mushroom, a green plant, and an animal using their nutrition and cell features.

Chapter review

1. How do fungi usually obtain nutrients?
Show answer

They absorb nutrients from organic material or other sources.

2. Which kingdom traditionally includes organisms that photosynthesize and have cellulose cell walls?
