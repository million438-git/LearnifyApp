Taxonomic Hierarchy and Binomial Nomenclature

Grade 9 Biology • Ethiopian curriculum-oriented learning resource

Learning objectives

  • Order the classification ranks
  • Understand genus and species
  • Write scientific names correctly
  • Distinguish common and scientific names

Study notes

A common taxonomic hierarchy proceeds from domain to kingdom, phylum (division in many plants), class, order, family, genus, and species. Each level groups organisms with increasingly shared characteristics toward species. In binomial nomenclature, each species has a two-part scientific name: genus followed by specific epithet. The genus begins with a capital letter and the second part begins with a lowercase letter; both are conventionally italicized when typed, such as Homo sapiens.

Practical or thinking activity

Write a scientific name with the genus capitalized and both words italicized when typed.

Chapter review

1. What two parts make up a binomial scientific name?
Show answer

The genus name and the specific epithet.

2. Which comes first: family or genus?
Show answer

Genus comes before family in the hierarchy from broad to specific.
