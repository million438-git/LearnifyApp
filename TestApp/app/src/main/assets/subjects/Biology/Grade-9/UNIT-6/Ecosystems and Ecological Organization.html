Ecosystems and Ecological Organization

Grade 9 Biology • Ethiopian curriculum-oriented learning resource

Learning objectives

  • Define ecology and ecosystem
  • Distinguish biotic and abiotic factors
  • Compare population and community
  • Explain habitat and niche

Study notes

Ecology studies relationships between organisms and their environment. An ecosystem includes living organisms and nonliving factors interacting in an area. Biotic factors include plants, animals, fungi, and microorganisms; abiotic factors include light, water, soil, temperature, and minerals. A population is members of one species in an area; a community includes populations of different species. Habitat is where an organism lives, while a niche describes its role and use of resources.

Practical or thinking activity

Survey a small outdoor area and record living organisms and nonliving environmental factors without disturbing wildlife.

Chapter review

1. What is an ecosystem?
Show answer

A system in which organisms interact with one another and with nonliving environmental factors.
