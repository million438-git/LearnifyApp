Types of Asexual Reproduction

Grade 9 Biology • Ethiopian curriculum-oriented learning resource

Learning objectives

  • Identify types of asexual reproduction
  • Give examples
  • Compare efficiency and variation
  • Explain vegetative propagation

Study notes

Asexual reproduction includes binary fission, budding, fragmentation, spore formation, and vegetative propagation. In binary fission, one cell divides into two daughter cells. Budding forms a new individual from an outgrowth. Fragmentation occurs when a fragment develops into a new organism in species capable of this. Vegetative propagation forms new plants from stems, roots, or leaves, naturally or with human assistance. These methods can be efficient but may produce less genetic variation than sexual reproduction.

Practical or thinking activity

Observe a safe plant cutting or local example of vegetative propagation and record the conditions needed for growth.

Chapter review

1. What is budding?
Show answer

A form of asexual reproduction in which a new individual grows as an outgrowth from the parent.

2. Give one example of vegetative propagation.
Show answer

New plants developing from potato tubers, runners, bulbs, stems, or suitable cuttings.
