Observing Cells with a Microscope

Grade 9 Biology • Ethiopian curriculum-oriented learning resource

Learning objectives

  • Prepare a wet mount safely
  • Focus correctly
  • Distinguish magnification from resolution
  • Record observations accurately

Study notes

Microscope observations depend on magnification, resolution, illumination, and slide preparation. A thin specimen allows light to pass through. A wet mount commonly uses a drop of water and a coverslip lowered at an angle to reduce air bubbles. Stains can improve contrast but may alter living cells. Drawings should be large, clear, labelled, and based on what is actually observed. Magnification enlarges an image, while resolution describes the ability to distinguish nearby details.

Practical or thinking activity

Make a labelled biological drawing from an observed prepared slide, recording the objective lens used.

Chapter review

1. Why is a thin specimen useful for a light microscope?
Show answer

It allows sufficient light to pass through for viewing.

2. What is resolution?
Show answer

The ability to distinguish two close points as separate details.
