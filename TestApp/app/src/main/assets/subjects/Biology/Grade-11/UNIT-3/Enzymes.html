🔬 3.1 What are enzymes?

Enzymes are protein molecules that act as biological catalysts — they accelerate chemical reactions by lowering activation energy. All enzymes are proteins (amino acid chains). They remain unchanged after reaction.

Chew bread slowly → sweeter taste (starch → maltose). Amylase in saliva.

⚙️ 3.2 Properties & functions

Physical properties

  • Denaturation (heat >40°C, extreme pH, heavy metals)
  • Soluble in water, dilute glycerol
  • Colloidal nature, high molecular weight
  • Biocatalyst – tiny amount converts large quantity

Chemical properties

  • Heat & pH sensitive – optimum required
  • Controlled by activators/inhibitors
  • High efficiency (100–10,000 substrates/sec)
  • Specificity (bond, group, substrate, optical, co‑factor)

Functions & Table 3.1

  • Lipases – split fats
  • Amylase – starch → sugars
  • Maltase – maltose → glucose
  • Trypsin – proteins → amino acids
  • Lactase – lactose → glucose+galactose
  • Helicase – unwinds DNA
  • DNA polymerase – copies DNA

🧩 3.3 Protein structures

  • Primary: amino acid sequence (peptide bonds)
  • Secondary: α‑helix or β‑sheet (H‑bonds)
  • Tertiary: 3D folding (disulfide, ionic, H‑bonds)
  • Quaternary: multiple subunits (e.g., hemoglobin)

🔐 3.4 Models & transition state

Lock and key: rigid active site fits substrate. Induced fit: active site molds around substrate.

🎚️ 3.5 Regulation

  • Allosteric enzymes: have regulatory sites for effectors.
  • Covalent modification: phosphorylation.
  • Inhibition: competitive (active site blocked), non‑competitive, uncompetitive.

🏷️ 3.6 Types

Structural: simple / conjugated (apoenzyme + cofactor).
Six classes: oxidoreductases, transferases, hydrolases, lyases, isomerases, ligases.

📌 Unit summary

Enzymes are protein biocatalysts. Activity depends on temp, pH, [S], inhibitors. Cofactors (coenzymes) assist. Models: lock‑key & induced fit. Regulation: allosteric, covalent, inhibition. Six enzyme classes. Malting important in Ethiopia.

🎯 20 Exam‑style Questions

Q1
Define enzymes. What is activation energy and how do enzymes affect it?
✅ answer: Enzymes are protein biocatalysts that lower the activation energy – the minimum energy required for reactants to convert to products. They speed up reactions without being consumed.
Q2
True or False? All enzymes are proteins, but not all proteins are enzymes. Explain.
✅ answer: True. Enzymes are a subset of proteins with catalytic activity. Other proteins have structural, transport, or regulatory roles (e.g., collagen, hemoglobin).
Q3
List three physical properties of enzymes.
✅ answer: (i) Denaturation by heat/pH, (ii) soluble in water/glycerol, (iii) high molecular weight, colloidal.
Q4
What is the difference between lock‑and‑key and induced‑fit models?
✅ answer: Lock‑and‑key: active site is rigid and perfectly fits substrate. Induced fit: active site changes shape to mold around substrate upon binding.
Q5
Name the four levels of protein structure and briefly describe each.
✅ answer: Primary (amino acid sequence), secondary (α‑helix/β‑sheet), tertiary (3D folding of one polypeptide), quaternary (multiple subunits).
Q6
How does temperature affect enzyme activity? Include optimum and denaturation.
✅ answer: Rate increases with temperature up to an optimum (~37°C human). Above optimum, enzyme denatures (loses shape) and activity drops sharply.
Q7
Explain competitive and non‑competitive inhibition.
✅ answer: Competitive: inhibitor binds active site, blocks substrate. Non‑competitive: inhibitor binds elsewhere, changing active site shape.
Q8
What is an allosteric enzyme? Give an example of regulation.
✅ answer: Allosteric enzymes have additional regulatory sites. Effectors (activators/inhibitors) bind there and cause conformational change, altering activity.
Q9
List the six main classes of enzymes according to their catalytic function.
✅ answer: Oxidoreductases, transferases, hydrolases, lyases, isomerases, ligases.
Q10
What is the role of cofactors and coenzymes? Give an example.
✅ answer: Cofactors (metal ions) or coenzymes (vitamin derivatives) assist enzyme function. Example: NAD⁺ is a coenzyme for dehydrogenases.
Q11
Describe the three steps of malting in Ethiopian traditional beverages.
✅ answer: 1) Steeping (soaking barley), 2) Germination (sprouting, producing amylases), 3) Kilning (drying to halt growth).
Q12
Why does raw potato produce bubbles with hydrogen peroxide, but boiled potato does not?
✅ answer: Raw potato contains catalase enzyme that breaks H₂O₂ into water and oxygen (bubbles). Boiling denatures catalase, so no reaction.
Q13
Define turnover number of an enzyme.
✅ answer: The number of substrate molecules converted to product per enzyme molecule per second when the enzyme is saturated.
Q14
What does the Michaelis‑Menten equation describe? Identify Vmax and KM.
✅ answer: It relates reaction rate to substrate concentration. Vmax = maximum rate, KM = substrate concentration at half Vmax (affinity).
Q15
Give two industrial applications of enzymes and name the enzyme used.
✅ answer: 1) Detergents – protease/amylase/lipase to remove stains. 2) Cheese making – rennin.
Q16
What is meant by ‘enzyme specificity’? Give one example.
✅ answer: Enzymes act only on specific substrates. E.g., urease catalyzes hydrolysis of urea only.
Q17
Explain why enzymes are sensitive to pH changes.
✅ answer: pH alters charges on amino acid residues, disrupting ionic/H‑bonds that maintain active site shape, leading to denaturation or reduced activity.
Q18
What is the role of the Ethiopian Physical Society? (bonus: but you can mention Ethiopian Biochemists?)
✅ answer: (Context: Ethiopian science communities) Ethiopian biochemical societies promote research, education, and curriculum development; they also support young scientists.
Q19
Differentiate between holoenzyme, apoenzyme, and cofactor.
✅ answer: Apoenzyme = protein part only (inactive). Cofactor = non‑protein helper. Holoenzyme = apoenzyme + cofactor (active).
Q20
Mention two Ethiopian traditional drinks that rely on malting and the key enzyme involved.
✅ answer: Tella and Borde. Amylase (from malt) converts starch to sugars for fermentation.
