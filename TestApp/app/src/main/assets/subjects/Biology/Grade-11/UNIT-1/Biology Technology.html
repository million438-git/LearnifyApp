1. Introduction to biotechnology

Biotechnology is the use of living organisms or their components to develop products and processes that benefit humans. It includes ancient techniques (fermentation, selective breeding) and modern tools (genetic engineering, cell culture).

📘 REMEMBER: Biotechnology = biology + technology – applying biological knowledge to solve problems.
🌟 WOW: The term “biotechnology” was coined in 1919 by Karl Ereky, but humans have used it for thousands of years (making bread, cheese).
2. Genetic engineering

Genetic engineering directly manipulates an organism’s DNA. Key tools:

  • Recombinant DNA – combining DNA from different sources.
  • CRISPR‑Cas9 – precise gene editing.
  • Plasmids and vectors – deliver genes into host cells.

Applications: insulin production, disease‑resistant crops, gene therapy.

📘 REMEMBER: Genetically modified organisms (GMOs) have altered DNA to express desired traits.
3. Medical biotechnology
  • Recombinant proteins – human insulin, growth hormone, clotting factors.
  • Monoclonal antibodies – diagnostics, targeted therapy (cancer).
  • Gene therapy – introduce healthy genes to treat disorders.
  • Vaccines – mRNA vaccines (COVID‑19) are a biotech breakthrough.
💉 WOW: The first recombinant drug (human insulin) was approved in 1982 – produced by bacteria.
4. Agricultural biotechnology
  • GM crops – pest resistant (Bt cotton), herbicide tolerant, enhanced nutrition (Golden Rice).
  • Molecular farming – growing pharmaceuticals in plants.
  • Marker‑assisted selection – speeds up traditional breeding.
🌾 REMEMBER: Bt crops produce a protein toxic to specific insects, reducing pesticide use.
5. Industrial & environmental biotechnology
  • Fermentation – produce antibiotics, amino acids, biofuels (ethanol).
  • Enzymes in industry – laundry detergents, cheese making, leather processing.
  • Bioremediation – using microbes to clean oil spills, heavy metals.
  • Bioplastics – biodegradable plastics from bacteria.
🌱 WOW: Some bacteria can digest plastic – a growing field of research!
6. Bioinformatics

Bioinformatics combines biology, computer science, and information technology to analyse biological data (DNA sequences, protein structures).

  • Genomics – sequencing entire genomes.
  • Proteomics – study of all proteins.
  • Drug design – computer‑aided discovery.
🧬 REMEMBER: The Human Genome Project (completed 2003) was a massive bioinformatics effort.
7. Ethical & social issues
  • GMO safety and labelling – consumer concerns.
  • Gene editing in humans – germline editing raises ethical questions.
  • Patenting life forms – who owns a gene?
  • Biopiracy – exploiting indigenous knowledge without benefit‑sharing.
⚖️ WOW: Many countries have strict regulations on genetic modification – the debate continues.

📋 60 practice questions

Q1
What is biotechnology?
  • A) Using machines to study biology
  • B) Using living organisms to make products or solve problems
  • C) The study of fossils
  • D) Breeding animals only
Correct answer: B
Why? Biotechnology applies biological systems or organisms to develop useful products. A is too narrow; C is paleontology; D is only a small part.
Q2
Which of the following is an example of traditional biotechnology?
  • A) CRISPR gene editing
  • B) Making yogurt with bacteria
  • C) mRNA vaccines
  • D) DNA sequencing
Correct answer: B
Why? Yogurt production uses bacteria (fermentation) – an ancient biotech. A, C, D are modern techniques.
Q3
Recombinant DNA technology involves:
  • A) Combining DNA from two different sources
  • B) Separating DNA fragments by size
  • C) Making many copies of DNA
  • D) Cutting DNA with enzymes
Correct answer: A
Why? Recombinant DNA means joining DNA from different organisms. B describes gel electrophoresis, C is PCR, D is restriction enzymes (a step in the process, but not the definition).
Q4
What is the main function of restriction enzymes in genetic engineering?
  • A) To join DNA fragments
  • B) To cut DNA at specific sequences
  • C) To amplify DNA
  • D) To carry DNA into host cells
Correct answer: B
Why? Restriction enzymes (restriction endonucleases) recognise short DNA sequences and cut them. Ligase joins fragments (A), PCR amplifies (C), plasmids are vectors (D).
Q5
A plasmid is often used as a:
  • A) Source of restriction enzymes
  • B) Vector to carry foreign DNA into bacteria
  • C) Method to visualise DNA
  • D) Type of restriction enzyme
Correct answer: B
Why? Plasmids are small circular DNA molecules that can be engineered to carry foreign genes into bacteria – they are cloning vectors.
Q6
CRISPR‑Cas9 technology allows scientists to:
  • A) Edit genes precisely
  • B) Only cut DNA randomly
  • C) Increase mutation rate randomly
  • D) Join DNA fragments
Correct answer: A
Why? CRISPR‑Cas9 is a gene‑editing tool that makes targeted cuts in DNA, enabling precise modifications.
Q7
The first recombinant DNA drug approved for human use was:
  • A) Human growth hormone
  • B) Insulin
  • C) Erythropoietin
  • D) Interferon
Correct answer: B
Why? Human insulin (Humulin) produced by genetically modified E. coli was approved in 1982 – first recombinant drug.
Q8
Golden Rice is genetically modified to produce:
  • A) Vitamin A (beta‑carotene)
  • B) Vitamin C
  • C) Iron
  • D) Protein
Correct answer: A
Why? Golden Rice contains genes that produce beta‑carotene, which the body converts to vitamin A, helping prevent deficiency.
Q9
Bt cotton is resistant to:
  • A) Herbicides
  • B) Insects
  • C) Fungi
  • D) Viruses
Correct answer: B
Why? Bt cotton produces a protein from Bacillus thuringiensis that is toxic to certain insects (like bollworms).
Q10
Monoclonal antibodies are used for:
  • A) Gene therapy
  • B) Cancer treatment and diagnostics
  • C) Creating GMOs
  • D) Increasing crop yield
Correct answer: B
Why? Monoclonal antibodies are lab‑made molecules that bind to specific targets; widely used in cancer therapy (e.g., trastuzumab) and diagnostic tests (pregnancy, COVID‑19).
Q11
Gene therapy aims to:
  • A) Replace a faulty gene with a healthy copy
  • B) Remove all genes from a cell
  • C) Increase the number of chromosomes
  • D) Silence all gene expression
Correct answer: A
Why? Gene therapy introduces a functional gene to compensate for a defective one, treating genetic disorders.
Q12
What is a common vector used to deliver genes in gene therapy?
  • A) Bacteria
  • B) Viruses
  • C) Plasmids
  • D) Liposomes
Correct answer: B
Why? Modified viruses (retroviruses, adenoviruses) are often used because they naturally infect cells and deliver genetic material. Plasmids and liposomes are also used, but viruses are the most common in clinical trials.
Q13
Polymerase Chain Reaction (PCR) is a technique to:
  • A) Separate DNA fragments by size
  • B) Make many copies of a specific DNA sequence
  • C) Cut DNA at specific sites
  • D) Join DNA fragments
Correct answer: B
Why? PCR amplifies DNA exponentially, generating millions of copies from a tiny sample – essential in forensics, diagnostics, research.
Q14
Gel electrophoresis separates DNA fragments based on:
  • A) Size (length)
  • B) Nucleotide sequence
  • C) Colour
  • D) Electrical charge type
Correct answer: A
Why? DNA fragments are negatively charged and move through a gel toward positive electrode; smaller fragments move faster and farther, so they separate by size.
Q15
What is the purpose of DNA ligase in genetic engineering?
  • A) Cutting DNA
  • B) Joining DNA fragments
  • C) Amplifying DNA
  • D) Visualising DNA
Correct answer: B
Why? DNA ligase catalyses the formation of phosphodiester bonds, sealing nicks and joining fragments – essential for creating recombinant DNA.
Q16
Stem cells are important in biotechnology because they:
  • A) Can differentiate into various cell types
  • B) Produce antibodies
  • C) Are easy to culture
  • D) Never divide
Correct answer: A
Why? Stem cells have the unique ability to develop into many different cell types, offering potential for regenerative medicine.
Q17
Bioinformatics primarily deals with:
  • A) Designing mechanical devices
  • B) Analysing biological data using computers
  • C) Breeding organisms
  • D) Studying ecosystems
Correct answer: B
Why? Bioinformatics uses computational tools to store, analyse, and interpret biological data (sequences, structures).
Q18
What is the main advantage of using microorganisms to produce human proteins (like insulin)?
  • A) They are easy to grow and multiply rapidly
  • B) They produce the protein exactly as human cells do
  • C) They never make mistakes
  • D) They do not require nutrients
Correct answer: A
Why? Bacteria like E. coli can be cultured quickly and in large quantities, making protein production efficient and cost‑effective. However, they may not always produce proteins with correct post‑translational modifications (so sometimes yeast or mammalian cells are used).
Q19
Which technique is used to detect specific DNA sequences in a sample?
  • A) PCR
  • B) Southern blot
  • C) Western blot
  • D) ELISA
Correct answer: B
Why? Southern blot detects specific DNA sequences using gel electrophoresis and hybridisation with a labelled probe. PCR amplifies but does not by itself identify; Western blot detects proteins, ELISA detects antibodies/antigens.
Q20
RNA interference (RNAi) is used to:
  • A) Increase gene expression
  • B) Silence specific genes
  • C) Edit genes permanently
  • D) Cut DNA
Correct answer: B
Why? RNAi uses small RNA molecules to degrade mRNA or block translation, effectively silencing gene expression.
Q21
Which of the following is a potential risk of GMOs?
  • A) Increased use of pesticides
  • B) Gene flow to wild relatives
  • C) Always causes allergies
  • D) Reduced crop yield
Correct answer: B
Why? One environmental concern is that genes from GM crops might transfer to wild relatives, potentially creating herbicide‑resistant weeds. A is not always true (Bt crops reduce pesticide use); C is not always; D is opposite (usually yield increases).
Q22
Bioremediation uses organisms to:
  • A) Produce antibiotics
  • B) Clean up pollutants
  • C) Improve crop taste
  • D) Create vaccines
Correct answer: B
Why? Bioremediation employs microbes or plants to degrade or remove environmental contaminants (oil, heavy metals).
Q23
What is a transgenic organism?
  • A) An organism that has genes from another species
  • B) An organism with extra chromosomes
  • C) An organism that never mutates
  • D) A cloned organism
Correct answer: A
Why? Transgenic means containing a gene or genes from another species introduced using genetic engineering.
Q24
The enzyme reverse transcriptase is used to:
  • A) Make DNA from an RNA template
  • B) Make RNA from DNA
  • C) Cut DNA
  • D) Join DNA fragments
Correct answer: A
Why? Reverse transcriptase synthesises complementary DNA (cDNA) from RNA – important in studying gene expression and making cDNA libraries.
Q25
Which of the following is an example of a biorefinery product?
  • A) Bioethanol from corn
  • B) Plastic from petroleum
  • C) Steel from iron ore
  • D) Concrete
Correct answer: A
Why? Biorefineries convert biomass into fuels, chemicals, and materials – bioethanol is a common biofuel produced by fermentation.
Q26
Monoclonal antibodies are produced by:
  • A) Hybridoma cells
  • B) Bacteria
  • C) Viruses
  • D) Plant cells
Correct answer: A
Why? Hybridoma cells (fused B‑cell and myeloma cell) are engineered to produce identical monoclonal antibodies.
Q27
What is the main purpose of a DNA microarray?
  • A) To sequence a whole genome
  • B) To measure expression levels of many genes simultaneously
  • C) To edit genes
  • D) To amplify DNA
Correct answer: B
Why? DNA microarrays (gene chips) allow researchers to monitor expression of thousands of genes at once.
Q28
Which of the following is a potential benefit of genetically modified crops?
  • A) Reduced pesticide use
  • B) Always higher nutritional value
  • C) They never fail
  • D) They require more water
Correct answer: A
Why? Bt crops can reduce insecticide applications. B is not always; C is false; D is opposite (some are drought‑tolerant).
Q29
The term “bioethics” refers to:
  • A) Ethical issues arising from biological research and its applications
  • B) The study of animal behaviour
  • C) The business of biotechnology
  • D) Laboratory safety rules
Correct answer: A
Why? Bioethics examines moral dilemmas in medicine, genetics, biotechnology, etc.
Q30
What is a major ethical concern about human gene editing?
  • A) It is too expensive
  • B) Changes could be passed to future generations (germline editing)
  • C) It always causes cancer
  • D) It is impossible
Correct answer: B
Why? Editing germline cells (sperm, eggs, embryos) alters genes in all descendants, raising profound ethical and safety questions.
Q31
Vaccines work by:
  • A) Introducing antibodies directly
  • B) Stimulating the immune system to produce memory cells
  • C) Killing pathogens instantly
  • D) Blocking pathogen entry
Correct answer: B
Why? Vaccines contain antigens that trigger an immune response, creating memory cells for future protection.
Q32
mRNA vaccines (like those for COVID‑19) work by:
  • A) Injecting a weakened virus
  • B) Using mRNA to instruct cells to produce a viral protein, triggering immunity
  • C) Directly neutralising the virus
  • D) Containing antibodies
Correct answer: B
Why? mRNA vaccines deliver genetic code for a viral spike protein; our cells produce the protein and mount an immune response.
Q33
What is the role of a selectable marker in genetic engineering?
  • A) To identify cells that have taken up the foreign DNA
  • B) To cut DNA
  • C) To make DNA visible
  • D) To increase mutation rate
Correct answer: A
Why? Selectable markers (e.g., antibiotic resistance genes) allow only transformed cells to grow, making selection easy.
Q34
The first animal to be cloned from an adult somatic cell was:
  • A) Dolly the sheep
  • B) Copycat (cat)
  • C) Polly the sheep
  • D) Andi the mouse
Correct answer: A
Why? Dolly (1996) was cloned from a mammary gland cell – first mammal cloned from an adult cell.
Q35
What is a genomic library?
  • A) A collection of all the genes of an organism cloned into vectors
  • B) A list of all proteins in an organism
  • C) A database of mutations
  • D) A physical collection of organisms
Correct answer: A
Why? A genomic library contains fragments of an organism's entire genome inserted into vectors (plasmids, phages).
Q36
Proteomics is the study of:
  • A) All proteins in a cell/organism
  • B) All genes
  • C) All metabolites
  • D) All lipids
Correct answer: A
Why? Proteomics = large‑scale study of proteins, their structures and functions.
Q37
Which of the following is NOT a biotechnology product?
  • A) Penicillin
  • B) Cheese
  • C) Stone
  • D) Insulin
Correct answer: C
Why? Stone is a geological material; penicillin (fungus), cheese (fermentation), insulin (recombinant) are biotech products.
Q38
What is a key advantage of using yeast instead of bacteria for producing human proteins?
  • A) Yeast grows faster
  • B) Yeast can perform eukaryotic post‑translational modifications
  • C) Yeast never mutates
  • D) Yeast is cheaper
Correct answer: B
Why? Yeast (eukaryote) can add sugar chains and fold proteins more like human cells, often producing functional proteins.
Q39
The CRISPR system was originally discovered as:
  • A) A bacterial immune defence against viruses
  • B) A method to sequence DNA
  • C) A way to make vaccines
  • D) A type of plasmid
Correct answer: A
Why? CRISPR‑Cas is a natural adaptive immune system in bacteria that cuts viral DNA.
Q40
What is a “knockout” organism?
  • A) An organism with a gene permanently inactivated
  • B) An organism with extra genes
  • C) An organism that cannot reproduce
  • D) A sterile organism
Correct answer: A
Why? Knockout organisms have a specific gene made non‑functional, used to study gene function.
Q41
The enzyme Taq polymerase, used in PCR, is obtained from:
  • A) A thermophilic bacterium (Thermus aquaticus)
  • B) Human cells
  • C) E. coli
  • D) Yeast
Correct answer: A
Why? Taq polymerase is heat‑stable, isolated from bacteria living in hot springs.
Q42

  • A) A DNA molecule used to carry foreign genetic material into a host cell
  • B) An enzyme that cuts DNA
  • C) A type of virus that causes disease
  • D) A machine that sequences DNA
Correct answer: A
Why? Vectors (plasmids, viruses) are delivery vehicles for recombinant DNA.
Q43
Which of the following is a technique used to insert foreign DNA into a plant genome?
  • A) Agrobacterium‑mediated transformation
  • B) Electroporation
  • C) Gene gun
  • D) All of the above
Correct answer: D
Why? All three methods are used: Agrobacterium naturally transfers DNA, electroporation uses electric pulses, gene gun shoots DNA‑coated particles.

  • A) It can be done independent of climate and season
  • B) Plants grow faster in culture
  • C) It always yields higher amounts
  • D) It is cheaper than field growth
Correct answer: A
Why? Cell culture allows year‑round production in controlled conditions, not reliant on weather. B and C are not always true; D can be expensive.

Q47

  • B) Negative electrode because DNA is positively charged

Why? DNA’s phosphate backbone gives it a negative charge, so it migrates toward the positive electrode.

  • A) Cuts DNA at specific sites
  • B) Joins DNA fragments together
  • C) Amplifies DNA

Q59

  • B) They always produce exactly the human form
  • C) They require no nutrients
  • D) They are never contaminated

Why? Microbes like bacteria or yeast grow fast, are easy to scale up, and are cost‑effective. B is false (post‑translational differences can occur), C false (need nutrients), D false (contamination risk exists).
Q60
