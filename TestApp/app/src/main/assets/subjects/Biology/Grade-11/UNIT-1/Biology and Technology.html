Q1
What is Biology?
  • A) Deals with the form and properties of matter and substances.
  • B) Science of life and living organisms.
  • C) Deals with Solar System and Universe.
  • D) Deals with the nature and properties of matter and energy.
✅ Correct answer: B
Why? Biology comes from Greek words bios (life) and logos (study). It is the natural science that studies life and living organisms, including their physical structure, chemical processes, molecular interactions, development, and evolution. Options A and D describe physics/chemistry, C describes astronomy.
Q2
Which statement is true regarding biologists?
  • A) All Biologists don’t look down microscopes in laboratories.
  • B) Some Biologists become astrobiologists.
  • C) Biologists take part in biomedical research.
  • D) Biologists help in the development of new drugs and vaccines.
  • E) All of them
✅ Correct answer: E (All of them)
Why? Every statement correctly describes biologists. A: "All Biologists don’t look down microscopes" – true, many field biologists never use microscopes. B: astrobiology (study of life in space) is a real field. C & D: biomedical research and drug development are common careers for biologists. So all statements are true.
Q3
Paleobiology is a study of _________
  • A) The biology of fossil animals and plants.
  • B) Human immuno-deficiency virus (HIV)
  • C) Deals with Human Genome.
  • D) None of these.
✅ Correct answer: A
Why? Paleobiology combines paleontology and biology; it uses fossil evidence to study the evolution, behaviour, and ecology of ancient organisms. Option B refers to virology, C to genomics. A is correct.
Q4
How many chromosomes have been found in a human cell?
  • A) 26
  • B) 46
  • C) 58
  • D) 68
✅ Correct answer: B (46)
Why? Human somatic cells contain 46 chromosomes (23 pairs). This was confirmed by early cytogenetic studies and the Human Genome Project mapped these 46 chromosomes. 46 is the correct diploid number.
Q5
Gregor Mendel Was a ______?
  • A) physiotherapists
  • B) botanists
  • C) zoologists
  • D) Monk
✅ Correct answer: D (Monk)
Why? Gregor Mendel was an Augustinian monk in the 19th century. While he conducted botanical experiments, his primary occupation was as a monk. He wasn't formally trained as a botanist but is now considered the father of genetics.
Q6
What is Gregor Mendel known for?
  • A) Father of modern genetics
  • B) Father of inheritance.
  • C) Father of Gene.
  • D) All of the above
✅ Correct answer: A (Father of modern genetics)
Why? Mendel is universally called the "father of modern genetics" for his discovery of fundamental laws of inheritance (dominance, segregation). While his work relates to inheritance and genes, the most precise and common title is A. Option B and C are not standard; "father of gene" is not used.
Q7
Applied science is a study of_________?
  • A) the process by which scientists approach their work
  • B) experimental science the use of experiments to obtain information
  • C) the use of scientific research to meet certain human needs
  • D) All of the above
✅ Correct answer: C
Why? Applied science uses existing scientific knowledge to develop practical applications, e.g., technology, medicine. Option A describes scientific method, B describes experimental science in general. C accurately defines applied science.
Q8
What is spontaneous generation?
  • A) the appearance of living organisms from non-living matter.
  • B) the appearance of non-living organisms from living matter.
  • C) the appearance of living organisms from living matter.
  • D) All of the above
✅ Correct answer: A
Why? Spontaneous generation (abiogenesis) is the obsolete hypothesis that living organisms arise from nonliving matter (e.g., maggots from rotten meat). It was disproved by Pasteur and others. Option B makes no sense; C describes biogenesis.
Q9
IV stands for_____
  • A) Independent Variable
  • B) Intra Variable
  • C) Interdependent Variable
  • D) Inside Variable
✅ Correct answer: A
Why? In scientific experiments, IV = Independent Variable, the factor that is deliberately changed by the researcher. The other options are not standard abbreviations.
Q10
DV stands for_____
  • A) Dependent Variable
  • B) Dependency Variable
  • C) Depend Variable
  • D) Depending Variable
✅ Correct answer: A
Why? DV = Dependent Variable, the outcome that is measured; it 'depends' on the independent variable. The other terms are grammatically incorrect in scientific context.
Q11
If you measure the carbon dioxide uptake by wheat plants as the light intensity changes over the day, you cannot control the effect of change in temperature. It could be a___
  • A) Dependent Variable
  • B) Independent Variable
  • C) Controlled variables.
  • D) Confounding variable
✅ Correct answer: D (Confounding variable)
Why? A confounding variable is an extra factor that influences both the independent and dependent variables, potentially distorting the results. Here temperature changes with light intensity and affects CO₂ uptake, so it's confounding.
Q12
Which of these best describes what science is?
  • A) a body of knowledge
  • B) a way of doing experiments
  • C) a way of looking at and thinking about the natural world
  • D) a series of ideas
✅ Correct answer: A
Why? Science is broadly defined as a systematic enterprise that builds and organizes knowledge in the form of testable explanations and predictions. It is indeed a body of knowledge (facts, laws, theories) accumulated through the scientific method. Option C describes the approach, but the most encompassing description is A.
Q13
The scientific method involves:
  • A) putting forward hypotheses in a form that can be tested
  • B) carrying out experiments
  • C) analysing results and drawing conclusions
  • D) all of these
✅ Correct answer: D
Why? The scientific method includes all these steps: forming a testable hypothesis, performing experiments, and analyzing results to draw conclusions. So D is correct.
Q14
Which of the following is NOT a type of biologist?
  • A) geneticist
  • B) entomologist
  • C) astrophysicist
  • D) doctor
✅ Correct answer: C
Why? Astrophysicist is a physicist/astronomer who studies space and celestial bodies, not biology. Geneticist (biology of genes), entomologist (insects), and doctor (medical/health) are biology-related fields.
Q15
Scientists often use statistics when drawing conclusions because:
  • A) statistics are more accurate than human opinion
  • B) statistics are more reliable than human opinion
  • C) statistics are infallible
  • D) None of these
✅ Correct answer: D
Why? Statistics quantify uncertainty and allow researchers to assess the probability that results are due to chance. They are not infallible, nor are they simply "more accurate" than opinion; they provide a standardized method to evaluate data. The best reason is that conclusions must be peer‑reviewed and reproducible, and statistics help communicate confidence. But among the options, D (None of these) is correct because A and B are subjective, and C is false. The actual reason is to allow other scientists to repeat and challenge results, but that's not listed.
Q16
The independent variable in an experiment is the variable that:
  • A) is measured by the experimenter
  • B) is controlled by the experimenter
  • C) is changed (manipulated) by the experimenter
  • D) upsets the reliability of the results
✅ Correct answer: C
Why? The independent variable is the one the experimenter deliberately manipulates to observe its effect. Option B is vague (controlled variables are different). C is precise.
Q17
The dependent variable in an experiment is the variable that:
  • A) is measured by the experimenter
  • B) is controlled by the experimenter
  • C) is changed (manipulated) by the experimenter
  • D) upsets the reliability of the results
✅ Correct answer: A
Why? The dependent variable is the outcome that is measured; it responds to changes in the independent variable. Hence it is measured.
Q18
Having a control condition in an investigation:
  • A) gives a ‘standard’ to compare against
  • B) increases the validity of the experiment
  • C) increases the reliability of the experiment
  • D) both A and B
✅ Correct answer: A
Why? A control provides a baseline to compare the experimental group. While it can improve validity, the primary purpose is to have a standard for comparison. The question specifically asks "having a control condition …" and the best match is A. B and C are consequences but not the direct definition.
Q19
Publishing reports of biological investigations in scientific journals is important because:
  • A) it allows biologists all over the world to understand your reports
  • B) it allows biologists all over the world to repeat your investigations
  • C) it allows biologists all over the world to challenge your results
  • D) all of the above
✅ Correct answer: D
Why? Publication enables the scientific community to read, replicate, and critique research – all essential for self‑correction and progress. Hence all three statements are true.
Q20
The scientific method is more reliable than opinion based on personal observation because:
  • A) scientists are more reliable than other people
  • B) the scientific method involves gathering information from controlled experiments to prove or disprove a hypothesis
  • C) observation is not a valid scientific technique
  • D) scientific method always gives the correct answer
✅ Correct answer: B
Why? The scientific method relies on controlled, repeatable experiments and objective evidence, reducing bias and chance. Opinion is subjective. A is false (scientists aren't inherently more reliable), C is false (observation is part of science), D is false (science can be refined). B accurately captures the strength of the method.
Q21
The reliability of an experiment is increased by:
  • A) carrying out repeat experiments
  • B) minimising personal judgement
  • C) working as quickly as possible
  • D) using the most appropriate apparatus
✅ Correct answer: A
Why? Reliability (consistency) is improved through replication – repeating experiments yields consistent results. Minimizing judgement (objectivity) and using appropriate apparatus improve validity, but the direct way to increase reliability is repetition. Working quickly may introduce errors.
