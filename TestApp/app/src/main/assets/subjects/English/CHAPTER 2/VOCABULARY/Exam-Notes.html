🔤 synonyms

why it matters exams love to test nuance — two words may seem alike but carry different intensity or formality.
  • abandon – leave, desert, forsake, relinquish
  • brave – courageous, valiant, intrepid, audacious (audacious can be slightly negative)
  • rich – wealthy, affluent, opulent (places), well-to-do
📌 exam edge: In sentence completion, identify connotations: "She felt ___ after winning" — 'elated' (strong joy) vs. 'happy' (mild). pick the sharper fit.
🎯 collocation aware

⚖️ antonyms

power opposites direct opposites, but also gradable (hot/cold) & complementary (dead/alive).
  • ephemeral ⇄ permanent / eternal
  • mitigate ⇄ aggravate / intensify
  • candid ⇄ secretive / reserved
  • exacerbate ⇄ alleviate
🧠 trap alert: 'nonplussed' (confused) looks like 'unaffected' — but its antonym is 'composed'? no! it means bewildered, so antonym = unfazed? careful: actual meaning is confused → antonyms: unperturbed? better: clear-headed. always check meaning first.

📖 word meanings

depth over surface same word, multiple shades (polysemy). entrance exams test less common meanings.
  • harbor – (n) port; (v) to conceal or hold (harbor a grudge)
  • weather – (v) to withstand (weather the storm)
  • table – (v) UK: propose discussion / US: postpone indefinitely
  • pitch – black sticky substance / sales talk / throw / slope
🔎 context clues: "The manager decided to table the motion" – in US exam = kill; in UK = bring forward. read surrounding text for region.

🔍 context‑based

words in wild meaning shifts depending on surrounding words.
  • run – run a company / run out of time / run for office / a run on the bank
  • clear – clear water / clear idea / clear the room / clear a debt
🧩 strategy: Cover the word. Read sentence for logic: "Despite the rain, the parade ___." despite = contrast, so word must be 'continued' or 'went on' not 'cancelled'.

🔁 prefixes & suffixes

build & decode 70% of advanced vocabulary uses affixes.
  • mal- (bad) → malady, malpractice, malodorous
  • -cide (killing) → homicide, pesticide, genocide
  • ante- (before) → anteroom, antedate, antecedent
  • -logue (speech) → monologue, prologue, eulogy
🧠 reverse engineer: 'benevolent' → bene=good, volent=wishing → kind. unknown word 'omnipotent' → omni=all, potent=power → all‑powerful.

👯 homophones & homonyms

sound alike / same spelling homophones = different meaning & spelling, same sound. homonyms = same spelling, different meaning.
  • homophones: aisle/isle, idle/idol, palate/palette/pallet, cymbal/symbol
  • homonyms: bark (dog / tree), bat (animal / sports), crane (bird / machine)
  • lead (metal / to guide) – same spelling, different sound (heteronyms)
✍️ writing test trick: "The priest gave an ___ (alter/altar) call." altar = sacred table, alter = change → context decides. Always think meaning, not sound.

🤝 collocations

words that stick together natural partnerships – sound wrong if mixed.
  • heavy rain (not ‘strong rain’) / strong wind (not ‘heavy wind’)
  • make a decision (not ‘do a decision’)
  • fast food (not ‘quick food’ though quick meal ok)
  • pay attention / give attention (both ok but 'pay' more common)
💡 exam shortcut: if two words sound ‘off’ to a native ear, it’s the wrong collocation. learn chunks: “utterly ridiculous”, “deeply regret”.

⚡ phrasal verbs

verb + particle = new meaning essential for reading and use of English.
  • come up with (think of an idea)
  • put up with (tolerate)
  • look down on (despise)
  • fall through (fail to happen)
  • run out of (exhaust supply)
🧩 separable? "turn down the offer" / "turn the offer down". If pronoun → must separate: turn it down (not turn down it). particle = preposition/adverb.

🎭 idioms & expressions

cultural phrases meaning not literal.
  • spill the beans – reveal a secret
  • hit the sack – go to sleep
  • under the weather – feel ill
  • once in a blue moon – rarely
  • bite the bullet – face something difficult
📚 entrance exams: idiom questions usually test the least obvious. “She was feeling a bit under the weather” doesn’t mean under rain. Always eliminate literal choices.

🏗️ word formation

noun / verb / adj / adverb FCE, CAE style – use correct form in gap.
  • broad (adj) → broaden (v) → breadth (n) → broadly (adv)
  • decide (v) → decision (n) → decisive (adj) → indecisive (adj)
  • economy (n) → economic (adj) → economical (saving money) → economise (v)
  • compete (v) → competition (n) → competitive (adj) – competitor (person)
✏️ key: is gap a noun? after ‘the’ often noun. after ‘very’ → adjective. “She acted very ___. (responsible / responsibly)?” adjective = responsible. “She acted with great ___.” → responsibility.

  • ⏳ council (group) / counsel (advice/advise)
  • 📄 stationary (still) / stationery (paper)
  • ⚖️ principal (main / head) / principle (rule)
  • 🎭 compliment (praise) / complement (go well with)
  • 🌬️ affect (verb) / effect (noun most times)
