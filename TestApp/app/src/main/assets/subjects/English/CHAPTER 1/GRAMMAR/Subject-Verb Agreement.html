📘 MASTER RULES · singular & plural · indefinite pronouns · collective nouns · either/neither/each/everyone
1️⃣ SINGULAR & PLURAL BASICS
  • Singular subject → singular verb: The dog runs.
  • Plural subject → plural verb: The dogs run.
  • Compound with 'and' → usually plural: Ali and Hana are here.
  • Or / nor → verb agrees with closer subject: Either the teacher or the students are wrong.
  • Intervening phrases (together with, as well as) do NOT change number: The captain, as well as his players, is excited.
⚡ exam spotlight: “The quality of the products is excellent.” (subject = quality → singular)
2️⃣ INDEFINITE PRONOUNS
  • Always singular: anyone, everyone, someone, no one, nobody, each, either, neither, much, little, a little.
  • Always plural: several, few, both, many.
  • Context-based (sing/plural): all, some, most, none, any – look at the noun that follows.
✔ Everyone has finished.   Several were late.
✔ Most of the water is gone.   Most of the students are here.
3️⃣ COLLECTIVE NOUNS
  • Group as a unit → singular: family, team, committee, government, audience, staff.
    The team wins every match.
  • Members acting individually → plural (less common but tested): The family have different opinions.
  • Always plural in English: police, people, cattle.
    The police are coming.
⚡ Ethiopian favourite: “The committee has reached a decision.” (singular unit)
4️⃣ EITHER / NEITHER / EACH / EVERYONE
  • Either / Neither (as pronoun) + singular verb: Neither is correct.
  • Either … or / Neither … nor → verb agrees with second subject: Neither the manager nor the employees want overtime.
  • Each + singular noun → singular: Each student has a book.
  • Each of + plural noun → still singular: Each of the students is ready.
  • Everyone / Everybody → always singular: Everyone likes cake.
🚨 error: "Everyone are happy" ✘ → "Everyone is happy" ✔

📌 ADDITIONAL HIGH‑YIELD RULES (ENTRANCE LEVEL)

  • • Titles & names: “The United States is a country.” (singular)
  • • Time, money, distance → singular: “Ten years is a long time.”
  • • Fractions/percentages + of-phrase: 50% of the population is / 50% of the residents are
  • • There is / There are → agrees with noun that follows: “There is a book.” / “There are three pens.”
  • • Relative clauses (who/which/that) verb agrees with antecedent: “She is one of those teachers who inspire students.” (antecedent = teachers → plural)
  • • The number of / A number of → the number (singular), a number (plural).
