📘 DETAILED NOTES · PARTS OF SPEECH
🏷️ NOUNS
types
proper, common, concrete, abstract, collective, compound
countable / uncountable
countable: book/books; uncountable: water, advice, furniture.
many books, much water, a lot of advice.
⚡ The team (collective) has won.
⚡ I need information (uncountable).
👥 PRONOUNS
personal
I, you, he, she, it, we, they (subject); me, him, her, us, them (object)
reflexive
myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, yourselves, themselves
relative
who, whom, which, that, whose
demonstrative
this, that, these, those
⚡ She loves herself. • The man who called is my uncle.
⚡ VERBS
main / auxiliary / modal
main: run, eat, write
auxiliary: be, have, do (help form tenses)
modal: can, could, may, might, must, shall, should, will, would
⚡ She is writing (aux + main).
⚡ You must go (modal).
🌈 ADJECTIVES
degrees of comparison
positive: tall, beautiful
comparative: taller, more beautiful
superlative: tallest, most beautiful
⚡ big – bigger – biggest
⚡ intelligent – more intelligent – most intelligent
⏱️ ADVERBS
manner, place, time, frequency
manner: quickly, carefully
place: here, outside, everywhere
time: now, then, soon, yesterday
frequency: always, never, often, rarely
⚡ She sings beautifully (manner).
⚡ They always arrive early (frequency).
📍 PREPOSITIONS
Show relationship: in, on, at, under, over, between, among, during, by, with, about, etc.
⚡ The book is on the table.
⚡ We will meet at 5 pm.
🔗 CONJUNCTIONS
coordinating
and, but, or, nor, for, so, yet (FANBOYS)
subordinating
because, although, if, when, while, since, as, etc.
⚡ I like tea but she likes coffee.
⚡ Although it rained, we went out.
❗ INTERJECTIONS
Express emotion: Oh!, Wow!, Alas!, Ouch!, Hey!, Bravo!
⚡ Wow! That's amazing.
⚡ Alas! She is gone.

🎯 ADVANCED / ENTRANCE LEVEL RULES

  • • Countable/uncountable: advice, furniture, information, luggage, news → always uncountable (much advice).
  • • Reflexive pronouns: never used as subject; "He himself did it" (emphatic).
  • • Relative pronouns: who (people), which (things), that (both), whose (possession).
  • • Modal verbs: no 'to' after modal (can go, not can to go).
  • • Adjective order: opinion–size–age–shape–colour–origin–material (a beautiful small old round red Ethiopian wooden table).
  • • Adverb placement: frequency before main verb (always comes), after 'be' (is always).
  • • Prepositions after certain words: interested in, good at, depend on, listen to, etc.
  • • Correlative conjunctions: both...and, either...or, neither...nor, not only...but also.
