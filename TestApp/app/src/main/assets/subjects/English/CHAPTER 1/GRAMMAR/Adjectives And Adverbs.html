📌 adj vs adv

  • Adjective modifies noun: a quiet room
  • Adverb modifies verb/adj/adv: speaks quietly
  • 🔸 linking verbs (feel, look, taste) → adjective
  • 🔹 "She felt bad" (not badly) – emotion

📊 comparatives & superlatives

  • 1‑syll: small → smaller → smallest
  • 2‑syll ‑y: happy → happier → happiest
  • 2+ syll: beautiful → more beautiful → most beautiful
  • ⚠️ irregular: good/better/best; bad/worse/worst; far/farther/further

🧩 order of adjectives

  • OSASCOMP – Opinion, Size, Age, Shape, Color, Origin, Material, Purpose
  • a lovely small old rectangular green French wooden writing desk
  • determiner first: the, a, my

🔧 adverb formation

  • adj + ‑ly: quick → quickly
  • ‑y → ‑ily: happy → happily
  • ‑ic → ‑ically: dramatic → dramatically
  • ‑le → ‑ly: gentle → gently
  • ⚠️ irregular: fast, hard, late, early, daily

🔹 1. ADJECTIVES & ADVERBS — the fine line

📘 Definition: Adjectives describe nouns. Adverbs describe verbs, adjectives, or other adverbs.
adjective She is a careful driver.
➡️ linking verb: She seems careful.
adverb She drives carefully.
➡️ She drives very carefully.
🚨 EXAM CLASSIC: linking verbs + adjective
✦ “The soup tastes delicious.” ✦ “He felt bad about the news.”
⚡ tricky pairs — same form (adj & adv)
✅ fast – a fast car / drives fast · ✅ hard – works hard (hardly = barely) · ✅ late / early / daily
💡 good vs well – She speaks good English. / She speaks English well. (well = healthy: “I feel well”)

📈 2. COMPARATIVE & SUPERLATIVE – everything you need

🔸 one syllable → ‑er / ‑est tall → taller → tallest · big → bigger (double g)
🔸 two syllables -y → ‑ier / ‑iest happy → happier → happiest
🔸 two+ syllables → more / most beautiful → more beautiful → most beautiful
🔸 IRREGULAR good/well → better/best · bad/badly → worse/worst · far → farther/further
🧪 advanced structures • the + comp, the + comp • comp + and + comp • as ... as • less / least
⚠️ double comparative trap: “more better” ✘ · use “better” alone.

📏 3. ORDER OF ADJECTIVES – OSASCOMP

OPINION → SIZE → AGE → SHAPE → COLOR → ORIGIN → MATERIAL → PURPOSE
🌟 examples: ✔ a lovely small old rectangular green French wooden writing desk. ✔ a beautiful big new black Italian leather bag.
🚫 common mistakes: ✘ red small bag → a small red bag. ✘ a wooden round table → a round wooden table.

⚙️ 4. ADVERB FORMATION – from adjective to adverb

+ly quick→quickly
-y → -ily happy→happily
-ic → -ically dramatic→dramatically
-le → -ly gentle→gently
-ue drop e true→truly
-ll add y full→fully
⚠️ irregular adverbs: fast, hard, late, early, straight, daily. “He works hard” (adv) vs “hardly” (barely).
🧠 special: public → publicly, shy → shyly.

🧬 comparative & superlative adverbs

📊 comparative

faster, more quickly, better, worse

📊 superlative

fastest, most quickly, best, worst
✅ “He reacted the most calmly.” “He drives more carefully than his brother.”

🎯 THE ENTRANCE EXAM GOLDEN RULES
• After linking verbs → adjective. • Adverbs often end in -ly, but not always. • Double comparatives are illegal. • Order: never color before size; origin near material. • -y → -ily, -ic → -ically, -le → -ly. • “Hard” vs “hardly” changes meaning. • Use “the” in superlatives.
