Physical vs Chemical Properties

  • Physical properties: sensed or measured (state, malleability, solubility, viscosity, melting/boiling points).
  • Chemical properties: how substance reacts with another.

📝 NOMENCLATURE (IUPAC naming)

I. Binary compounds (two elements) → end in -ide

Metal + non‑metal: [metal name] [stem of non‑metal + ide]. e.g. NaCl = sodium chloride; CaO = calcium oxide; AlN = aluminum nitride; K₂S = potassium sulfide. (Hydrogen treated as metal when written first.)

Non‑metal + non‑metal: use Greek prefixes (mono, di, tri …) – see later.

Valence electrons & trends

  • Valence electrons: outer electrons (count 1→8).
  • Ionization energy: energy to remove one electron. Decreases ↓ down group, increases → across period.
  • Atomic radius: increases down group (more shells), decreases across period (nucleus pulls tighter).
  • Electron affinity: tends to increase across period; halogens highest.
  • Electronegativity: increases left→right, decreases down group.
📈 Ionization energy trend: easier to remove electron farther from nucleus; across period more protons = harder.

⚛️ Chemical Bonding

Octet rule: atoms want 8 e⁻ in outer shell. Ionic: transfer electrons; Covalent: share.

Ionic bond: metal + non‑metal (e.g. Na⁺ Cl⁻). Covalent bond: share electrons (polar if ΔEN 0.5‑1.7, pure if same).

Lewis dot diagrams show valence electrons.

Intermolecular vs Intramolecular forces

  • Intra = within molecule (ionic, covalent, metallic) → chemical properties.
  • Inter = between molecules → physical properties. Relative strength: dispersion (London) < dipole‑dipole < hydrogen bonds.

📐 Naming with multiple valences (Stock & old)

  • Stock: Roman numeral indicates charge: FeCl₂ iron(II) chloride; FeCl₃ iron(III) chloride.
  • Old (‑ous / ‑ic): FeCl₂ ferrous chloride (lower charge), FeCl₃ ferric chloride (higher).
  • Always use subscript of non‑metal to deduce metal’s valence (reverse crossover).

Polyatomic ions

  • NH₄⁺ ammonium, CN⁻ cyanide, OH⁻ hydroxide.
  • Oxyanions: -ate (more O), -ite (less O); per- (more), hypo- (less). ClO⁻ hypochlorite, ClO₂⁻ chlorite, ClO₃⁻ chlorate, ClO₄⁻ perchlorate.
  • Add hydrogen: HCO₃⁻ hydrogen carbonate (bicarbonate).
Examples: BaSO₄ barium sulfate; Ca(NO₃)₂ calcium nitrate; Fe(NO₃)₂ iron(II) nitrate.

Binary non‑metal compounds

Greek prefixes: mono, di, tri, tetra, penta, hexa, hepta, octa, nona, deca.
CO = carbon monoxide, CO₂ = carbon dioxide, N₂O₅ = dinitrogen pentoxide.
Common names: NO nitric oxide, N₂O nitrous oxide.

Hydrates

e.g. calcium sulphate dihydrate, cobalt(II) chloride hexahydrate.

Acids nomenclature

  • Hydro acids: hydro + halogen stem + ic : HCl hydrochloric acid.
  • Oxoacids: if anion ends in -ate → -ic acid (HNO₃ nitric acid). if anion ends in -ite → -ous acid (HNO₂ nitrous acid).
  • H₂SO₄ sulfuric, H₂SO₃ sulfurous, HClO hypochlorous, HClO₂ chlorous, HClO₃ chloric, HClO₄ perchloric.

🧮 Moles and Mass

  • 1 mole = 6.022×10²³ particles (Avogadro’s number).
  • Molar mass (g/mol) = atomic mass from periodic table (in grams).
  • \( n = \frac{N}{N_A} \) and \( n = \frac{m}{MM} \) ⇒ \( \frac{N}{N_A} = \frac{m}{MM} \).

Percent composition

\(\% = \frac{\text{mass of element}}{\text{total mass}} \times 100\). Law of definite proportions.

Empirical formula

Simplest whole‑number ratio. Steps: find moles of each element → divide by smallest → convert to integers.

Mole ratios from balanced equations

N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃ ⇒ 1 mol N₂ : 3 mol H₂ : 2 mol NH₃
4NH₃ + 5O₂ → 4NO + 6H₂O ⇒ use coefficients as conversion factors.

Excess & limiting reagents

Limiting reagent is completely consumed; excess remains. Example: 2 mol Mg + 5 mol O₂ (balanced 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO) – Mg limiting, O₂ excess.

Arrhenius theory

Acid dissociates in water → H⁺; base → OH⁻. Limitations: NH₃ has no OH⁻ but basic.

Brønsted‑Lowry

Acid = proton (H⁺) donor; base = proton acceptor. Conjugate pairs: HA + B ⇌ HB⁺ + A⁻.

💧 Example: HCl + H₂O → H₃O⁺ + Cl⁻ (acid HCl, base H₂O, conj. acid H₃O⁺, conj. base Cl⁻).

Strong acids (6):

HClO₄, HI, HBr, H₂SO₄, HCl, HNO₃ — dissociate 100%.

Weak acids:

CH₃COOH, CO₂(aq), SO₂(aq), HF, HCN.

Strong bases (6):

LiOH, NaOH, KOH, Ca(OH)₂, Sr(OH)₂, Ba(OH)₂ — group 1 & 2 hydroxides.

Weak bases:

NH₃, amines (R‑NH₂).

Amphiprotic / amphoteric

Can act as acid or base (e.g. H₂O, HCO₃⁻).

Autoionization of water & pH

H₂O + H₂O ⇌ H₃O⁺ + OH⁻, at 25°C [H₃O⁺]=[OH⁻]=1.0×10⁻⁷ M (neutral).

\(pH = -\log[H_3O^+]\), \(pOH = -\log[OH^-]\), pH + pOH = 14 (25°C).

⛽ Gas Laws & Ideal Gas

Standard conditions

  • STP: 0°C (273 K) , 1 atm (101.3 kPa, 760 torr)
  • SATP: 25°C, 100 kPa

Pressure conversions

1 atm = 760 mmHg = 760 torr = 101.325 kPa
Convert: multiply/divide accordingly (examples in notes).

Ideal Gas Law

\(PV = nRT\) where R = 8.314 L·kPa·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹ (or 0.0821 L·atm·mol⁻¹·K⁻¹).

Example: mass of Ne in 0.88‑L tube, 90 kPa, 30 °C → find n = PV/(RT), then mass = n×MM.

Periodic trends (summary)

  • Ionization energy: increases →, decreases ↓
  • Atomic radius: decreases →, increases ↓
  • Electron affinity: generally increases →
  • Electronegativity: increases →, decreases ↓
