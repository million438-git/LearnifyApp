Lesson notes

In water, acids increase the concentration of hydrogen ions (often represented more precisely as hydronium ions), while bases accept hydrogen ions or produce hydroxide ions in introductory aqueous models. Strong and weak describe the extent of ionization, not simply how concentrated a solution is.

The pH scale commonly runs from 0 to 14 for dilute aqueous solutions near room temperature. A pH below 7 is acidic, pH 7 is neutral, and pH above 7 is basic under those conditions. The scale is logarithmic, so a one-unit pH change corresponds to a tenfold change in hydrogen-ion activity.

Indicators help estimate acidity or alkalinity. Litmus turns red in acidic solution and blue in basic solution. Universal indicator displays a range of colours; a calibrated pH meter can provide a more precise measurement.
