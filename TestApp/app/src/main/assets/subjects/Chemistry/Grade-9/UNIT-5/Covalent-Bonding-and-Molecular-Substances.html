Lesson notes

Covalent bonding occurs when atoms share pairs of electrons. It is common between nonmetal atoms. A single covalent bond represents one shared pair; a double bond represents two shared pairs.

A water molecule, H₂O, contains two O–H covalent bonds. An oxygen molecule, O₂, contains a double bond in the usual introductory model. Carbon dioxide, CO₂, has two C=O double bonds.

Many simple molecular substances have relatively low melting and boiling points compared with ionic lattices because the forces between separate molecules are often weaker than the covalent bonds within each molecule. Giant covalent structures, such as diamond, are an important exception.
