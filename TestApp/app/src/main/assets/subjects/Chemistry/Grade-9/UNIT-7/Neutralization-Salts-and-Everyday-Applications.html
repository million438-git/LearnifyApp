Lesson notes

A typical acid–base neutralization forms a salt and water: hydrochloric acid + sodium hydroxide → sodium chloride + water. The net ionic equation is H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l) in the simple aqueous model.

Salts can be prepared by different methods depending on the acid, base and salt solubility. A soluble salt may be obtained by reacting an appropriate acid with an insoluble base, filtering excess solid and carefully crystallizing the solution. Preparation must follow a teacher-approved procedure.

Neutralization has useful applications, including treating acidic soil with an appropriate liming material and managing certain industrial waste streams. The correct substance and quantity depend on the actual conditions; indiscriminate chemical addition can cause harm.
