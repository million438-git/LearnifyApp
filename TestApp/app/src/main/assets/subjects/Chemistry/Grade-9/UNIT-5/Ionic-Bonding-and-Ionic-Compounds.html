Lesson notes

Ionic bonding involves electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions. In a simple model, a metal transfers electrons to a nonmetal, producing positive and negative ions.

Sodium chloride contains Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions in a 1:1 ratio. Magnesium chloride contains Mg²⁺ and Cl⁻ ions in a 1:2 ratio, so the overall charge is zero.

Many ionic compounds have high melting points. They conduct electricity when molten or dissolved in water if mobile ions are present, but not as solids when their ions are fixed in a lattice. Solubility varies from compound to compound.

Try it: Work out the formula for a compound made from Al³⁺ and O²⁻. Check that the total positive and negative charges balance.
