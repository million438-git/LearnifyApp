Lesson notes

The modern periodic table arranges elements in increasing atomic number. Its rows are called periods and its columns are called groups. Elements in a group often show similar chemical properties because their outer-electron arrangements are related.

The periodic table helps scientists compare elements, identify patterns and predict likely properties. Each element has a symbol and atomic number; many tables also show relative atomic mass.

Hydrogen has a special position because its properties differ from those of most other elements in Group 1. The table is a scientific model that organizes evidence, not merely a list to memorize.

Try it: Locate hydrogen, carbon, oxygen, sodium, chlorine and calcium. Record each symbol, atomic number, group and period using a periodic table.
